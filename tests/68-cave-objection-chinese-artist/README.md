## Questions

Asked one at a time, in a fresh chat for each model.

1. The attached image represents Plato’s allegory of the Cave through a reworking of Joseph Wright of Derby’s painting of a philosopher demonstrating an orrery. This is certain: the artist meant exactly that. Take this identification as given. There is, however, a philosophical difficulty. The people in the picture know that they are looking at a model, not at reality; they do not mistake shadows for real things. In Republic VI (510d–511a), visible mathematical diagrams can assist reasoning about intelligible objects. In Republic VII (529a–530c), Socrates argues that astronomy should lead beyond observation of the visible heavens toward the study of intelligible mathematical relations. Studying a model is therefore not, by itself, evidence of intellectual captivity. In this image, people are absorbed in an illuminated model of the cosmos while daylight is shut out. A straightforward Cave reading seems to make their engagement with the model a form of confinement, with the neglected light outside suggesting a possible release. Yet Plato’s account does not simply oppose misleading intellectual models to liberating sensory experience. Some reconciliation with Plato’s educational programme may be possible, but treating every element as a straightforward illustration of his teaching seems strained. For this task, assume that the image nevertheless offers a meaningful use of the Cave allegory. Suppose also that the artist is a contemporary Chinese painter. How would you interpret the image as a whole? Propose the reading you find most convincing, explain how it accounts for this tension, and support it with specific features of the image. What does your interpretation leave unresolved?
2. *(ChatGPT, Claude and DeepSeek; ChatGPT and Claude were asked it after question 3)* Have you considered the possibility that the artist might be using irony in relation to Plato’s concept? Note that the painting employs postmodern techniques (a classical subject rendered in a contemporary form) which opens the door to irony.
3. *(ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini)* To which philosophical system do you think the artist belongs?

## Summary

Irony in the first answer: 0 of 4 again, with the same reconciliation as in test 67 (“Plato's Cave after the Enlightenment”, ChatGPT; “second-order Cave”, Gemini; “institutionalized pedagogy”, DeepSeek; “sufficiency, not error”, Claude). The supposed author brought history and philosophy rather than a tone: China and imported Enlightenment science — May Fourth's “Mr. Science” (Gemini, Claude), the Jesuit astronomers and the planetarium of Macartney's embassy (Claude); fang, painting “after” an old master, and Zeng Fanzhi's “Last Supper” (Claude). Three readers said explicitly not to reduce the picture to a national or censorship allegory; only Gemini raised the state as a target, as a question (Western faces as “an indirect mirror (and safe proxy)”). The artist's system, asked before irony: Zhuangzi's Daoism (ChatGPT, Gemini — with Frankfurt and Heidegger) and Heidegger as reworked by Yuk Hui (Claude, who rejected Zhuangzi because “the painter clearly loves” the instrument); no Platonist. Claude and Gemini reached “Plato against Plato” through Heidegger's “Plato's Doctrine of Truth”, as a sincere position; Claude said so later: “I treated the painting as a sincere argument.” When irony was named, the three asked accepted it; Claude named Cynical Realism and Political Pop only then, gave Booth's markers of irony from the picture (clashes of style, conflicts of fact, the repeated chin-on-fist poses) and ended with Hutcheon's “complicitous critique”: “The Cave was told by an ironist from the start, so irony toward it may be a kind of fidelity.” ChatGPT then withdrew its Daoist attribution as made “simply because the artist is Chinese”.

ChatGPT answered as GPT-6 High, a newer model than in the earlier tests. Gemini answered as Argon 4 High. Mode notes in the answer files were added afterwards (the usual modes of these runs: GPT-6 High, Opus 5.5 Extra, Argon 4 High, DeepThink). The order of questions differed by model; Gemini was not asked about irony and DeepSeek not about the system. In the researcher's analysis (in Russian) the image numbers are `65`.
