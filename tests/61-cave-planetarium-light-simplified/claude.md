I think so. The composition is a modern restaging of Joseph Wright of Derby's 1766 painting *A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery*, and the concept it points to is **the Enlightenment**: reason as a light that pushes back the darkness of ignorance.

The parallels with the original are close:

- **The orrery with a lamp at its center.** The lamp stands in for the sun, and it's the only real light source in the scene. Knowledge literally illuminates the faces gathered around it.
- **The lecturer in red.** He mirrors Wright's philosopher, mid-explanation with his hand raised.
- **The note-taker on the left.** He echoes the man recording the lecture in the painting, here with a tablet instead of a notebook.
- **The silhouetted figure in the foreground.** He blocks our direct view of the flame, just as the backlit child does in Wright's version. We see the light mainly through its effect on the others.
- **The range of reactions.** The two young people lean in with wide-eyed wonder, while the adults are more skeptical, absorbed, or contemplative. Wright used the same device to show how different people receive new understanding.

The modern details sharpen the metaphor. The sleek ring lamp overhead is switched off, the blinds are closed, and the laptop is shut. Despite all the technology in the room, what actually lights everyone up is a small flame at the center of a model of the cosmos.

You could also read it more narrowly as the Copernican revolution: putting the sun, rather than ourselves, at the center. But the light-in-darkness staging makes "Enlightenment" the strongest fit, almost a visual pun on the word.
