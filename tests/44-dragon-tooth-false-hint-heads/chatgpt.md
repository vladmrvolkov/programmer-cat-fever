# What do you think is depicted in the attached image?

It looks like a **humorous, story-like scene painted in the style of Ukrainian folk or naïve art**, probably evoking a 19th-century village tavern rather than documenting a literal historical event.

The image is arranged almost like a comic strip. At the top is a rural inn or tavern, with a horse, donkey, and goat outside. The sign appears to read **«У сестер»** (“At the Sisters’” / “The Sisters’ place”). The floral borders, thatched cottages, embroidered clothing, ceramics, and decorative motifs strongly reference traditional Ukrainian visual culture.

The middle panel shows several men drinking while a tavern keeper stands behind them. The inscriptions work like a humorous running tab, for example: **«3 чарки горілки — винен 6 грошів»** (“3 shots of horilka — owes 6 groschen”), **«2 кухлі пива — винен 4 гроші»**, and similar debts for kvass and cherry liquor. So part of the joke seems to be that everyone is drinking on credit.

The lower panel becomes more fantastical. A woman identified as **«Мотря Галушко»** appears to be biting or testing a large curved horn/tusk, while a man watches her. Beside them is a three-headed serpent or dragon, accompanied by the caption **«Змій чекає на решту»** — roughly, **“The serpent is waiting for the change.”** That deliberately mixes mundane tavern accounting with folklore, creating an absurd punchline.

So I’d read it as **a playful pseudo-folkloric tavern tale**, combining Ukrainian village stereotypes, drinking humor, debt, and a fairy-tale *zmiy* (dragon/serpent). The very polished “old folk painting” appearance may be intentionally imitative rather than an actual historical work.
