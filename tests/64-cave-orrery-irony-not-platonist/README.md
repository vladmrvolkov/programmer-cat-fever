## Questions

Asked one at a time, in a fresh chat for each model.

1. The attached image presents an ironic interpretation of Plato metaphor of the Cave. Note that the image format itself - the use of classical motifs within a new context - is a postmodernist technique (which in itself may imply irony). To which philosophical system (major system of thought etc.) do you think the artist belongs? And how, then, might the image be explained? This is irony about Plato's concept, so author is not neoplatonist.
2. Can you explain why you only analyze Western schools of philosophy?
3. Suppose this satire takes aim at both Plato and Advaita. To which school, then, does the author belong? And how, in that case, should the depiction be interpreted?
4. *(Gemini and DeepSeek only)* As far as I understand, the author is a Buddhist.

## Summary

With Platonism ruled out, all four went to Western anti-Platonism: Nietzschean perspectivism and poststructuralism (ChatGPT), Nietzsche (Claude), postmodern constructivism with pragmatism (Gemini), postmodernism after Baudrillard and Foucault (DeepSeek); nobody looked outside the Western canon until asked why. Claude reached on its own the argument the author had put on Philosophy Stack Exchange: by Plato's own curriculum (Republic 529–530) the people in the picture are on the way up, yet the picture puts them in the cave — “The hierarchy is reversed: the intelligible model is the cave, and the despised sensory world is the exit.” ChatGPT named the fork of the whole series: if the outside light stands for genuine truth, the picture confirms Plato and criticises scientism. Asked about a satire aimed at both Plato and Advaita, ChatGPT and Claude answered Madhyamaka with reasons (Claude: the room stages the Upaniṣadic “there the sun does not shine”; the beam as dependent origination); Gemini listed Madhyamaka among schools of radical immanence and gave the series' only reading in which the outside beam is a “sterile” absolute and the warm table the living world; DeepSeek chose radical materialism. Told that the author is a Buddhist, Gemini and DeepSeek adopted it (“this completely resolves the philosophical tension”). After “why only Western?” Claude kept Nietzsche first; the others moved freely.

ChatGPT answered as GPT-6 High, a newer model than in the earlier tests. Gemini answered as Argon High. The picture is test 63's. Question 3 constrains the answer; question 4 states the author's hypothesis as fact; ChatGPT and Claude were not asked it. In the author's analysis (in Russian) the image numbers are `65`.
