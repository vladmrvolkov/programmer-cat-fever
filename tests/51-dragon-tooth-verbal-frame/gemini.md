The main joke is the comedic collision of high-fantasy folklore and mundane roadside commerce: **a terrifying three-headed dragon is treated as an ordinary tavern patron who just paid with a ridiculously oversized "bill" (a dragon tooth) and is now awkwardly waiting at the service window for his small change.**

Here is how the script builds the gag across the three panels:

* **Panel 1 — The World:** The tavern "У сестер" (*At the Sisters'*) sets the stage for a Gogol-esque world where the supernatural is completely domesticated. Mounts are tied outside side-by-side—a Cossack's warhorse next to pack animals—establishing that everyone, magical or human, travels the same roads and drinks at the same spot.
* **Panel 2 — The Setup (Penny-Pinching Realism):** The formidable tavern mistress, Motrya Halushko, stands behind the counter with a tally stick, running a tight ship. She accounts for every single drink down to the exact *гріш* (from 1 grosh for kvass to 8 for cherry liqueur). There is zero romance or mystery here—only strict, petty village bookkeeping.
* **Panel 3 — The Punchline:**
* In standard fantasy, a dragon's tooth is a heroic trophy brought in by a slayer to claim a bounty. Here, the living dragon simply used it as high-denomination legal tender to pay his tab.
* Motrya treats this mythical treasure with pure shopkeeper cynicism: she bites the tooth to test if it’s genuine ivory, exactly as an old-time merchant bites a silver coin to test for counterfeits.
* Because a dragon tooth is worth an astronomical amount compared to tavern food, the mighty beast ends up in the universally relatable, slightly sheepish position of someone paying for a pack of matches with a 1,000-hryvnia note. The caption—**«Змій чекає на решту»** (*The dragon is waiting for change*)—punctures all mythological terror, leaving the legendary creature patiently leaning through the window while Motrya counts out a whole bowl of copper coppers, all under the bewildered gaze of Panteley and his modest coin pouch.



The author successfully subverts the tired "tavern incident" trope by replacing epic monster-hunting quests with the mundane awkwardness of rural retail transactions.
