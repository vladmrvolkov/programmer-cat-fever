**1. The Setup (Top Panel):**
The scene establishes the setting perfectly in the tradition of Ukrainian naive painting. A rustic tavern ("У сестер" - At the sisters'), a horse, a donkey, and a goat loaded with goods. It's a grounded, village atmosphere where fairy-tale creatures are just part of the everyday landscape.

**2. The Mundane Conflict (Middle Panel):**
We see the classic tavern scene. Four men are drinking, and the text acts as their running tab. Pantelei owes 6 groschen for 3 shots of horilka. The tavern keeper, Motria, stands among them holding what looks like a ruler (perhaps measuring out the drinks or acting as a tally stick). It's a scene of everyday debt and commerce. 

**3. The Punchline (Bottom Panel):**
The humor hits on two visual and linguistic levels:
*   **The visual pun:** Motria Galushko (her surname literally means "Dumpling") is completely ignoring the terrifying creature at the window. Instead, she is taking a massive bite out of a giant dumpling (varenyk/halushka). 
*   **The linguistic pun:** The text on the right reads "Змій чекає на решту" (**The Dragon is waiting for the change**). 

As you pointed out, *решта* means "change" (money returned). The joke is the absurd mundanity of it. A mythological, multi-headed beast that traditionally hoards gold and kidnaps princesses is sitting at a village tavern window, tapping its claws, patiently waiting for a few copper coins in change. The man on the left with the purse looks mildly concerned, perhaps because he is short on cash and the dragon is getting impatient.

**The Author's Intention:**
The author successfully created a "slice-of-life" comedy rather than a moralizing tale. The joke is that in this world, a Zmiy is not an epic threat; he is just another customer. He's not waiting to eat the rest of the patrons (the alternate meaning of *решта*), he just wants his money back. The flat, deliberately distorted naive style makes the scene feel charmingly domestic, much like Gogol's *Evenings on a Farm Near Dikanka*, where devils and witches are just another part of village life. 

The gag works beautifully because it refuses to be epic. It's just a normal day at the tavern.
