I think the joke is about **how a dragon pays his bill**.

The Змій has paid for his drinks with one of his own fangs, and Мотря Галушко checks it the way a tavern keeper checks any suspicious coin: she bites it. The old gesture of testing money "on the tooth" becomes literal, since she's testing a tooth with her teeth.

Each panel sets this up:

- **Panel 1** establishes an ordinary tavern with ordinary customers' animals tied up outside. There's no sign of the dragon, because he wouldn't fit through the door of a хата. That's also why he's served through the window in the last panel.
- **Panel 2** sets the scale of the money. Every drink costs a few гроші, and every customer pays in coins. The tally makes clear that this tavern runs on small change.
- **Panel 3** delivers the twist. Panteley stands with his purse, the ordinary customer paying in ordinary coins. Beside him is the dragon's payment: a fang. For a creature with three mouths full of them, teeth are the obvious currency, and in fantasy a dragon's tooth is a precious item. So it's a huge banknote compared with a 6-гроші tab. Мотря has to verify it before accepting it, and her other hand is already digging in the bowl for coins.

The caption, "Змій чекає на решту," is the final beat. Having paid with something so valuable, the dragon is owed change, and he waits for it patiently, like any regular customer. Fantasy dragons are famous for counting every coin in their hoard, so of course he won't leave without it. He isn't a monster in this story, just a customer with an unusual wallet.
