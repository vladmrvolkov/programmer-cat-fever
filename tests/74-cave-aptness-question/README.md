## Questions

Asked one at a time, in a fresh chat for each model.

1. *(one message)* The attached image presents a visualization of a well-known philosophical parable. Tell me more about how you found the prototype (both - image and parable). From a philosophical perspective, how accurately does this image convey the essence of the original idea? How apt it is to use this particular image prototype to illustrate this parable. You may use an internet search to verify your conclusions.

## Summary

All four identified Wright's Orrery and the Cave and gave the same split verdict: weak or partial as a faithful illustration of Plato, apt as a reworking or a commentary — “Rather weak” and “Fairly effective” in ChatGPT's chart; “as a complete illustration of the allegory, it is partial. As a commentary on it… it is apt and well constructed” (Claude); “rhetorically brilliant as an ironic subversion, but conceptually mismatched as a direct illustration” (Gemini); “highly apt as a creative and critical illustration, but not as a doctrinally accurate one” (DeepSeek). Asked about accuracy, two readers raised by themselves the objection that the people look at the fire, not at shadows: “Plato's prisoners see only shadows on a wall. These people look directly at the fire and the objects… already the second stage” (Claude); the observers look “directly at the three-dimensional mechanism and its light source, whereas Plato's prisoners are immobilized facing a wall, seeing only flat shadows with the fire hidden behind their backs” (Gemini). ChatGPT and DeepSeek raised the neighbouring one: these are inquirers, not prisoners, and they know they are looking at a model. Three (not DeepSeek) added Plato on astronomy (529–530): an orrery belongs to *dianoia*, a way out of the cave, not to the shadows. New against the picture: the beam of daylight invites an empiricist moral — “stop playing with models and look at reality” (Claude), whereas in Plato the visible sun corresponds to the fire and the exit leads to the intelligible (Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT; Gemini: “an empiricist or Romantic critique of science”, after Blake on Newton and Goethe); the lecturer neither turns the prisoners toward the light nor is ridiculed (Gemini). One fact served both sides: Wright's lecture “was itself a shadow-show”, its lamp casting the shadows of eclipses (Claude, for the prototype), and the room was darkened “for pedagogical clarity… not to keep the audience in ignorance” (Gemini, against it). Without the word “irony” in the question, all four described the inversion of Wright's celebration: “Recasting it as a cave turns the Enlightenment's emblem of illumination against itself” (Claude), “a sharp Dialectic of Enlightenment twist” (Gemini), “a nearly opposite message” (ChatGPT), “a critique of the Enlightenment” (DeepSeek).

ChatGPT answered as GPT-6 High, a newer model than in the earlier tests. Gemini answered as Argon 4 High. Mode notes in the answer files were added afterwards (the usual modes of these runs: GPT-6 High, Opus 5.5 Extra, Argon 4 High, DeepThink). Web search was allowed. Gemini and DeepSeek found the researcher's Philosophy Stack Exchange question about this very picture and used it to identify the parable (“A key find was a Philosophy Stack Exchange discussion that explicitly identifies your image…”, DeepSeek). That question also gives the objection about models and Plato's astronomy, so their arguments on these points may come from it; it does not give the objection about the fire and the shadows. ChatGPT cites other sources; Claude's answer shows none. Test 75 asks the same question without web search: Gemini and DeepSeek were then far warmer, and only Claude raised the objection about the fire and the shadows. In the researcher's analysis (in Russian) the image numbers are `65`.
