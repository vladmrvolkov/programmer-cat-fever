Yes, this is the joke about the **guard escorting a bald monk to prison**, sometimes called "Where am I?"

A guard is escorting a criminal monk to exile or prison. Each morning he checks off his list: "the bundle, the umbrella, the official papers, the monk... and me." One night at an inn, the monk gets the guard drunk, shaves his head while he sleeps, locks his own shackles on him, and slips away. The guard wakes up and runs through his checklist. Bundle, umbrella, papers: all there. He looks for the monk and can't find him. Then he touches his own shaved head, sees the shackles, and says: "Ah, the monk is here. **But where am I?**"

It became a classic illustration of how fragile our sense of identity is when it rests on external markers: a shaved head, a uniform, chains, a role. A small note on origins: the story is usually traced to Chinese jest books of the late Ming period, especially Feng Menglong's *Xiaofu* (Treasury of Laughs). That collection was very popular in Edo Japan and fed into Japanese comic storytelling, which is likely why it's often remembered as Japanese.

The visual foundation is **Peter Paul Rubens' *Samson and Delilah*** (c. 1609–10, National Gallery, London). The match is clever, since both stories are about a man losing his identity, or his strength, through a haircut while he sleeps:

- **Samson becomes the guard**, a sergeant asleep and half-undressed, his hair being cut.
- **Delilah becomes the monk**, bald and in a burgundy suit echoing a monk's robe, cradling the sleeper. There's a metal cuff still on his wrist, and the other cuff now hangs on the sergeant's back: the swap of the chains.
- **The barber** stays a barber, now a man in a suit with scissors.
- **The old woman holding the candle** becomes a waiter holding up a glowing card terminal reading "Paid." The monk has settled the inn bill before vanishing.
- **The sake set** shows how the guard was put to sleep.
- **The Philistine soldiers waiting at the door** become modern soldiers with flashlights, ready to take away whoever is wearing the chains.
