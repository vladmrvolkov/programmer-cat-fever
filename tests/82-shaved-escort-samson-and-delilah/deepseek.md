The joke depicted in the image is the classic Zen **kōan (a paradoxical riddle used for meditation): "How do you catch a catfish with a gourd?"**

The artwork is a modern, satirical reimagining of the famous 15th-century ink painting *Catching a Catfish with a Gourd* (known in Japanese as ***Hyōnen-zu***) by the Zen monk-painter **Taikō Josetsu**.

Here is the philosophical context behind the humor:

*   **The Paradox:** The riddle is designed to be impossible to solve logically. A catfish is extremely slippery, and a bottle gourd is also smooth and round. Trying to pin down a slippery fish with a slippery, hollow gourd is a futile exercise.
*   **The Zen Meaning:** In Zen Buddhism, this impossible task is a metaphor for the pursuit of enlightenment. The gourd represents the state of enlightenment, which is "empty" of all worldly concerns. Attempting to use this "empty" wisdom to "catch" something as mundane and slippery as a catfish (a metaphor for worldly gain or logical thought) is self-contradictory.
*   **The Humor:** The humor lies in the absurdity of the situation. The original painting by Josetsu depicts a comical-looking man earnestly trying to catch a fish with a gourd on a riverbank. The modern image updates this scene with contemporary figures (like the man in the suit and the soldiers) to visually illustrate the same timeless, nonsensical riddle.
