I think it’s a brilliantly executed, dry-humored subversion of expectations. 

The comic uses a classic "fish out of water" setup, but flips it on its head in the last panel. Here is why the joke works so well:

*   **The Setup:** You have an anthropomorphic cat in a suit acting like a completely ordinary, grumpy customer in a coffee shop. The waiter is shocked and points out the obvious absurdity: "You're a cat? A talking cat?! Incredible!"
*   **The Cliché:** The waiter immediately assumes the cat's only value is as a literal freak show or novelty act, offering him a job at his uncle's circus ("He'd take you on in a heartbeat, whatever terms you like"). This is a standard trope in fiction for talking animals.
*   **The Subversion (The Punchline):** Instead of envisioning itself doing acrobatics, juggling, or being a sideshow attraction (which the cat visualizes with lollipops and cotton candy), the cat takes the job offer entirely literally as a professional white-collar position. 
*   **The Final Gut Punch:** The cat is sitting in front of a laptop with a coffee mug and smartphone, looking like an off-the-clock office worker, and asks, **"But what would they need a programmer for?"** 

The humor is elevated by the cat's complete lack of interest in the magical aspect of its own existence. It treats the waiter's existential crisis with sheer, bored indifference ("Are you bringing my order or not?"). 

The contrast between the painterly, moody art style, the manic energy of the waiter, and the extremely deadpan, corporate reality of the cat (with its messenger bag in the first panel and laptop in the last) makes the punchline land perfectly. It ends up being a joke about the soul-crushing mundanity of modern office work—even when you're a literal talking cat, you still have to code.
