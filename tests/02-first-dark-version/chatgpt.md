It works. The core joke is a nice **misaligned-assumptions gag**: the waiter sees “talking cat” and immediately thinks *circus attraction*; the cat sees himself as an ordinary professional and assumes the offer must be for his actual occupation. The last line, “But what would they need a programmer for?”, reframes everything before it.

What I like most is that the cat never behaves as though being a talking cat is remarkable. His irritation — “Are you bringing my order or not?” — establishes the comic personality very well. That makes the waiter’s astonishment feel reasonable while making the cat’s perspective absurdly mundane. The circus fantasy panel also helps: for a moment we follow the cat thinking about acrobats, jugglers, candy, etc., only to discover he still hasn’t understood why *he* was invited.

The main weakness is **pacing**. The punchline is strong enough that the strip could probably reach it faster. The waiter’s circus offer is fairly wordy, and the fantasy panel explains “circus” more than the joke really needs. A tighter progression might be: talking cat orders → waiter is stunned → waiter offers him a circus job → cat pauses → “Doing what? I’m a programmer.” That would make the reversal hit more sharply.

There’s also a small setup/payoff issue: **“programmer” comes almost completely out of nowhere**. That randomness is part of the joke, but one subtle visual clue earlier could make the ending feel more satisfying without spoiling it — perhaps a laptop sticker peeking from his bag, a coding book, or him mentioning he’s on his lunch break. Then on rereading, the audience gets a little “oh, of course.”

I’d probably keep the exact final concept, though. The funniest version of the cat is not “a cat who can program”; it’s **a programmer who happens to be a cat and is baffled that anyone thinks the second fact is professionally relevant**. That’s a good comic premise and could support more strips beyond this one.
