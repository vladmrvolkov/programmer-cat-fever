The two images offer a sharp contrast in how to translate the **Epimenides paradox**—one illustrates the pure mechanics of formal logic, while the other translates it into sharp social satire.

### The Left: The Pure Recursive Loop

The left panel treats the paradox as a monumental, philosophical puzzle:

* **Literal Grounding:** Epimenides stands on a cliffside pedestal engraved with *"I AM CRETAN"*, establishing the essential factual premise of the contradiction.
* **The Visual Ouroboros:** The ribbon looping around his head with the phrase *"ALL CRETANS ARE LIARS"* forms an unbroken, circular arrow. It visually mimics an infinite loop: if the statement is true, Epimenides is lying, which makes it false; if it is false, Cretans tell the truth, making it true.
* **Tone:** Contemplative and academic. It frames the paradox as an intellectual trap—a thought experiment suspended in an idealized Mediterranean landscape.

### The Right: Pragmatic Satire

The cartoon transforms abstract logic into a punchy workplace comedy:

* **The Setting:** A corporate job interview where the nervous applicant blurts out the famous paradox.
* **The Punchline:** The executive, waving a sheet of dubious candidate *"REFERENCES"*, beams and remarks, *"At last, someone I can trust."*
* **The Layered Irony:** The joke operates on two levels. Logically, trusting a confessed liar is self-defeating. Societally, in an environment saturated with exaggerated credentials and polished corporate deception, a candidate who openly admits to dishonesty paradoxically appears as the most transparent person in the room.

### Abstract Mechanics vs. Human Absurdity

Together, the two panels form a brilliant pair. The classical version explains **what the paradox does to the mind** (trapping it in endless recursion), while the cartoon shows **what happens when the paradox collides with human behavior** (we find pragmatic comfort in known dishonesty). The left illustrates formal philosophy; the right captures everyday cynicism.
