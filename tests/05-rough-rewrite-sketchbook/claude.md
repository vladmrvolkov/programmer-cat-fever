There's a really good joke in this, and a few structural snags keep it from landing as cleanly as it could.

The core gag is relatable: a supernatural shapeshifting cat with a deadline would rather run off to join the circus. Her daydream of acrobats and lollipops versus "this programming mess" is the heart of the piece, and anyone who's freelanced will recognize the feeling. It also fits the folklore, since bakeneko are said to walk upright and dance, so a circus career is oddly on-brand for her.

The culture clash is fun too. A Japanese yokai demands daifuku in a French café and screams "NOT A CROISSANT!", and the waiter panics so hard he translates himself mid-scream: "CHAT! CAT!" That's my favorite line. Her introducing herself by breed as if it's her name ("Freelance programmer, Cornish Rex") is a nice touch, and her body language is perfectly feline: bored, entitled, chin on paw, threatening violence over pastry. The delayed double take also works. She orders like a normal customer, and he only notices she's a talking cat once she's already seated.

The biggest problem is reading order. A two-page spread is usually read one page at a time, which means she thinks "Circus, you say?" before the waiter has mentioned his uncle's circus. If you read across the rows instead, it works beautifully: she orders and he's shocked; she threatens him and he makes a panicked circus pitch; she's tempted and then she's gone. That's clearly the rhythm you intended, but many readers will hit the payoff before the setup. Swapping the panel positions, or making each row visually bridge the gutter, would fix it.

The ending is also soft. It's unclear who says the final "BAKENEKO?" next to the coffee cup. Is she still there, invisible? Is it the cup? Another customer? The last beat should be the sharpest, and right now it's a shrug. The crossed-out "scam" from the title never pays off either. Did she dine and dash, or did he invent an uncle to get rid of her? One clear closing image would tie it together. For example, she could leave her laptop behind, still showing "DEADLINE: 0," so her deadline is now his problem, or leave an unpaid bill signed with a paw print.

Two small things: "No heels! Bah!" appears twice and floats outside the panels, so it reads like a leftover note. If it's meant to be her shedding her human disguise, which is a nice idea, use it once, inside a bubble. Cat lovers may also notice she's drawn more like a Sphynx or Devon Rex than a Cornish Rex, which has a curly coat. Unless that's deliberate, and a shapeshifter lying about her breed would be a great hidden joke.

Overall it's charming and funny, and fixing the panel order and sharpening the last beat would make it land much harder.
