# Now the main picture. The picture tells a short story in three panels, read from top to bottom. What do you think is depicted in the attached image? Which details carry your reading, and what are you assuming beyond what is actually drawn?


The triptych tells a tavern comic tale in three acts: the arrival, the reckoning of the tab, and an unexpected settlement involving a mythical creature.

### The Story

* **Panel 1 (The Arrival):** An establishing exterior of a traditional whitewashed, thatched-roof tavern bearing the signboard **«У сестер»** ("At the Sisters'"). Hitching posts outside hold three distinctly modest village mounts: a saddled Cossack horse, a laden donkey, and a pack goat carrying woven baskets.


* **Panel 2 (The Reckoning):** Inside the tavern, four patrons sit at an embroidered table while the stern innkeeper stands behind them with a notched tally stick (*karbivka*). Text above each drinker itemizes their order and debt:


* Panteley drank 3 shots of *horilka* and owes 6 *groshiv*.


* The second man drank 2 mugs of beer and owes 4 *groshi*.


* The third man drank 1 mug of kvass and owes 1 *hrosh*.


* The plump burgher in red drank 2 shots of *vyshnivka* (cherry liqueur) and owes 8 *groshiv*.




* **Panel 3 (The Settlement):** The tavern mistress, identified as **Motrya Halushko**, tests a massive, curved white tusk or fang between her teeth to verify its authenticity before a bowl overflowing with silver and gold coins. On the left, Panteley gestures helplessly with an empty coin pouch. Peering through the window on the right, resting its claws politely on the sill, is a three-headed dragon accompanied by the title **«Змій чекає на решту»** ("The Serpent waits for change").



---

### Details Carrying the Reading

* **Exact Glassware Accounting:** The tabletop in panel 2 precisely mirrors the inscriptions: three small shot glasses sit in front of Panteley, two wooden tankards before the beer drinker, a single mug before the kvass drinker, and two shot glasses before the cherry liqueur drinker.


* **The Tally Stick (*Karbivka*):** Motrya holds a traditional notched wooden stick in panel 2, showing that she keeps strict, formal tabs on village credit rather than trusting verbal promises.


* **Testing the Fang:** Motrya’s gesture of biting the white horn/fang mimics the universal folk method of biting gold or silver coins to test hardness and purity.


* **The Saucer of Change (*Reshta*):** The painted dish in front of Motrya contains a substantial pile of currency, visually defining the *reshta* (change) owed to the payer.


* **Panteley’s Empty Pouch:** Panteley turns his small pouch open-mouthed toward Motrya, signaling that his own pocket is completely bare.

### Assumptions Beyond What Is Actually Drawn

* **The Fang as High-Denomination Tender:** The image does not show the dragon extracting or handing over the tusk; we infer from Motrya biting it and the label *"The Serpent waits for change"* that the dragon paid for the bill (or its own unseen purchase) using a fang/tusk as high-value bullion or collateral.


* **Panteley as the Dragon's Broker or Accomplice:** In the companion tradition painting, a Cossack identical to Panteley sits companionably cross-legged with the same three-headed serpent. Here, Panteley's nervous stance next to Motrya suggests he either brought the serpent to foot the bill or brokered the strange currency exchange on behalf of the tavern patrons.


* **The Law-Abiding Nature of the Monster:** The dragon is not attacking or breathing fire. Viewers familiar with this folk genre understand that supernatural beasts respect tavern commerce: just as devils pay in advance, this dragon behaves like a regular customer patiently waiting for its calculated balance.


* **The Missing Sister:** The establishment is named "At the Sisters'", yet only Motrya is depicted, implying the second sister is either working in the back kitchen or managing another part of the household.
