These look like visualizations of Noam Chomsky's **"Colorless green ideas sleep furiously,"** the sentence from *Syntactic Structures* (1957) that he used to show that a sentence can be grammatically correct while being meaningless.

In the vase, the inscription ΓΡΕΕΝ spells "GREEN" in Greek letters. The Erinyes, labeled "Goddesses of Rage," are the Furies, and they're asleep, so you get "sleep furiously." Setting it in ancient Greece also hints at "ideas," since the word comes from Plato's Greek.

In the forest painting, everything is green, and the cut-outs are shaped like thought bubbles, so they read as "ideas." The figures inside them are pale and nearly colorless, and they're sleeping while a storm rages furiously around them.

Both images take a sentence designed to be impossible to picture and make it surprisingly literal.
