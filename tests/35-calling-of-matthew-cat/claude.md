This is a parody of Caravaggio's **"The Calling of Saint Matthew"** (1599–1600), restaged in a modern café.

**How it maps to the original:**

- **The composition and light.** The dark room, the window high on the back wall, and the diagonal shaft of light from the upper right are taken straight from Caravaggio. In the original, that light falls on the people Christ is calling.
- **The pointing figures.** Christ and Saint Peter enter from the right and point at the table. Here they're a waiter and a bearded cook. The waiter's limp, outstretched hand copies Christ's gesture, which itself echoes Adam's hand in Michelangelo's *Creation of Adam*.
- **The "Who, me?" gesture.** In the painting, Matthew the tax collector touches his chest as if asking whether Christ means him. That role goes to the **cat**, paw on chest, looking unimpressed.
- **The other figures at the table.** The young man in the grey hoodie, hunched over his laptop and ignoring everything, stands in for the youth slumped over his coins at the end of Caravaggio's table. The old man with glasses peering over his shoulder is the bespectacled elder in the original. The figure on the stool with his back to us matches the turned-away man in the foreground.

**The joke:**

The cat wears a lanyard with an employee badge and has a paw on a laptop, so it clearly sees itself as a programmer. Everyone else just sees a cat, and the circus poster on the table tells you where they're "calling" it: the circus, where cats perform. The caption is the cat's indignant reply: *"But what would they need a programmer for?"*

It parallels the original nicely. Matthew was called away from his trade, tax collecting, to an entirely different vocation. The cat is being called to a new career too, and it's quite certain there's been a misunderstanding about what it is.
