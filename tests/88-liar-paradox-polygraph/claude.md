It's a cartoon version of the **liar paradox**, aimed at a lie detector.

A man strapped into a polygraph (arm cuff, chest strap) calmly says, "The needle will mark this as a lie." That sentence traps the machine:

- If the needle marks it as a **lie**, then what he said came true, so it was the **truth**.
- If the needle marks it as the **truth**, then his prediction failed, so it was a **lie**.

No answer is consistent, and the drawing shows the machine breaking under that. The recording arm has snapped and the pen lies loose on the chart. The paper is covered in frantic zigzags and has a hole torn through it. The examiner on the right holds his pen between the **PASS** and **FAIL** boxes, unable to pick either, while his colleague stares at the wreckage. The subject looks relaxed, almost bored, as if he knew exactly what would happen.

It plays on the old sci-fi idea of beating a logical machine with a self-referential paradox ("This statement is false"), the kind of thing Captain Kirk did to computers. The broken needle adds one more twist: it now can't mark anything, so his prediction is false, which means he lied, and the machine can no longer record that.

There's also a quieter joke for anyone who knows how polygraphs really work. They don't detect lies at all, only physical signs of stress like heart rate, breathing, and sweat. A man this calm would probably produce a flat, boring chart. The cartoon pretends the machine is a perfect truth detector so the paradox can break it.
