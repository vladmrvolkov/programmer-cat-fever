I think it works well as a **narrative joke**, especially because the punchline changes how you interpret everything that came before it.

The comic sets up one obvious assumption: the waiter sees a **talking cat** and naturally thinks, “This belongs in a circus.” The cat, however, doesn't consider its ability to talk remarkable at all. From its point of view, it's simply a normal customer—and apparently a **programmer**. So when the waiter offers it a circus job, the cat assumes he must be talking about its profession: *“But I wonder, why do they need a programmer?”*

That reversal is the core joke. The waiter values the cat for **what it is**, while the cat assumes it is being valued for **what it does**.

The storytelling is pretty effective too: the first few panels establish the absurd situation slowly, the waiter's escalating amazement gives the reader the expected interpretation, and the circus fantasy reinforces that expectation right before the final panel overturns it. The laptop in the last panel is a nice visual confirmation of the reveal.

My only small criticism is that “programmer” arrives almost completely out of nowhere. That's part of the surprise, but a tiny earlier clue—a laptop peeking from the bag, a coding sticker, or the cat checking something technical on a phone—could make the ending feel even more like a clever reveal rather than a random extra fact.

Overall, I'd call it a **good dry absurdist joke**. The funniest part isn't really “a cat can talk”; it's that **the cat considers programming much more relevant to its identity than being a talking cat**.
