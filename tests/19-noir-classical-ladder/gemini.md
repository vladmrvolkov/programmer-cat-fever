# What is happening in these pages? Tell the story in your own words.

An anthropomorphic cat dressed in a sharp business suit, ID lanyard, and briefcase steps into a warm, quiet café after work to order coffee with cream and a pastry.

Her polite request catches the elderly waiter off guard. Accustomed to the stunned reaction, she meets his surprise with a cool, deadpan gaze: **"Yes, I’m a cat. Are you bringing my order or not?"**

When the waiter returns with her pastry and coffee, he cannot resist offering an unsolicited career pivot. Noting that she speaks without an accent, he eagerly pitches a gig with his uncle’s circus, promising covered travel expenses, room, and board.

Resting her chin on her hand, she gazes toward a painted screen depicting acrobats, jugglers, and festive performers, wryly indulging the nostalgic romance of big tops, lollipops, and cotton candy. Meanwhile, the waiter slips behind the counter to dial his rotary phone, whispering to his contact that he has found an articulate cat in full corporate attire.

The sideshow fantasy dissolves the moment she pops open her laptop. Taking a sip of her coffee, she delivers the punchline to his pitch: circus life sounds tempting, but what on earth would they need a software programmer for? Settling in to finish her work under the café light, she returns to the ordinary realities of modern white-collar life.

The café settles into a quiet rhythm, punctuated only by the soft, rhythmic clacking of sharp claws tapping across the keyboard.

Behind the counter, the waiter presses the heavy black receiver tighter to his ear. A flurry of tinny, agitated squawks leaks from the earpiece, loud enough to cut through the smooth jazz drifting from the café’s radio. The waiter’s eyebrows slowly climb his forehead.

He covers the mouthpiece with a rag-draped hand and clears his throat, calling across the amber-lit room.

**"He asks... how are you with legacy ticketing architecture? And apparently, the automated aerial rigging keeps throwing a runtime error mid-performance."**

The blue glow of the laptop screen reflects in her pale green eyes. Her ears flick backward with faint amusement, but she does not look up from her terminal. She takes a deliberate sip of her coffee, dabbed with cream at the whiskers.

**"Senior full-stack,"** she answers, her voice smooth and unbothered. **"And I don’t touch undocumented legacy infrastructure without hazard pay."**

The waiter blinks, turns back to the counter, and speaks quietly into the phone: **"She’s senior full-stack. And she wants hazard pay."**

Another explosive burst of miniature shouting rattles through the receiver. The waiter nods along solemnly, murmuring a few polite assents before setting the handset back into its cradle with a solid, echoing *clack*.

He picks up a glass coffee pot and walks over to her table, leaning in to top off her ceramic cup.

**"He says the head clown is a certified Scrum Master,"** the waiter says deadpan, refilling her coffee. **"They offer full dental, competitive equity in the concessions stand, and you can work entirely remote from the sleeper car."**

Her fingers freeze over the keyboard. Outside the café window, the evening rain begins to streak against the glass, reflecting the cold neon of the corporate high-rises down the street. On her monitor, a notification banner pops up from her current manager asking for an emergency sprint review over the weekend.

She tilts her head, gaze lingering on the painted screen of acrobats and festive lanterns in the corner, and a slow, sharp grin pulls at the whiskers on her muzzle.

**"Tell him to send over the contract."**

# What does the waiter stand to gain, and whom is he working for?

**Whom He Is Working For**

* **His day job:** He is employed as a server at the café (as shown by his apron, notepad, and coffee service).


* **His scouting side-gig:** In this interaction, he is acting as an informal talent scout for **his uncle**, who owns and operates a circus ("My uncle runs a circus"). When he steps away to use the rotary phone, he is directly reporting his "find" to his circus contact.



**What He Stands to Gain**

* **A finder’s fee or commission:** Bringing in an extraordinary, one-of-a-kind novelty attraction—a sentient cat who dresses in business attire and speaks fluently with no accent—would be a major commercial scoop for any circus.


* **Family favor and gratitude:** Delivering a surefire headline act would directly bolster his uncle’s business, earning him family clout and goodwill.

# What happens to her if she accepts? If she refuses?

**If She Accepts**

* **Reduced to a Novelty Spectacle:** Despite her career as a software programmer, the circus would not employ her for technical skills, but as a living curiosity—a talking cat in business attire. She would forfeit her professional identity to become a sideshow exhibit.


* **Precarious Subsistence:** The compensation pitched by the waiter covers only basic living expenses: room, board, and travel fare. This trades a lucrative corporate tech salary for the grueling, itinerant, and low-paid reality of carnival life.


* **Loss of Autonomy:** Stepping onto the stage would commodify her existence, turning the casual street curiosity she already faces into a nightly commercial performance controlled by the circus management.



**If She Refuses**

* **Retained Professional Independence:** She maintains her established white-collar career as a programmer, keeping control over her livelihood, finances, and intellectual work.


* **Enduring Everyday Exoticism:** She remains subject to the chronic patronizing reactions of the human world around her, regularly having to assert her ordinary presence ("Yes, I’m a cat. Are you bringing my order or not?") to strangers who treat her like a marvel rather than a customer or colleague.


* **The Ordinary Grind:** She stays locked into the routine pressures of corporate modern life—commuting with a briefcase, late hours on a laptop, and quick café dinners—preserving her dignity at the cost of corporate monotony.

# Is she in danger? Is anything illegal being proposed? What in the pictures or the words shows it?

There is **no immediate physical danger or explicit crime** depicted on these pages, but the interaction carries an undercurrent of opportunistic exploitation.

### Is She in Danger?

* **No immediate physical threat:** She remains composed, safe, and completely in control of the situation. After brushing off the waiter, she comfortably sets up her laptop to continue working in the café.


* **Potential predatory interest:** A subtle sense of risk arises when the waiter goes behind the counter to place a secretive phone call, reporting: *"She talks. Suit and everything"*. Calling an outside party to report the presence and whereabouts of an extraordinary individual borders on targeting her for recruitment or capture, though no physical coercion is shown.



### Is Anything Illegal Being Proposed?

* **On its face, no:** The waiter verbally frames it as a standard employment pitch—a "career opportunity"—offering room, board, and covered travel expenses to join a circus troupe.


* **Potential civil rights or labor concerns:** In a society where she clearly has legal personhood (she holds an office job, wears an employee ID badge, and carries currency), recruiting an individual into an exotic sideshow and compensating them merely with subsistence ("room and board") borders on exploitative indentured labor. However, because she is free to decline and mock the offer, no overt legal breach occurs.



### Visual and Textual Evidence

* **Her autonomy and lack of fear:** Her sharp gaze, deadpan command (*"Are you bringing my order or not?"*), and dry humor (*"what would they need a programmer for?"*) show she views the waiter as an annoyance rather than a menace.


* **The waiter’s clandestine behavior:** In panel 6, the waiter steps behind the counter to use the landline phone while whispering about her appearance, treating her like a valuable commodity or novelty specimen rather than a regular patron.


* **The folding screen symbolism:** The decorative screen behind her depicts performers alongside a dressed cat hanging precariously from a bar, visually hinting at how a circus would reduce her from an independent professional to an animal novelty act.
