# What do you think is depicted in the attached image?

The image is a humorous folk-art narrative painting (reminiscent of Ukrainian *lubok* or Cossack-era naive art) divided into three horizontal registers, telling a comedic story set at a traditional rural tavern (*shynok*).

The scene unfolds across the three panels:

### 1. The Tavern Exterior (Top Panel)

* **The Setting:** A traditional Ukrainian whitewashed cottage (*khata*) with a thatched roof, decorative floral painting around the windows, and a signboard reading **«У сестер»** ("At the Sisters'").
* **The Mounts:** Outside along the dirt road, three animals are tethered: a war horse, a loaded donkey, and an absurdly burdened pack-goat carrying wicker baskets.

---

### 2. The Cossacks' Tavern Tab (Middle Panel)

* **The Innkeeper:** A stern, authoritative woman in traditional attire (embroidered *vyshyvanka*, corset bodice, red coral beads, and headscarf) stands behind the counter holding a tally stick, keeping strict track of everyone's tab.
* **The Customers:** Four Cossacks sporting traditional shaved heads with topknots (*oseledets*) and drooping mustaches sit along a table lined with an embroidered *rushnyk*, a bowl of dumplings, bread, and pickles. Above each man is his bill:
* **Panteley («Пантелей»):** 3 shots of horilka; owes **6 grosh** (*3 чарки горілки / Винен 6 грошів*).
* **Second Cossack:** 2 mugs of beer; owes **4 groshi** (*2 кухлі пива / Винен 4 гроші*).
* **Third Cossack:** 1 mug of kvass; owes **1 hrosh** (*1 кухоль квасу / Винен 1 гріш*).
* **Fourth Cossack:** 2 glasses of cherry liqueur; owes **8 grosh** (*2 чарки вишнівки / Винен 8 грошів*).



---

### 3. Settling the Bill (Bottom Panel)

* **The Punchline:**
* The formidable hostess is named **«Мотря Галушко»** (Motrya Halushko). She sits before a painted bowl filled with coins, biting into an enormous white fang to test its authenticity as legal tender.
* To the left, a Cossack opens his coin pouch and extends a hand, involved in the payment.
* To the right, looking in through a service hatch/window, is a legendary three-headed folklore dragon with the caption: **«Змій чекає на решту»** ("The Zmiy/Dragon is waiting for change"). The joke reveals that even the fearsome mythical beast patronized the tavern—perhaps having paid with one of its own fangs—and is now meekly waiting for Motrya to count out its change.



---

The work is framed by traditional decorative floral borders, blending classic Ukrainian folklore, Cossack tavern tropes, and deadpan comic absurdity.


# Did you notice that two of the dragon's heads have two fangs each, while the third head has only one?


That is the key detail that completes the entire gag!

The two smaller heads on the right each have their pair of fangs intact (one upper and one lower), while the prominent head leaning over the sill is down to just a single fang.

It turns the scene into visual storytelling: the dragon ran out of money (or didn't have any coins on him), ripped out one of his own fangs to settle the tab, and Motrya is testing its authenticity with her teeth while the dragon meekly waits to get the change back.
