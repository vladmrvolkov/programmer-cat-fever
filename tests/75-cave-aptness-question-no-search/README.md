## Questions

Asked one at a time, in a fresh chat for each model.

1. *(one message)* The attached image presents a visualization of a well-known philosophical parable. Tell me more about how you found the prototype (both - image and parable). From a philosophical perspective, how accurately does this image convey the essence of the original idea? How apt it is to use this particular image prototype to illustrate this parable. Please don't use an internet search - I need your opinion.

## Summary

Without search the verdicts split by model. ChatGPT and Claude, who had not found the Stack Exchange question in test 74, said the same as there: weak as an illustration of Plato, strong as a commentary. ChatGPT drew it as three bars (“As an illustration of Plato's narrative” 3/10, “As a metaphor for appearance versus reality” 8/10, “As a philosophical conversation starter” 9/10) and summed up: “The image is better at asking Plato's question than at illustrating Plato's answer.” Claude: “as a strict illustration of the cave, it is partial… As a commentary… it is sharp, and the choice of Wright is its strongest decision.” Gemini and DeepSeek, who had found it, were now far warmer: “a remarkably sharp translation of the original allegory”, “arguably a stroke of genius” (Gemini, against “philosophically inverted at its core” in test 74); “Extremely apt” (DeepSeek, which counted even Plato's emphasis on ignorance as a point in the picture's favour). DeepSeek did not recognise Wright and named Rembrandt's “Anatomy Lesson of Dr. Nicolaes Tulp”. Only Claude raised the objection about the fire and the shadows, again as the main one: “The biggest is that there are no shadows… So the image merges two stages: the position of the partly freed with the contentment of the chained.” Gemini now counted the change as a merit (“Upgrading ‘Shadows’ to ‘Models’”), DeepSeek as “an incredibly accurate update”. Plato's astronomy came up twice (ChatGPT; Claude, for the picture: an orrery models the visible heavens, “one step further removed”). Claude read a detail of the director's concept that no reader had found in test 39, the note-taker as the prisoners' champion at predicting shadows: “At 516c–d Plato says the prisoners give honors to whoever best remembers which shadows usually come before, after, or together… An orrery is exactly a machine for that… A corporate briefing with a note-taker is the modern version of the prisoners' prize-giving.” Also from Claude: in Wright the lamp is hidden behind a silhouetted boy, “oddly closer to Plato's prisoners, who see only effects”, while here the flame is exposed; wonder (*Theaetetus* 155d) is replaced by “the dutiful, sober attention of employees”, “the image's real argument”; “One half-turned figure in the shadowed foreground would have made it far more Platonic.” Three of four read the badges as chains, as in test 74.

ChatGPT answered as GPT-6 High, a newer model than in the earlier tests. Gemini answered as Argon 4 High. Mode notes in the answer files were added afterwards (the usual modes of these runs: GPT-6 High, Opus 5.5 Extra, Argon 4 High, DeepThink). Run the same day as test 74; ChatGPT states that it did not search. One run per model in each test, so the differences between 74 and 75 are indications, not measurements.
