# 1

**"The Arms of Fantasy"** (*"An armorial of inheritance, composite ancestry and rebellion"*) is an infographic poster that maps the literary genealogy of fantasy fiction using the rules of heraldry. Across **40 entries** connected by **84 source relations**, it represents foundational genres and individual writers as coats of arms whose designs visually accumulate and transform as influences pass from earlier eras to later ones.

## How It Is Organised

* **Six chronological columns (left to right):** The chart progresses historically across six headed stages—**Traditions** (5 entries), **Early Foundations** (6 entries), **Myth & the Weird** (4 entries), **Mid-Century** (8 entries), **New Directions** (8 entries), and **Late Century** (9 entries).


* **Orthogonal lineage network:** Thin connecting lines run through vertical routing channels between columns, tracing all 84 influence paths from source shields on the left to descendant shields on the right.


* **Loose thematic bands (top to bottom):** Related branches stay broadly aligned horizontally to keep lineages legible—folklore and fairy-tale traditions track along the top (e.g., *Folktales & folklore* → *George MacDonald* → *C. S. Lewis* → *Angela Carter* / *Philip Pullman*), Arthurian and northern epic traditions run through the upper-middle, eastern/weird traditions sit in the lower-middle (*Eastern philosophy & antiquity* → *Lord Dunsany* → *Clark Ashton Smith* / *H. P. Lovecraft* → *Jack Vance*), and adventure/sword-and-sorcery lineages follow the bottom (*Haggard & adventure fiction* → *Robert E. Howard* → *Fritz Leiber* → *Karl Edward Wagner* → *Glen Cook*).


* **Escalating visual density:** Reading left to right illustrates how fantasy grows increasingly hybrid over time, moving from bold, single-pattern foundational shields on the left to densely subdivided composite shields on the right.



## Rules for Building Each Writer's Arms

* **Foundational base motifs:** The earliest traditions establish clean, unquartered heraldic designs that act as visual DNA for the rest of the chart. For example, *Folktales & folklore* is a red field with three gold lozenges, *Arthuriana* is a white field with a red cross, *Northern & Celtic epics* is a gold field with a blue saltire, *Eastern philosophy & antiquity* is a green field with a gold ring, *Gothic & Romantic literature* uses black-and-white vertical stripes, and *Haggard & adventure fiction* is red with a white diagonal stripe.


* **Direct inheritance:** A writer descending primarily from one tradition adopts that predecessor's shield directly as their main field—such as *George MacDonald* and *C. S. Lewis* keeping the folklore field, *T. H. White* keeping the Arthurian cross, or *Mervyn Peake* keeping the Gothic stripes.


* **Central personal device:** As noted in the poster's key (*"The central shield is the writer's personal device"*), individual authors place a small shield (an inescutcheon) in the center of their arms to represent their own distinct contribution on top of their inherited lineage.


* **Quartering for multiple sources:** As stated in the key (*"Quartering combines sources"*), writers who draw on more than one tradition divide their shield into sections combining those source arms. Two-source writers use classic 2×2 quartering, with the order reflecting emphasis—for instance, *Clark Ashton Smith* and *H. P. Lovecraft* both combine *Lord Dunsany*'s green-and-gold ring with Gothic stripes, simply swapping which motif occupies quarters 1 & 4 versus 2 & 3. Writers with more influences divide the shield into larger multi-quarter grids.


* **Nested quarters for composite ancestry:** Following the rule that *"Earlier arms survive inside later shields"* and *"Nested quarters carry earlier ancestry,"* inheriting from a writer whose arms are already quartered shrinks that entire composite shield into a single quarter of the new author's arms. Across several generations, this creates recursive miniature sub-quarters—most visibly in late-century shields such as *George R. R. Martin*, *Neil Gaiman*, *Terry Pratchett*, and *Glen Cook*.


* **Colour inversion for rebellion:** Matching the subtitle's theme of *"rebellion,"* writers who deliberately subvert or counter an inherited tradition invert its colours. This stands out clearly along the top row, where both *Philip Pullman* (reacting against the *Folktales* / *C. S. Lewis* lineage) and *Angela Carter* (in the folklore quarters paired with her Gothic stripes) flip the classic red field with gold lozenges into a gold field with red lozenges.

## Errors, Rule Inconsistencies, and Misleading Features

### 1. Broken "Personal Central Shield" Rule

Sheet 1 states that *"The central shield is the writer's personal device"*, yet several shields on Sheet 2 break or muddy that rule:

* **Andrzej Sapkowski has no personal device at all:** His 3×3 grid simply places his fifth source, **Poul Anderson**, in the center cell `(2,2)` (with Anderson's own central shield), omitting any central shield for Sapkowski himself.


* **Terry Brooks and Stephen Donaldson have no personal devices of their own:** Both retain **Tolkien's** blue-with-gold-chevron central shield unchanged (upright in Brooks, rotated 180° in Donaldson).


* **Robert Jordan appears to reuse Tolkien's central device:** His central shield is also dark blue with a gold chevron, visually identical to Tolkien's own inescutcheon.


* **George R. R. Martin's personal device copies an existing lineage motif:** His central shield is a blue field with a gold saltire—the exact field design used by *William Morris*, *E. R. Eddison*, and *Poul Anderson*, which invites misreading his personal mark as a saltire-lineage source.



### 2. Contradictions Around Colour Reversal and Rebellion

Sheet 2 is subtitled *"A closer look at assembly and rebellion"* and gives only one explanatory rule at the bottom: *"Only the contested quarter changes colour; other sources keep their own arms."* Several shields conflict with or go beyond this rule:

* **Unexplained colour reversal in the saltire lineage:** On Sheet 1, *Northern & Celtic epics* is a **gold field with a blue saltire**. Yet *William Morris*, *E. R. Eddison*, and *Poul Anderson* all reverse those colours to a **blue field with a gold saltire**, while *Robert E. Howard* and *Tolkien* `(1,1)` keep the original gold-and-blue. Inside Tolkien's and Moorcock's shields, both palettes sit side by side—making Morris, Eddison, and Anderson look like colour-reversed (contested) quarters under the poster's own rule, even though they are ordinary influences.


* **Stephen Donaldson breaks the colour-reversal rule:** Donaldson signals rebellion not by changing any colours, but by rotating Tolkien's entire shield—outline, quarters, and central device—**180° upside down**. Sheet 2's footer never mentions upside-down reversal.


* **Unexplained heraldic labels on Brooks and Jordan:** Both **Terry Brooks** (a white **3-point label** across the top) and **Robert Jordan** (a white **5-point label** across the top of his entire shield) rely on traditional heraldic cadency marks for direct heirs (eldest son and grandson), a mechanism completely absent from Sheet 2's footer. Worse, Jordan's full-width label cuts across both his *Tolkien* quarter (Q1) and his *Robert E. Howard* quarter (Q2), visually making Q1 look like *Terry Brooks* and obscuring the top of *Howard*.


* **Only two shields on Sheet 2 actually match its footer:** **Angela Carter** (inverting *Folk tales & folklore* in Q1 & Q4 while keeping *Gothic* normal) and **Andrzej Sapkowski** (inverting *Arthuriana* in `(1,2)` from white/red to red/white while keeping his other 8 cells normal). Prominent Tolkien critics/subverters on the same sheet—**Moorcock**, **Martin**, and **Pratchett**—have completely uncontested, normal-coloured Tolkien quarters.


* **Contested vs. uncontested Arthuriana inside Sapkowski:** Sapkowski inverts *Arthuriana* in `(1,2)` (red field, white cross) while keeping *T. H. White* uninverted in `(3,1)` (white field, red cross). Because T. H. White's arms are visually just Arthuriana's cross (and cropping hides any White personal device), the same motif reads simultaneously as contested and uncontested within one shield.



### 3. Structural Distortions and Loss of Information

* **3-source quartering skews visual weight (Robert Jordan):** To fit 3 sources (*Tolkien, Howard, Brooks*) into a 2×2 grid, Jordan duplicates Q1 (*Tolkien*) in Q4. Because Q3 (*Terry Brooks*) is itself just Tolkien with a label, **three of Jordan's four quarters are Tolkien's shield**, visually dwarfing *Robert E. Howard*.


* **Shield-curve cropping erases sources and misidentifies nested arms:** Fitting 6-, 8-, or 9-cell rectangular grids into a pointed shield severely crops the bottom corners. In **Tolkien**, *Edwardian tales* and *Eddison* are heavily truncated; in **Moorcock**, *Tolkien*'s *MacDonald* and *Eddison* cells are cut off entirely; and in **Sapkowski**, *Roger Zelazny* `(3,3)` is cropped down to a single green-and-gold ring, making him visually indistinguishable from *Eastern philosophy & antiquity* or *Lord Dunsany* without reading the caption.


* **Untraceable routing channels on Sheet 1:** Because Sheet 1 bundles all 84 source relations into dense, monochrome, unlabelled vertical channels, the line network cannot reliably be traced across multiple columns, forcing readers to rely on micro-captions or already-known shield motifs to verify ancestry.


# 5

**8 / 10.** Turning literary genealogy into a generative heraldic system—where real rules of marshalling, cadency, and reversal act as a visual grammar for influence and rebellion—is genuinely original and unusually well matched to fantasy fiction. It stops short of a 9 or 10 because the system's legibility breaks down under its own mathematical weight in later generations, and several rule inconsistencies undercut its precision as a readable data visualisation.

## Most Original Element

**Using formal heraldic grammar as a generative algorithm rather than mere illustration.** Most "coats of arms of famous authors" projects invent bespoke thematic clipart for each writer. Here, every shield is *constructed* from its predecessors' visual DNA: foundational motifs combine through quartering, multi-generation influences shrink into recursive nested sub-quarters, direct heirs receive traditional heraldic cadency labels (the 3-point white label across *Terry Brooks* and 5-point label across *Robert Jordan*), and literary rebellion is encoded through heraldic inversion—colour-reversed contested quarters in *Angela Carter*, *Andrzej Sapkowski*, and *Philip Pullman*, or an outright 180° inverted shield of abasement for *Stephen Donaldson*. Even before you read a single name, scanning the full poster from left to right makes the historical evolution of fantasy visceral: clean, bold archetypal fields gradually fracture into densely woven patchwork hybrids.

## Most Predictable Element

**The chronological swimlane layout and largely conventional canon.** Beneath the heraldic invention sits a very standard left-to-right timeline divided into six familiar historical stages (*Traditions*, *Early Foundations*, *Myth & the Weird*, *Mid-Century*, *New Directions*, and *Late Century*) linked by orthogonal routing lines. The literary map itself also follows a well-trodden, heavily Anglophone spine—tracing fairy tales through *MacDonald* and *Lewis*, weird fiction through *Dunsany* and *Lovecraft*, sword-and-sorcery through *Howard* and *Leiber*, and positioning *J. R. R. Tolkien* as the genre's gravitational centre for *Brooks*, *Donaldson*, *Jordan*, *Martin*, and *Pratchett* (with *Stanisław Lem* and *Andrzej Sapkowski* standing out as rare non-Anglophone inclusions).

## Weakest Part

**Scalability and rule breakdown under recursive nesting.** The exact mechanism that makes the concept brilliant also causes the visualisation to fail at high complexity:

* **Destructive cropping in multi-source shields:** Forcing 6-, 8-, and 9-source rectangular grids—themselves filled with nested quarters—into pointed shield outlines slices away bottom-corner ancestors. Inside *Michael Moorcock*, parts of *Tolkien*'s shield disappear beneath the lower-right curve, and inside *Andrzej Sapkowski*, *Roger Zelazny*'s composite arms are cropped down to a single green-and-gold ring, making them visually indistinguishable from *Eastern philosophy & antiquity* or *Lord Dunsany* without checking the text caption.


* **Untraceable line bundles:** Routing 84 source relations through tightly packed, monochrome vertical channels on the full chart makes multi-column connections nearly impossible to follow by eye.


* **Inconsistent visual rules:** Unexplained colour flips in the saltire lineage (*William Morris*, *E. R. Eddison*, and *Poul Anderson* reversing *Northern & Celtic epics*' gold-and-blue palette despite ordinary influence), missing personal central devices (*Sapkowski*, *Brooks*, and *Donaldson*), and an incomplete key on the close-up panel that omits both upside-down inversion and cadency labels force the reader to guess whether a visual quirk is deliberate symbolism or a design slip.



As a poster-sized conceptual piece, it succeeds memorably because the metaphor itself tells a compelling story about how genres compound and push back against their ancestors; tightening its visual grammar and solving the late-century crowding would push it from clever concept art into a truly airtight information graphic.

## Why Most Arms Are Unreadable Without Names

* **Purely abstract geometry:** Every foundational tradition and every writer's central personal shield is built from non-figurative shapes—crosses, saltires, bends, lozenges, rings, stripes, chevrons, and checks. None of those shapes point directly to an author's stories or themes, and some even invite misreading—the prominent gold ring on a green field represents *Eastern philosophy & antiquity* and *Lord Dunsany*, not Tolkien.


* **Visual near-duplicates within single-source lineages:** Writers who descend mainly from one predecessor share the exact same background field and differ only by tiny, arbitrary central shields. Without reading labels, *George MacDonald*, *C. S. Lewis*, and *Stanisław Lem* all share the red field with three gold lozenges; *William Morris*, *E. R. Eddison*, and *Poul Anderson* all share the blue field with a gold saltire; *Walter Scott* and *T. H. White* both sit on *Arthuriana*'s white field with a red cross; and *Clark Ashton Smith* and *H. P. Lovecraft* are simply mirror-swapped 2×2 combinations of the same two motifs.


* **Dense, cropped late-century mosaics:** In the rightmost column, composite shields such as *George R. R. Martin*, *Robin Hobb*, *Neil Gaiman*, *Gene Wolfe*, *Terry Pratchett*, and *Glen Cook* break down into crowded grids of tiny, edge-cropped sub-quarters that cannot be mapped to specific writers by eye alone.


* **The few deducible exceptions:** A knowledgeable viewer who already understands the chronological columns and rules could reasonably guess four or five outliers strictly from structure and subversion:


* **J. R. R. Tolkien** stands out in the *Mid-Century* column as an unusually massive 8-source hub whose composite shield is then inherited wholesale by multiple later writers.


* **Terry Brooks** and **Stephen Donaldson** sit immediately to Tolkien's right bearing *only* Tolkien's arms—one adding a traditional 3-point heir's label and the other flipping the entire shield upside down—signalling "direct heir" and "explicit anti-Tolkien rebel".


* **Angela Carter** (colour-inverted *Folktales* quartered with *Gothic* stripes) and **Philip Pullman** (a late-century whole-shield colour inversion at the far end of the *Folktales* → *MacDonald* → *Lewis* line) stand out cleanly to anyone who knows their famous subversions of fairy tales and Narnia.





## What Would Make Each Writer's Arms Recognisable

Keeping the poster's generative inheritance system intact while making individual authors recognisable on sight would require giving the *personal* layer of each design real semantic weight—using techniques drawn straight from historical heraldry:

* **Figurative charges in the central shield:** The armorial already defines the central shield as each writer's personal device, yet fills it with generic geometric marks (and omits it altogether on *Terry Brooks*, *Stephen Donaldson*, and *Andrzej Sapkowski*). Placing a bold, iconic heraldic charge inside every writer's inescutcheon—such as a lamppost or lion for *C. S. Lewis*, an eight-pointed star for *Michael Moorcock*, a compass for *Philip Pullman*, a turtle for *Terry Pratchett*, a ruined tower for *Mervyn Peake*, or a wolf's head for *Andrzej Sapkowski*—would identify the author immediately while leaving the surrounding quarters purely genealogical.


* **Canting arms (visual puns on surnames):** Traditional European heraldry solves unlabelled recognition through *canting*—encoding a family name directly as imagery. A wavy blue band (*brook*) for *Terry Brooks*, a mountain *peak* for *Mervyn Peake*, a *wolf* for *Gene Wolfe*, a *martin* bird for *George R. R. Martin*, a cartwheel for *Angela Carter*, or a cauldron for *Glen Cook* would stay authentically heraldic while letting readers decode names without text.
* **Individual crests above each shield:** Historically, once aristocratic shields accumulate eight or sixteen quarterings, almost nobody identifies the bearer from the crowded grid inside the shield; they look at the **crest** mounted above it, which belongs solely to the individual. Adding a clean, single-symbol crest above each writer's shield would preserve the abstract geometric beauty of the inheritance system inside the shield while making every entry recognisable at a glance.
* **Semantic motifs for the foundational traditions:** Because the starting column relies on abstract ordinaries that must be memorised from captions, swapping them for unmistakable archetypal charges—a rose or spindle for *Folktales*, a crown or sword for *Arthuriana*, a knot for *Northern & Celtic epics*, a lotus or sun for *Eastern philosophy*, a pointed arch for *Gothic*—would let viewers read an unlabelled composite shield as a recipe of genres and deduce who wrote it.


* **Inheriting personal emblems rather than recursive grids:** Shrinking an entire 8-cell shield like Tolkien's into a single corner of *Michael Moorcock* or *Andrzej Sapkowski* blurs fine detail and slices off bottom-row ancestors along the shield's curve. If a descendant's quarter displayed a predecessor's single bold personal emblem instead of recursively nesting all of that predecessor's sub-quarters, even nine-source late-century shields would stay crisp enough to read like a visual bibliography.



As designed, the armorial works better as a **structural map of how genres compound** than as a portrait gallery of individual authors—it trades instant personal recognisability for the visual impact of seeing clean early patterns fracture into late-century complexity.
