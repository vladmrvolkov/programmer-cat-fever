Both diptychs illustrate the **commodification of dissent** (often called **recuperation** in media theory): the irony of how seeing through an illusion or rebelling against a system is quickly repackaged into a trendy consumer identity, conformity uniform, or piece of merchandise.

### 1. The Visual Breakdown

* **The Modern Consumer (*They Live* Parody):**
* **Panel 1 (The Awakening):** Referencing John Carpenter’s 1988 sci-fi satire *They Live*, a man looks through special sunglasses at a glamorous lifestyle advertisement and sees the hidden subliminal command underneath: **"BUY"**.


* **Panel 2 (The Co-optation):** Rather than rejecting consumerism after learning the truth, he walks into a store still wearing the "truth-revealing" sunglasses as a fashion statement and proudly buys a T-shirt with **"BUY"** printed across the chest.




* **The Ukiyo-e Cats (*The Emperor’s New Clothes* Parody):**
* **Panel 1 (The Awakening):** In a scene blending traditional Japanese woodblock prints with *The Emperor's New Clothes*, a crowd of cats bows before a sumptuously robed Emperor. Only one small kitten wearing round glasses sees the reality shown in the top inset: the Emperor is completely naked.


* **Panel 2 (The Co-optation):** Once the truth is out and the naked Emperor is exposed, the crowd joins in pointing and laughing—but they do so while wearing identical blue coats stamped with the kanji **裸** (*hadaka*, meaning "naked"). In the background, a shop is briskly selling those exact coats, commemorative prints, fans, and round glasses, leaving the kitten holding its head in dismay.

### 2. Core Themes Shared by Both Works

* **Illusion of Enlightenment:** Both narratives begin with the promise that "putting on the glasses" and seeing the truth will set you free from manipulation. Instead, awareness simply creates a new market segment.


* **Rebellion as an Aesthetic:** Being "awake" to propaganda or authority becomes a badge of social status. The man admires how cool he looks wearing the critique on his chest, and the cats buy matching uniforms to prove they are in on the joke.


* **Recuperation & Capitalist Realism:** Sociologist Guy Debord and cultural theorist Mark Fisher described how consumer culture neutralizes subversive ideas by absorbing and monetizing them. When exposing the system is turned into a T-shirt or a matching coat, the system still wins—you are still buying the product and following the crowd, just with an extra layer of irony.
