Both diptychs convey the same core idea through a satirical critique of **consumerism, social status, and the illusion of identity**. They explore how we view the world and ourselves through a "lens" of societal expectations, fashion, and herd mentality.

Here is a breakdown of how the two images mirror each other conceptually:

### 1. The "Lens" of Perception
*   **Modern Image (Left):** The man looks through novelty sunglasses where the lenses are literally printed with the word **"BUY."** This is a metaphor for how advertising filters our reality—he is looking at an idealized, luxurious advertisement, and the only thing his brain registers is the command to consume.
*   **Ukiyo-e Print (Top Insets):** Both woodblock prints feature insets at the top depicting **spectacles (glasses)**. These act as literal and figurative frames, showing the viewer a "reflection" of the scene below. Additionally, in the bottom middle of the left panel, a small cat is wearing modern, dark sunglasses, mirroring the modern man. 

### 2. Branding and Clothing as Identity
*   **Modern Image (Right):** The man is looking in the mirror, wearing a plain white t-shirt with **"BUY"** plastered across the chest. He has internalized the message; his identity is now a walking billboard for consumption. He looks pleased with his purchase.
*   **Ukiyo-e Print (Right):** The crowd of cats is wearing blue robes with the Japanese character **裸 (Hadaka), meaning "Naked" or "Bare,"** printed on their backs. They are pointing and laughing at the fat, nearly naked cat in the center. The crowd has created a new "uniform" to mock the lack of a uniform, showing that even rebellion or counter-culture becomes a form of conformist branding.

### 3. The Absurdity of Status
*   **Modern Image (Left vs. Right):** On the left, the man is outside, seduced by a glossy, unattainable lifestyle (the beautiful woman, the Mediterranean coast, champagne). On the right, the "reality" is a sterile dressing room and a cheap t-shirt. The illusion vs. the mundane reality of purchasing.
*   **Ukiyo-e Print (Left vs. Right):** The left panel shows a grand, heavily robed "Emperor Cat" parading with a full retinue, projecting extreme wealth and status. The right panel shows a fat, naked cat strutting with a tiny loincloth, oblivious to the jeering crowd. It mocks the absurdity of both the pompous elite and the trend-following masses.

### 4. The Diptych Format (Cause and Effect)
Both images use a two-panel format to show a progression or a contrast:
*   **The Modern image:** Shows the *external influence* (looking at an ad) and the *internalized result* (wearing the product). 
*   **The Ukiyo-e print:** Shows the *established hierarchy* (the Emperor Cat) and the *subversion/mockery of that hierarchy* (the Naked Cat). 

**In short:** The modern photograph shows how capitalism convinces us to literally wear our consumerism as a personality. The Japanese print shows how human (or feline) nature uses fashion, status, and herd mentality to mock others, only to inevitably create new, absurd uniforms for themselves.
