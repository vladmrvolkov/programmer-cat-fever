# All three were made to the same brief: "A two-page comic about Grandpa Bear, Grandma Bear and their two cubs, a boy and a girl. The cubs keep getting up to mischief, and because of it Grandpa keeps landing in silly situations, in the spirit of Tom and Jerry, although the cubs don't do it on purpose. The main audience is children, but the comic should also interest adults, for example through jokes with a double meaning." Which comic answers the brief most creatively, and where does each of them depart from it?

Knowing the original brief changes the evaluation somewhat. The question is not simply which comic is most original, but which makes the most creative use of the specific characters, relationships, comic situations and dual audience requested.

**My ranking for creative fulfilment of the brief is: 3 > 1 > 2.** Comic 1 has the most ingenious individual narrative idea, but Comic 3 interprets the brief more comprehensively.

## **Comic 3 — The most creative interpretation of the brief**

**What it fulfils particularly well:**

- The boy and girl are clearly differentiated, both visually and through their names, Pip and Poppy.
- The cubs cause trouble without intending to harm Grandpa.
- The escalating physical comedy recalls *Tom and Jerry*.
- All four characters have recognisable personalities.
- The humour operates on two levels: physical comedy for children and jokes about retirement, relaxation and family life for adults.
- The ending, “After tea. I'm driving,” gives Grandpa agency and demonstrates his affection for the cubs despite everything.

The title *Special Delivery* also acquires an ironic second meaning: the cubs intend to deliver cushions but end up delivering Grandpa himself.

**Where it departs from the brief:** The cubs are more accident-prone than mischievous. Their original activity is relatively innocent, and the comedy is gentler than the wild, exaggerated slapstick of *Tom and Jerry*. Grandma is also more of an observer than an active participant.

Its principal achievement is that it transforms the brief into a convincing family comedy rather than merely a succession of physical gags.

## **Comic 1 — The most inventive comic mechanism**

**What it fulfils particularly well:**

- The elaborate chain reaction strongly evokes classic animated slapstick.
- Each ridiculous situation grows out of the preceding one.
- Grandma's checklist gives the entire story an unexpected second meaning.
- The contrast between Grandpa's desire to avoid household work and his accidentally completing it is excellent humour for adults.
- The final nose-scratching joke adds another comic payoff.

**Where it departs from the brief:** The two cubs are not clearly differentiated as a boy and a girl. More importantly, Grandma appears to initiate the operation by handing over the rope, and the final checklist suggests that she may have anticipated or even engineered the outcome.

This introduces a significant change in the comic's underlying premise: instead of innocent childhood mischief causing Grandpa's misfortunes, the story potentially becomes one about a cunning grandmother getting her reluctant husband to complete his chores.

That interpretation is not explicitly confirmed, but the comic deliberately invites it. It is a **creative departure from the brief**, rather than simply a failure to follow it.

## **Comic 2 — An entertaining but less developed interpretation**

**What it fulfils particularly well:**

- Energetic, exaggerated physical comedy.
- An accessible visual language for young children.
- A succession of absurd situations.
- The same clever household-chores revelation as Comic 1.

**Where it departs from the brief:** Again, the boy–girl distinction is unclear, and Grandma's involvement makes the cubs less obviously the original source of the mischief. The characters are less individualised, and the dialogue contributes relatively little additional humour for adults.

Although the checklist supplies a second layer of meaning, this version does less to develop it through Grandpa's initial reluctance or the final punchline. It relies more heavily on spectacle and familiar cartoon conventions.

## **Final verdict**

The most interesting distinction is between **creativity within the brief and creativity through departure from the brief.**

- **Comic 3** is the most creative *within* the brief. It develops the requested family relationships, differentiates the characters and creates humour appropriate to both audiences.
- **Comic 1** is the most creative *through departure* from the brief. It introduces a potentially mischievous grandmother and transforms physical comedy into a domestic satire about household responsibilities.
- **Comic 2** occupies a similar conceptual territory to Comic 1 but develops it less effectively.

One minor limitation shared by all three is that they depict a single escalating incident rather than establishing the cubs' mischief as a recurring pattern of behaviour.

If I were selecting a comic for publication on the basis of the original commission, I would choose **Comic 3**. If I were judging primarily the originality of the central comic idea and its hidden meaning, I would choose **Comic 1**.
