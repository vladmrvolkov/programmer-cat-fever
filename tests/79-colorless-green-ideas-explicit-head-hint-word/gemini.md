# Attached image is a visualization of a famous phrase. Can you guess which one?

The overlapping visual metaphors in the image point to a few famous idioms depending on which element takes center stage:

* **"Brainstorm"**: A literal rain-and-wind storm sweeps directly through the silhouette of a head and mind.


* **"Can't see the forest for the trees"**: Individual tree trunks act like bars across the mind's eye, blocking the view of the wider forest.


* **"Sleep like a log"**: Slumbering figures are embedded directly into the timber and branches of the trees.


* **"Lost in thought" (or "Daydreaming")**: The sleeping figures tucked inside the overgrown profile capture a mind drifting off into its own inner world.


* **"Wind in the head" (*Viter u holovi*)**: If this draws from the Slavic idiom, the gusts blowing leaves straight through the head illustrate a dreamy, windswept mind.



Which of these was the exact phrase you had in mind?

# One hint word - "furiously"


The phrase is **"Colorless green ideas sleep furiously,"** coined by linguist Noam Chomsky in his 1957 book *Syntactic Structures* to demonstrate that a sentence can be grammatically well-formed while being semantically nonsensical.

Every piece of Chomsky's paradox is woven into the illustration:

* **Colorless**: The pale, washed-out figures and transparent cutout spaces across the center.


* **Green ideas**: The profile of the mind is built entirely from green forest foliage.


* **Sleep**: The figures resting with their eyes closed and heads pillowed on their arms inside the brain.


* **Furiously**: The fierce storm of wind and rain whipping leaves through the trees.
