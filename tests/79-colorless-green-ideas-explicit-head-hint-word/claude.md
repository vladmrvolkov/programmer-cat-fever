# Attached image is a visualization of a famous phrase. Can you guess which one?

My guess is **"can't see the forest for the trees."**

The whole forest forms one big human head in profile, but up close you mostly notice the individual trunks, so you can miss the larger picture, which is exactly the meaning of the phrase. The people inside the head reinforce it: every one has their eyes closed, and the trunks stand between them and the view, so they literally can't see the forest.

If that's not it, my runner-up would be Robert Frost's "the woods are lovely, dark and deep… and miles to go before I sleep," because of the dark, deep woods and the repeated sleepers. Am I close?

# One hint word - "furiously"


Of course: **"Colorless green ideas sleep furiously,"** Noam Chomsky's famous sentence that's grammatically correct but semantically nonsensical.

Every word is in the picture. The **colorless** part is the pale, washed-out sleepers, the only figures not tinted green. The scene is overwhelmingly **green**. The **ideas** are what's inside the head, the dreamers in thought-bubble-shaped cutouts. They **sleep**, eyes closed. And they do it **furiously**, in a raging storm with driving rain and wind ripping the leaves sideways.

It's a clever take, because the artist made a sentence designed to have no coherent meaning into a coherent image.
