My guess is **Plato's Allegory of the Cave**, reimagined as a corporate meeting.

The room is dark, and everyone is transfixed by something lit by a **fire**. The orrery's "sun" is literally an oil-lamp flame, just as the prisoners in the cave see things by firelight rather than sunlight. They're studying a **model** of the cosmos, a crafted representation, the way the prisoners mistake shadows of carried objects for reality. The **real sun is outside**, shut out by the closed blinds; only a thin sliver of daylight slips through on the right, and nobody is looking at it. The man presenting with a clicker plays the role of the figures behind the prisoners who manipulate the objects and control what they see. The lanyards, suits, and tablet make the point that the cave is still with us, in conference rooms where people take polished models, dashboards, and presentations for the world itself.

The composition also nods to Joseph Wright of Derby's *A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery* (1766), an Enlightenment-era painting about the wonder of scientific explanation. Swapping the Enlightenment setting for an office adds irony: what was once a celebration of illumination becomes a picture of people content with a lamp-lit imitation.

A close runner-up reading would be "the map is not the territory," but the fire, the darkness, and the blocked-out sunlight point pretty strongly to Plato. Was that what you had in mind?
