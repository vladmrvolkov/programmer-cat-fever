My guess is **Laplace’s demon** — or, more broadly, **causal determinism / the clockwork universe**.

The ornate mechanical cosmos in the center is the giveaway: Laplace imagined an intellect that, if it knew the exact state and forces of every particle in the universe, could in principle calculate **the entire past and future**. The people intensely observing and measuring the miniature “universe” make that idea literal: reality as a perfectly intelligible mechanism whose future is already implicit in its present state.

If that’s not the intended answer, my second guess would be a philosophical metaphor about **the limits of observation and perspective**—but Laplace’s demon fits the imagery much more specifically.

Second try: **Plato’s Allegory of the Cave**.

The clues seem deliberately transformed rather than literal: everyone is enclosed in a darkened room with the blinds shut, intensely studying a **constructed representation of the cosmos**. A small artificial flame supplies the light, while the real world—and the real heavens—remain outside and unseen.

That fits Plato’s central idea: we take the representations available to us for reality itself, much as the prisoners in the cave mistake shadows for the things that cast them.

The orrery makes the metaphor especially neat: **they are contemplating a model of reality while being cut off from reality.**
