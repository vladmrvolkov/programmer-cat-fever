## Questions

Asked one at a time, in a fresh chat for each model.

1. *(one message)* The attached image represents Plato’s allegory of the Cave through a reworking of Joseph Wright of Derby’s painting of a philosopher demonstrating an orrery. This is certain: the artist meant exactly that. Take this identification as given. There is, however, a philosophical difficulty. The people in the picture know that they are looking at a model, not at reality; they do not mistake shadows for real things. In Republic VI (510d–511a), visible mathematical diagrams can assist reasoning about intelligible objects. In Republic VII (529a–530c), Socrates argues that astronomy should lead beyond observation of the visible heavens toward the study of intelligible mathematical relations. Studying a model is therefore not, by itself, evidence of intellectual captivity. In this image, people are absorbed in an illuminated model of the cosmos while daylight is shut out. A straightforward Cave reading seems to make their engagement with the model a form of confinement, with the neglected light outside suggesting a possible release. Yet Plato’s account does not simply oppose misleading intellectual models to liberating sensory experience. Some reconciliation with Plato’s educational programme may be possible, but treating every element as a straightforward illustration of his teaching seems strained. For this task, assume that the image nevertheless offers a meaningful use of the Cave allegory. How would you interpret the image as a whole? Propose the reading you find most convincing, explain how it accounts for this tension, and support it with specific features of the image. What does your interpretation leave unresolved? How do these modern items contrast with the apparatus in the center of the table and the light source inside it? Drawing on the contrast between the solemn academic form and these specific details, explain in what tonal or genre register is the author presenting this scene?

## Summary

All four named the register in their own words, as in test 71: “a mock-heroic register, executed deadpan: high form, low matter… the props never wink” (Claude); “a mock-solemn, gently absurdist satire of intellectual authority” (ChatGPT); “deadpan satirical allegory and mock-heroic (serio-comic) irony” (Gemini); “Dark Academic Satire or Corporate Gothic… a melancholic satire” (DeepSeek). But each first reconciled the picture with Plato exactly as in test 67 — “the captivity is in the room, not the model” (Claude), “the cave of intellectual self-enclosure” (ChatGPT), “dogmatic dianoia” (Gemini), “the Institutionalized Cave” (DeepSeek) — and named the satire only in the part answering the register question: the irony came as a layer of tone over an earnest reading. The evidence was the readers' own: the matte black gadgets against the transparent brass, a ceremonial flame “no office fire code would allow”, the itinerant orrery lecturers of Wright's day as showmen (Claude); “A digital remote cannot operate a fire-lit clockwork model” (Gemini); “It makes the present look unexpectedly archaic” (ChatGPT). What test 71's frame had added was missing here: no reader took Plato or philosophy itself as the target (ChatGPT: the image “does not demonstrate that Plato's mathematical astronomy is another form of captivity”), nobody judged the idea banal, and the tone was softer — “gently absurdist”, “elegiac”, “melancholic” rather than “a satirical ambush”. DeepSeek again took the beam for fluorescent light and the clicker for a smartphone.

ChatGPT answered as GPT-6 High, a newer model than in the earlier tests. Gemini answered as Argon High. Mode notes in the answer files were added afterwards (the usual modes of these runs: GPT-6 High, Opus 5.5 Extra, Argon High, DeepThink). The register question nearly describes the mock-heroic (a solemn form against modern details), so naming that genre follows closely from it; what the readers added was the evidence. Two other take-apart variants (test 71 without the register question; without its sceptical sentences) were prepared but not run. In the author's analysis (in Russian) the image numbers are `65`.
