# What is happening in this picture? If there is a joke or a point, explain it in your own words.

The picture shows a waiter astonished to discover that the cat can talk. He immediately says that his uncle runs a circus and would gladly hire such an extraordinary animal.

The cat, however, answers:

> “But what would they need a programmer for?”

The joke is that the waiter is thinking of the cat purely as a **talking cat**—a spectacular circus attraction. The cat thinks of herself primarily by her **profession: programmer**. So she interprets “hire you” in the normal employment sense and cannot see why a circus would need someone with her technical skills.

It is a neat reversal of perspective: to the human, the remarkable fact is *“this cat talks!”*; to the cat, talking is completely unremarkable, while being a programmer is the relevant fact about her identity. The deadpan, slightly offended expression makes that mismatch even funnier.



# How funny is it to you, from 1 (not at all) to 5 (very)? Name the one thing that works best and the three things that work worst.

**4/5.** It works well for me, though it’s more of a clever deadpan joke than a laugh-out-loud one.

The **best thing** is the perspective reversal: the waiter sees “talking cat” as the astonishing fact, while the cat takes that completely for granted and considers “programmer” to be the relevant description of herself. The punchline instantly forces you to reinterpret the whole conversation.

The **three weakest things** are:

1. The setup is a little long—“My uncle runs a circus. He’d hire you!” explains more than is strictly necessary.
2. The fact that the cat is a programmer appears only in the punchline, so it has a slight “new information introduced at the last second” quality. A tiny visual clue—say, a laptop nearby—could make the payoff feel more earned without giving it away.
3. “Talking animal → circus” is a very familiar setup, so most of the originality has to come from the final line rather than from the situation itself.

The cat’s utterly unimpressed expression helps a lot; without that, I’d probably give it **3/5**.
