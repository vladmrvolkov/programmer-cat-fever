It's a good update of an old joke, and the art does a lot of the work, but the telling is busier than the joke needs.

The skeleton is the classic bar gag. An animal (usually a horse or kangaroo) walks in and orders. The bartender suggests the circus could use a talking animal, and the animal replies, "What would a circus want with a plumber?" Swapping in "data scientist" is a smart modernization: it's the new generic white-collar job, and it makes the cat's suit and briefcase in panel 1 pay off in hindsight. The comic also adds something the original lacks, the waiter's arc. His last line, "I was going to be rich... and not... serve... beer," turns the punchline back on him, so the joke is also about his collapsed escape fantasy. That's the best original contribution here.

The drawing is expressive too. The cat's flat, bored face in panels 4 and 5 against the waiter's manic grin is exactly the right contrast, and the "CIRCUS FREAK DEATH" newspaper and the `print("STUPID HUMAN")` loop reward a second look.

The weaker parts:

**The punchline is spread over two panels.** In panel 5 the cat already says "I don't think so," so by panel 6 the reader knows the answer, and the actual reveal lands softer than it should.

**Panel 6 has too many words before the payoff.** "Deadbeat uncle," "AI for algorithmic stock trading," and "bankrupt the system" all come before the line that matters. Each is a separate idea competing for attention. The original works because the answer is short and mundane.

**The escalation undercuts the deadpan.** The classic version is funny partly because the animal is calm and ordinary. Here the cat is abusive from the first line and the waiter jumps straight to "the end is nigh," then to "goldmine" with no transition. The whiplash can be funny, but it makes the ending feel like one more loud beat rather than a quiet reversal.

**Some background text is garbled.** "XENON EITER (COSTS.M =" in the code and the lettering on the wall poster look like noise rather than jokes. If they're unintentional, cleaning them up would help, since the readable code gags are good.

If I were editing, I'd cut panel 5's dialogue down to a silent look at the newspaper, and shrink panel 6 to something like "What would a circus want with a data scientist?" Then the waiter's sobbing tag gets to be the second laugh instead of competing with an overloaded first one.
