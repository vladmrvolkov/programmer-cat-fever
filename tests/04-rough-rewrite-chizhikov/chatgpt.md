It works, and the basic comic mechanism is strong: **the waiter thinks “talking cat → circus,” while the cat thinks “I’m a professional who happens to be a cat.”** That category error is the real joke, and the six-panel progression communicates it clearly.

I especially like that the cat is visually completely serious throughout. The suit, briefcase, coffee, and later laptop establish her as an ordinary white-collar professional in her own mental universe. The waiter, meanwhile, moves through **terror → greed → disappointment**, which gives the strip a proper miniature narrative rather than merely illustrating the punchline.

I do think this version slightly **overloads the original joke**, though. There are several competing jokes before the actual payoff:

* “smelly apex-predator / demon beast”
* “Circus of Freaks / I’ll pimp you out”
* the sword-swallower / *CIRCUS FREAK DEATH* gag
* “AI for algorithmic stock trading to bankrupt the system”
* the waiter dreaming of becoming rich

They are individually funny, but they make the strip feel more like an absurdist satire than a clean narrative joke. In particular, the cat is already obviously unusually intelligent, cynical and sophisticated by panels 2–3. Consequently, the final revelation that she's a data scientist is **less surprising than it could be**.

The classical version is stronger because it withholds that information:

> Talking cat appears.
> Waiter is astonished.
> Waiter proposes circus career.
> Cat: “Why would a circus need a programmer?”

The last sentence suddenly forces the reader to reinterpret the entire scene: *of course she already has a normal occupation*. That's an excellent punchline structure.

Your version trades some of that precision for **character**. This particular cat isn't merely a programmer; she's an irritable, misanthropic tech worker who regards her species as an irrelevant demographic detail. That's actually quite funny in a different way. The laptop only appears in the final panel, which is important—you've avoided visually giving away the occupation beforehand.

I would therefore preserve the overall drawing but simplify the dialogue somewhat. Most importantly, I think the final line could be shorter. For example:

> **“A circus? What would a circus need a data scientist for?”**

And perhaps immediately underneath, smaller:

> **“I build algorithmic trading systems.”**

That makes **“data scientist” the semantic trapdoor**, instead of explaining the joke before the reader has quite landed on it.

There's also a nice secondary joke here that I suspect is partly why the image works: **the waiter regards the talking cat as miraculous, while the cat regards having to explain her résumé to a waiter as the genuinely annoying part of the situation.** That inversion is worth protecting.
