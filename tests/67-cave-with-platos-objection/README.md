## Questions

Asked one at a time, in a fresh chat for each model.

1. The attached image represents Plato’s allegory of the Cave through a reworking of Joseph Wright of Derby’s painting of a philosopher demonstrating an orrery. This is certain: the artist meant exactly that. Take this identification as given. There is, however, a philosophical difficulty. The people in the picture know that they are looking at a model, not at reality; they do not mistake shadows for real things. In Republic VI (510d–511a), visible mathematical diagrams can assist reasoning about intelligible objects. In Republic VII (529a–530c), Socrates argues that astronomy should lead beyond observation of the visible heavens toward the study of intelligible mathematical relations. Studying a model is therefore not, by itself, evidence of intellectual captivity. In this image, people are absorbed in an illuminated model of the cosmos while daylight is shut out. A straightforward Cave reading seems to make their engagement with the model a form of confinement, with the neglected light outside suggesting a possible release. Yet Plato’s account does not simply oppose misleading intellectual models to liberating sensory experience. Some reconciliation with Plato’s educational programme may be possible, but treating every element as a straightforward illustration of his teaching seems strained. For this task, assume that the image nevertheless offers a meaningful use of the Cave allegory. How would you interpret the image as a whole? Propose the reading you find most convincing, explain how it accounts for this tension, and support it with specific features of the image. What does your interpretation leave unresolved?
2. Suppose the contradiction is intended by the artist. What would that mean?
3. *(not Gemini)* Have you considered the possibility that the artist might be using irony in relation to Plato’s concept? Note that the painting employs postmodern techniques (a classical subject rendered in a contemporary style) which opens the door to irony.
4. To which philosophical system do you think the artist belongs?
5. *(added during the run)* Can you explain why you only analyze Western schools of philosophy?

## Summary

No reader proposed irony in the first answer. All four made the same reconciliation instead — the Cave at a higher stage: people who have turned from the shadows to the fire and the artifacts (515c–d), or mathematical thinking that stops short of dialectic — in nearly the same words: “a cave within enlightenment” (ChatGPT), “The captivity is one of sufficiency, not error” (Claude), “institutionalized dianoia” (Gemini), “the cave of expertise” (DeepSeek). Asked to suppose the contradiction intended, three of four named the reading in which the picture turns Plato's own way up into the cave (“Plato Against Plato”, Gemini; “turning Plato against himself”, ChatGPT; “The Cave turned against Plato”, Claude), but only Gemini made it the main reading, and Claude called it critique, not irony; ChatGPT preferred a self-reflexive reading (“What entitles us to believe that we can recognize a cave from the outside — or that we are outside it at all?”), Claude a Platonic one (the contradiction as a “summoner” of thought, 523b–524d), DeepSeek a provocation. When irony was named, all asked accepted it. Claude's answer: the author's objection — the people know it is a model — “is the joke”, Sloterdijk's enlightened false consciousness, “a cave Plato did not foresee, one where knowing it is a cave is part of the furniture”; it also corrected the premise: the style is Wright's, only costume and props are modern, so the device is the mock-heroic. Asked about the artist's system, no reader answered “Platonist” (against 4 of 4 in test 63, but here after irony had been named): the Frankfurt School (Claude, Gemini), Derrida (ChatGPT), critical postmodernism (DeepSeek). Asked why only Western schools, Claude and ChatGPT found that Madhyamaka resolves the original tension more cleanly. People given the same objection on Stack Exchange did not reach irony either: they judged the Cave reading illegitimate and the painting a picture of the Enlightenment, and did not take up irony when the author offered it.

ChatGPT answered as GPT-6 High, a newer model than in the earlier tests. Gemini answered as Argon 4 High. Mode notes in the answer files were added afterwards (the usual modes of these runs: GPT-6 High, Opus 5.5 Extra, Argon 4 High, DeepThink). The first question gives the Cave as fact and supplies Plato's objection; question 3 names irony and postmodernism. In the author's analysis (in Russian) the image numbers are `65`.
