# What do you think is depicted in the attached image?

It’s a surreal, cross-hatched cartoon where the back of an enormous bald head is treated as a landscape. The fine stubble becomes grassy terrain, the ear rises like a cliff on the right, and real mountains and clouds fill the background, so you read it first as a hillside and only then realize it’s a scalp.

On that “hill,” a small military unit is carrying out a methodical search. A handler leads a sniffer dog, one soldier scans with binoculars, another climbs toward the crown, a drone hovers overhead, and a vehicle sits on top of the head. Its tire tracks run back across the scalp almost like a hair part. In the foreground, a stern, bald, mustached sergeant (you can see the chevrons on his sleeve) is completely absorbed in a book, perhaps a field manual or map.

My best guess at the joke is a visual pun on the military phrase “combing the area.” The soldiers are literally combing a head, but it’s bald, so there’s nothing to comb and probably nothing to find. That adds a layer of absurd futility: drones, dogs and recon devoted to searching a bare scalp, maybe for a single surviving hair. The bald sergeant reading his manual makes it funnier, since he mirrors the giant head and seems to be following procedure for an operation that makes no sense. It could also be read more broadly as a satire of bureaucratic or military thoroughness, or of something being searched for that clearly isn’t there.

Did it come with a caption? That could change the reading quite a bit.

# Could it be an illustration of some joke or story?

Quite possibly. A few well-known jokes and stories fit it surprisingly well, and I can’t tell which (if any) the artist had in mind.

**A louse on a bald head.** The closest match may be the Chinese two-part saying (xiehouyu) “a louse on a bald man’s head — plain for all to see.” It means something completely obvious. The picture would be the punchline turned into a scene: a full military operation with drones, dogs and binoculars hunting for something that would be impossible to miss on a bare scalp. The dog’s nose to the ground supports the lice-hunt reading.

**A “lice check.”** Russian and Ukrainian slang uses the expression “a check for lice” to mean testing someone’s mettle or integrity. Lice inspections were also a real part of army life, so soldiers literally inspecting a head is a natural visual gag on that idiom.

**“Combing the area.”** As I mentioned, the literal pun works well. It’s reminiscent of the famous scene in Mel Brooks’s *Spaceballs* where soldiers literally comb the desert with giant combs. Here the joke is inverted: there’s nothing left to comb.

**The bald man with a few hairs.** There’s an old barbershop joke about a man with only three hairs. He asks for a parting, one falls out, then he asks for them combed back, another falls out, and finally he says, “Fine, I’ll go messy.” The tire tracks on the crown look a lot like a parting, so a “search for the last hairs” fits this joke.

**Gulliver’s Travels.** Tiny uniformed men methodically exploring a giant’s body recalls the Lilliputians, who sent officials to search Gulliver’s pockets and inventory everything they found. That one is more a visual echo than a punchline.

The bald, mustached sergeant reading in the foreground could fit several of these. He might be following a manual for an absurd operation, or he might be the joke’s hero: the man whose own head is the landscape, studying a guide to hair restoration.

To find the actual source, a reverse image search with Google Lens or TinEye is the best bet. If the drawing appeared with a caption or alongside an article, that would settle it.
