My guess is **the Enlightenment**. More specifically, this is a modern office version of Joseph Wright of Derby's *A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery* (1766), which is often used as the defining image of the Age of Enlightenment.

The composition follows the original closely:

- **The orrery with a flame at its center.** In Wright's painting a lamp stands in for the sun. It's the only light in the room, so knowledge literally lights up the listeners' faces.
- **The figures.** There's a white-haired lecturer, a man at the side taking notes (a tablet here instead of a notebook), a thoughtful man with his hand on his chin, two wide-eyed young people leaning in, and a dark figure in the foreground with his back to us.
- **The modern touches.** Lanyards, paper coffee cups, a laptop, a hoodie, and a ring-shaped pendant lamp that echoes the orbits.

I also briefly considered Plato's cave: a dark room, a single fire, a big shadow on the wall, a figure with his back to us. But here people are looking at the light source itself, not at shadows. That fits the Enlightenment much better, or maybe even the moment in Plato when the freed prisoner first turns toward the fire.
