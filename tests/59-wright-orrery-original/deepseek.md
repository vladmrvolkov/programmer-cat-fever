Yes, the concept is **the Enlightenment** (also known as the Age of Enlightenment).

The painting is *A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery* (1766) by Joseph Wright of Derby. It is widely considered a defining visual manifesto of 18th-century Enlightenment philosophy, capturing its core ideas through a dramatic, theatrical scene.

Here is how the painting illustrates the concept:

*   **The Central Metaphor of Light**: The most striking element is the lamp placed at the center of the orrery, standing in for the Sun. This light source illuminates the faces of the onlookers, serving as a powerful visual metaphor for the "light of knowledge" or "light of reason" dispelling the "darkness of ignorance". The painting is literally an experience of "Enlightenment"—the word itself implies bringing light where there was none.
*   **The Shift from Divine to Human Reason**: The lamp is a man-made mechanism that has replaced divine light as the central source of illumination. This symbolizes a profound philosophical shift away from traditional religious models of the universe and toward an empirical, scientific approach based on observation and reason.
*   **The Power of Science and Education**: The philosopher, a figure of authority, is demonstrating the workings of the solar system using a mechanical model (the orrery). This celebrates scientific inquiry and the idea that knowledge is a force for moral and intellectual uplift, available to everyone, from children to commoners.
*   **A New Universal Order**: The diverse audience members are illuminated with varying intensities, their faces showing different phases of wonder, contemplation, and intense study. This represents the ordered, harmonious system of the universe, and the painting suggests that through science and reason, humanity can understand this order.

In short, the painting perfectly captures the Enlightenment's faith in reason, science, and the progress of human knowledge.
