**The delayed take is the real gag.** The barista processes the order first, on autopilot, and *only then* has his freak-out about "a talking cat." That reversal (react-then-realize instead of realize-then-react) is funnier than a normal double-take because it pokes fun at how service-industry politeness can override basic reality-processing.

**The deadpan/hyper contrast carries the page.** The cat's flat, faintly irritated expression against the barista's full-body shonen-style meltdown (sweat drops, wobbly linework, scattered pencil and notepad) is doing a lot of comedic lifting. She's the "straight man" who treats her own impossibility as the least interesting thing in the room — she just wants her coffee and pastry, and everyone else's shock is an inconvenience.
