I think it's **Plato's Allegory of the Cave**. The image is also a modern restaging of Joseph Wright of Derby's *A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery* (1766), moved into a corporate meeting room with lanyards, takeaway cups, a laptop and a tablet in place of the notebook. The composition follows the original closely: the note-taker on the left, the rapt faces lit from below, the man pondering on the right, and the silhouetted figure in the foreground.

Several details point to the cave:

- **The real sun is shut out.** The blinds are drawn against daylight, and the only light in the room is a small flame posing as the sun inside a mechanical model.
- **The shadow on the wall.** The presenter casts a huge, distorted shadow behind him, much larger than the man himself, like the shadows the prisoners mistake for reality.
- **The puppeteer.** He holds a remote and gestures over the device, putting on a show the audience can't see behind.
- **The captive audience.** Everyone is fixed on the model rather than the world it represents, and the hooded figure in the center has his back to us, facing the display like a chained prisoner.

The unlit ring lamp hanging above him like a halo also adds a nice ironic touch about the "guru" in the room.

If that's not what you had in mind, my runner-up would be the clockwork universe or watchmaker analogy, since the orrery is that idea's classic symbol. Which one were you thinking of?
