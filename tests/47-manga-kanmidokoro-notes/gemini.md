# Why do you think the waiter brought her a fork (since daifuku is eaten with one's hands)?

The comic highlights this detail intentionally, framing the daifuku, the fork, and her fluffy paw side-by-side in a dramatic close-up at the bottom of the first page. Bringing a fork instead of letting her eat by hand—or providing a traditional wooden *kuromoji* pick—serves several practical and narrative purposes:

* **The practical dilemma of fur and starch:** *Daifuku* is dusted with fine potato starch (*katakuriko*) and filled with sticky sweet bean paste. Eating it with bare paws would get powder and sticky paste matted into her thick white fur.


* **Preserving professional dignity:** A regular cat would clean food off its paws by licking them. For an upright corporate professional in a business suit and company ID badge, grooming herself at the table would shatter her sleek, professional composure.


* **Protecting the keyboard:** As revealed on the next page, she came to the café to code on her laptop. Sticky, starch-covered paws would ruin her trackpad and keyboard.


* **The Western coffee pairing:** She explicitly orders "coffee with cream" alongside the traditional sweet, rather than hot green tea. This blend of Western and Japanese café culture is reflected in the tableware: coffee service typically includes Western metal cutlery rather than a bamboo or spicebush *kuromoji*.


* **The flustered waiter's improvisation:** In his panicked state after realizing his customer is a talking animal, the waiter scrambles to be accommodating. Not knowing how an anthropomorphic cat eats—and wanting to treat her as a refined patron rather than an animal—he defaults to handing her a fork.
