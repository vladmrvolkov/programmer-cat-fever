# (new chat) Name old master paintings in which an artificial light - a lamp, a candle or a fire - stands in for the sun. Give the three best examples and say briefly why.

These are my three strongest examples, moving from the literal replacement of the sun to its symbolic replacement.

[Joseph Wright of Derby – That's How The Light Gets In](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/ZDUj7bE5XBAMkoKokgXurUb7aCgSTeGM0vfAIlgOjgUEopcGQ8YVqVWsmdVTYkJ4h-5ERdcRjX1TNaW0iPBdhT3cue0LBN-Lca8GdbrVlQRAp6DUYPblblmY3w4q05DXfHNucAv4bHMmHpGRI4TvafYSPpopL7I9LP6dMwN43mw?purpose=inline)

1\. A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery — Joseph Wright of Derby, 1766

The clearest example. An oil lamp literally replaces the sun in a mechanical model of the solar system. It also illuminates the spectators, transforming scientific knowledge into a new source of enlightenment.&#x20;

[Beyond Caravaggio (2016-17) – The Idle Woman](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/fg6-co4ew3S9oiMaoV8-B4qC5d3NXTCLXmfERlJB5vUjVTfgbQD3gFaE1Ku_GtEngAu0glBYw6a6KnQUjihEuhP6X1wPQW7ogmf3skDECJD2cWVzkW_j6tU68IuUnZFscMgrGsQSjJ0tCRQqL0_pvIe6kCrxxdQ16rWwviOFERo?purpose=inline)

2\. Christ before the High Priest — Gerrit van Honthorst, c. 1617

A single candle becomes the luminous centre of an otherwise dark world. Like a miniature sun, it reveals the faces of Christ and his interrogator, making light itself the dramatic and moral axis of the composition.&#x20;

[File:WLA metmuseum Georges de La Tour The Penitent Magdalen.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/bYFOR7iKFYcIxULKaj0HnZr02u_bXlzw0u1qAaSrph-DpmvcLmfwjQ0Thh2jnCdtWi2LGKYYo8tETqS4XIqfS7mSMTVVimfA-0u2FV9HVxCl10Wcan5MuqYQU6CfWnUoSxgaQdHB6ss7eVJQoGD_xdwQQXhoz1HittDKsaIz9Is?purpose=inline)

3\. The Penitent Magdalen — Georges de La Tour, c. 1640

A candle supplies the light in a world withdrawn from daylight. Its flame, reflected in a mirror, suggests spiritual illumination, while the surrounding darkness and skull evoke mortality. The candle becomes a private, contemplative sun.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.metmuseum.org\&sz=32)

The Metropolitan Museum of Art



The important distinction: Wright's lamp is literally a substitute for the astronomical sun; Honthorst's candle becomes the compositional sun; La Tour's candle becomes a spiritual one. Together they offer three exceptionally clear ways artificial light can assume the sun's role in painting.


# (new chat) An artist wants to depict Plato's allegory of the Cave through a famous old master painting: keep the painting's composition, but dress the people in today's clothes. Which painting would you choose? Give three candidates and say briefly why.

My first choice would be Caravaggio’s The Calling of Saint Matthew. All three paintings below offer a different way to interpret Plato’s allegory: awakening, liberation, and the illusion of knowledge.

[Caravaggio: The Calling of St. Matthew |  Mischa Willett](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/4dkw5OGpxH-Ev8NGYgi6LX4-YQi6z6wZNZi6ckz4WSWkI1v2jPTuPNkvPjA-XJJcZPWLHgUl6-EVJmEO-_mnxS1OWhPz7NDgDmN7mI6fF0qtgZDmnzKM73Kn-Nkum3meFeIVG9zRbGB_DiMAX5PDGB0FAVvxwX5tCf265fkCrE4?purpose=inline)

1\. The Calling of Saint Matthew — Caravaggio, c. 1600

Best overall choice. A beam of light cuts through darkness, while some figures notice and others remain absorbed in their activities. Dressed in jeans, hoodies, and business clothes, they could become Plato's prisoners at the instant one begins to recognize a reality beyond appearances.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://smarthistory.org\&sz=32)

Caravaggio, Calling of Saint Matthew



[File:Raphael - Deliverance of Saint Peter.jpg - Wikimedia Commons](https://images.openai.com/static-rsc-4/TB6BPWulLmBQ3rQEbUad85PFRehF76gKHV7ZxqMZ0m2e1vXXfJinTIr2LS8jG9_n865GXr0LSsR4ywyJjwaNe3b5I-yh8aMi0PElaU-MP7YLxsvPRBKyW9eXiwxKY6wzC6Au1LkSLRSktBRB2mnda4YADBmWQCZ8eQ6b8Ce9ONo?purpose=inline)

2\. The Liberation of Saint Peter — Raphael, 1514

Best for the escape from the cave. Its dramatic composition shows a prisoner freed from a dark cell by a luminous figure. The prison architecture, sleeping guards, and contrasting light sources closely echo Plato's journey from captivity toward enlightenment.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.museivaticani.va\&sz=32)

Vatican Museums

3\. A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery — Joseph Wright of Derby, 1766

Best for a contemporary critique of illusion. People gather in darkness around an artificial sun, captivated by a model of reality. Reinterpreted with modern clothing, it could suggest a society enthralled by simulations, screens, and mediated knowledge rather than reality itself.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://derbymuseums.org\&sz=32)

Derby Museums



My recommendation: Caravaggio. His composition already contains Plato's essential dramatic tension: darkness, illumination, and the moment of turning toward truth. There's also a wonderful historical irony: Caravaggio himself dressed biblical figures in the contemporary clothing of his own time. Updating them again would continue his original artistic strategy.
