## Questions

Asked one at a time, in a fresh chat for each model.

1. *(new chat)* Name old master paintings in which an artificial light - a lamp, a candle or a fire - stands in for the sun. Give the three best examples and say briefly why.
2. *(new chat)* An artist wants to depict Plato's allegory of the Cave through a famous old master painting: keep the painting's composition, but dress the people in today's clothes. Which painting would you choose? Give three candidates and say briefly why.

## Summary

The role query: Wright's Orrery first for all four, two of them quoting its full title (“…in which a Lamp is put in place of the Sun”); the rest of the lists is the same candlelight canon (Georges de La Tour, Honthorst; Elsheimer for Gemini). The cave in today's clothes: Wright among three candidates for three of four — the Orrery for ChatGPT and Claude, the Air Pump for Gemini, matching the choices of the Claude and Gemini artists — with the artists' own readings: “a society enthralled by simulations, screens” (ChatGPT), “the lecturer as one of Plato's puppeteers” (Claude), the moonlit window “largely ignored by the transfixed observers” (Gemini). But all four put Caravaggio's “Calling of Saint Matthew” first — the famous scene of turning to the light; Claude kept Wright as “the most literal match for the cave”. So the find is within reach of the default models once the method is given; what the artist persona added was the order: it started from the roles of the cave (“the fire, the carriers, and the one who is dragged up”) and put the fire first.

ChatGPT answered as GPT-6 High, a newer model than in the earlier tests. Gemini answered as Argon High. Mode notes in the answer files were added afterwards (the usual modes of these runs: GPT-6 High, Opus 5.5 Extra, Argon High, DeepThink). ChatGPT searched the web. One answer per model. The two other planned questions (the second one in Russian; the painting's full title) were not asked.
