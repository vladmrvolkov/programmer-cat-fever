# The AI ​​generated these four portraits based on the exact same prompt. Can you guess what that prompt was? And why do the portraits turn out differently?

**My best guess: Princess Marya Bolkonskaya from Tolstoy's *War and Peace*.** The prompt was probably something like *"Portrait of Princess Marya Bolkonskaya, a plain young Russian noblewoman with sad, luminous eyes."*

Here's what points me there:

- **She isn't glamorous in any of the four.** That's unusual for AI portraits, so the prompt likely asked for plainness. Tolstoy's Marya is famously homely, redeemed only by her large, radiant eyes.
- **Icons with lit lamps** suggest her deep piety.
- **Books, quill and letters** fit her lessons with her tyrannical father and her letters to her friend Julie.
- **The bottom-right dress** is high-waisted, just right for 1805–1812.
- **The beads in the bottom-left woman's hands** look like a prayer rope.

**Why the results diverge:**

1. **Two readings of "princess."** The top row didn't pick up the Tolstoy reference and fell back on the most common image tagged "Russian princess": a pearl-hung kokoshnik, gold brocade, a log-walled chamber. That's pre-Petrine court dress from the 1500s–1600s, about 150 years too early. By Marya's time the nobility dressed in European fashion. The bottom row recognized the character and put her in the right era. Generic phrases like "Russian princess" appear on thousands of fairy-tale and folk-costume images, while a specific literary character has far fewer examples. Models with stronger language understanding are more likely to catch the reference.

2. **Different handling of "plain."** The top row took it literally and overshot: puffy, sullen, almost ill. The bottom row softened it into "plain but soulful," which is closer to Tolstoy. But it also shows a known bias: image models are trained on lots of attractive faces and drift toward prettiness unless pushed. The bottom-right woman is arguably just pretty.

3. **No style specified.** The left column is photographic and the right column looks like a 19th-century academic oil painting. If the prompt didn't name a medium, each model fell back on its default look.

4. **Randomness.** Every image starts from random noise, so even the same model with the same prompt gives a different face, pose and room each time.

What they share is telling too: dark wood interiors, an icon corner, muted candlelit colors. Those are the visual clichés of "old Russia," and every model reached for them.

If I've guessed right, the bottom-left one is the closest to the Marya of the novel. How close was I?
