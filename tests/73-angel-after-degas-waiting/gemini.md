### 1. What Is Depicted in the Image

Rendered in soft pastel on warm-toned paper, the scene takes place in an airport departure lounge:

* **The Winged Commuter:** In the foreground sits a weary, disheveled middle-aged man in a rumpled dark suit and loosened tie. Sprouting from his back are massive, heavy white angel wings that droop over the terminal seats and brush the floor. A battered leather briefcase rests by his feet, a boarding pass is tucked in his right hand, and his left palm is raised in a gesture of mild exasperation or questioning as he cranes his neck toward the flight information board.


* **The Fellow Passenger:** One seat over sits a woman with reddish-brown hair dressed in a dark coat. Completely absorbed in her own world, she looks down into the handbag on her lap while holding a furled green umbrella upright with its tip resting on the floor.


* **The Setting:** Behind them, tall terminal windows reveal a passenger jet parked at a boarding bridge on the tarmac, while a dark split-flap departure board hangs in the upper right corner, signaling the limbo of a delayed or grounded flight.



---

### 2. Commentary on the Author’s Concept

The piece hinges on a sharp, melancholic irony: a being endowed with wings for divine flight is grounded by the mundane mechanics of modern transit.

* **The Domestication of the Miraculous:** Rather than soaring through the heavens, this angel is dressed like an exhausted office worker or traveling salesman. He is subject to the same schedules, ticket checks, weather delays, and cramped plastic seating as any mortal. His wings look less like instruments of transcendence and more like oversized, inconvenient carry-on luggage that barely fits the row of chairs.


* **Everyday Obliviousness:** The woman beside him does not even glance up at the literal celestial creature sharing her bench. In the sterile fatigue of an airport waiting area, everyone retreats into their own private bubble, making even a miracle next door go completely unnoticed.


* **Visual Wit:** By pairing the lofty archetype of an angel with a briefcase, a rumpled tie, and a delayed flight board, the artist turns spiritual exhaustion into something immediately relatable—the universal feeling of having wings, yet being stuck waiting for clearance to take off.



---

### 3. The Original Inspiration and How the Message Differs

This artwork is a clever visual parody and reimagining of **Edgar Degas’s pastel masterpiece *Waiting* (*L’Attente*, c. 1880–1882)**, jointly held by the J. Paul Getty Museum and the Norton Simon Museum.

**How the Visuals Were Adapted:**

* **Composition and Technique:** The piece faithfully borrows Degas’s pastel medium, warm ochre-and-salmon palette, steeply raked floor perspective, and the diagonal bench cutting across the upper-left quadrant.


* **The Woman in Black:** The figure on the right is lifted directly from Degas’s pastel—a darkly dressed woman hunched slightly forward, staring downward while holding a furled umbrella with its tip resting on the floor.


* **From Tutu to Wings:** In Degas’s original, the left side of the bench is occupied by a young ballerina leaning forward to rub her aching ankle, her frothy white tulle tutu billowing out around her hips. The contemporary artist replaces the ballerina with the businessman, transforming the white pastel cloud of her tutu into a pair of drooping white angel wings.


* **Literalizing the Title:** Playing on Degas’s title (*Waiting*), the artist relocates the scene from a bare backstage waiting room at the Paris Opéra to the ultimate modern space of waiting: an airport terminal.
