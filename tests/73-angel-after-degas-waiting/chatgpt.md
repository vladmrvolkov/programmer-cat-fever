The painting depicts an unusual scene in an airport waiting lounge. A tired-looking man in a dark suit sits on a bench with a suitcase beside him and what appears to be a boarding pass in his hand. However, he is no ordinary passenger: he has enormous white angel wings. Next to him sits a woman who seems completely indifferent to his extraordinary appearance.

I think the artist uses irony and symbolism to explore modern life. Angels traditionally represent freedom, spirituality, and the ability to fly without limitations. Yet this angel is sitting in an airport, apparently waiting for a plane like everyone else. The paradox is striking: why would someone who already has wings need an airplane?

To me, the painting suggests that modern society has become so dependent on technology, rules, and routines that even a supernatural being might forget how to use its own abilities. The angel's weary posture conveys loneliness, exhaustion, and perhaps disappointment. The woman's indifference adds another meaning: people have become so absorbed in their everyday concerns that they no longer notice the extraordinary things around them.

The image also reminds me of Wings of Desire, directed by Wim Wenders in 1987. In that film, angels observe human existence, and one of them longs to experience ordinary human life. The film presents becoming human as a meaningful discovery, whereas this painting seems to suggest the opposite: by adopting human habits, even an angel may lose some of its freedom and individuality.

I cannot confidently establish that the film was the artist's direct inspiration, so this is a thematic comparison rather than a verified identification of the original image.

Overall, I find the painting both humorous and melancholic. Its central message could be expressed in one question: Have we become so accustomed to living within limitations that we have forgotten we possess wings of our own?

You're right. The image is a modern reinterpretation of Waiting (L'Attente) by Edgar Degas (c. 1882).

In Degas's original, a young ballerina sits beside an older woman, possibly her chaperone. The dancer bends forward, massaging her tired ankle, while her companion sits quietly with an umbrella. Degas reveals the exhaustion, anxiety, and hard work behind the seemingly effortless beauty of ballet.&#x20;

The modern artist preserves the composition, soft pastel technique, and atmosphere of waiting but replaces the ballerina with a man who has enormous angel wings. Instead of waiting backstage, the characters are waiting at an airport.

This change creates a humorous yet philosophical contrast. In Degas's painting, the ballerina represents grace and the illusion of weightlessness, but in reality she is exhausted by the physical demands of dancing. In the reinterpretation, the angel literally possesses the ability to fly, yet he appears tired and must wait for an airplane like an ordinary human being.

The main difference lies in the message. Degas explores the human vulnerability concealed behind artistic beauty, whereas the modern artist questions the limitations of contemporary society. The angel's wings symbolize freedom and unlimited possibilities, but his dependence on an airplane suggests that even extraordinary beings can become trapped in everyday routines.

Both works express weariness and anticipation, but the reinterpretation adds irony and absurdity. In my opinion, its central idea is that we sometimes forget our own abilities and freedoms because we have grown too accustomed to relying on external systems.
