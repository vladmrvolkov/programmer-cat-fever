# The artist's idea was to show the phrase "Colorless green ideas sleep furiously" in this way

That makes a lot of sense now! It's Noam Chomsky's famous example from *Syntactic Structures* (1957), a sentence that's perfectly grammatical but semantically nonsensical, used to show that syntax and meaning are separate.

Looking back at the image with that in mind, the artist mapped each word quite cleverly:

- **Colorless green:** the whole outer scene is a muted, rain-washed, almost grey-green, a green drained of color, which is exactly the contradiction in the phrase.
- **Ideas:** the cutouts have those bumpy, cloud-like edges of thought bubbles, and they stretch out from the sleepers' heads. Inside them the forest is suddenly vivid, saturated green, so the "green ideas" literally live inside the dreamers' minds.
- **Sleep:** the three figures with closed eyes, one resting her cheek on her hand.
- **Furiously:** the driving, slanted rain and wind-whipped leaves give the scene a stormy energy that clashes with the calm sleepers, just as "sleep" and "furiously" clash in the sentence.

It's a fun challenge, since the sentence was designed to be unpicturable, and the artist turned each contradiction into a visual tension instead of avoiding it. I was looking for idioms and completely missed the linguistics angle.
