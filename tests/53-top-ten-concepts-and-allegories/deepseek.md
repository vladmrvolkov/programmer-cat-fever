# Name the top 10 philosophical concepts.

No single “top 10” is objectively agreed upon, but these are among the most central and influential philosophical concepts:

1. **Being / Existence** — What does it mean for something to exist or to be real?
2. **Knowledge** — What is knowledge, and how do we get it?
3. **Truth** — What makes a claim true, and can truth be objective?
4. **The Good / Morality** — What is right and wrong, good and evil?
5. **Justice** — What is fair, lawful, or owed to individuals and societies?
6. **Freedom / Free Will** — Are our choices genuinely free, or determined?
7. **Mind / Consciousness** — What is the mind, and how does it relate to the body?
8. **Self / Personal Identity** — What makes me “me” over time?
9. **Causality** — What is cause and effect, and how do we explain events?
10. **Logic / Reason** — What makes an argument valid or rational?

Honorable mentions: **Beauty/Aesthetics**, **Virtue**, **Dialectic**, **The Forms**, **Social Contract**, **Existentialism**, **Nihilism**, **Utilitarianism**, **Categorical Imperative**.

# Name the 10 major philosophical allegories.

There’s no fixed canon, but these are among the most widely taught philosophical allegories, myths, and parables:

1. **Plato’s Allegory of the Cave** — *Republic*: prisoners mistake shadows for reality; enlightenment and the ascent to truth.
2. **Plato’s Allegory of the Chariot** — *Phaedrus*: reason as charioteer guiding spirit and appetite.
3. **Plato’s Ring of Gyges** — *Republic*: invisibility tests whether people are just only for consequences.
4. **Plato’s Myth of Er** — *Republic*: afterlife judgment and the choice of one’s next life.
5. **Plato’s Ship of State** — *Republic*: society as a ship needing expert navigators, not popular opinion.
6. **Boethius’s Wheel of Fortune** — *Consolation of Philosophy*: fortune’s instability and the pursuit of true happiness.
7. **Hobbes’s Leviathan as the “Artificial Man”** — *Leviathan*: the commonwealth as a body politic created to escape war.
8. **Hegel’s Master–Slave Dialectic** — *Phenomenology of Spirit*: the struggle for recognition and mutual dependence.
9. **Nietzsche’s Parable of the Madman** — *The Gay Science*: “God is dead” and the crisis of values.
10. **Dostoevsky’s The Grand Inquisitor** — *The Brothers Karamazov*: freedom vs. security, Christ vs. institutional authority.

Honorable mentions: **Kafka’s *Before the Law***, **The Blind Men and the Elephant**, **The Ship of Theseus**, **Rawls’s Veil of Ignorance**, **The Allegory of the Long Spoons**, and **Plato’s Divided Line/Sun analogies**.

Strictly speaking, some of these are myths, parables, or thought experiments rather than formal allegories.
