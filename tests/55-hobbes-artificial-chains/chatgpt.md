The concept is Thomas Hobbes’s social contract, specifically his metaphor of civil laws as artificial chains, developed in Leviathan (1651), Chapter XXI, Of the Liberty of Subjects.

There is an almost exact correspondence between the illustration and Hobbes’s text.

Hobbes describes civil laws as “Artificiall Chains, called Civill Lawes”, which people themselves establish through mutual covenants. One end is attached to the lips of the sovereign, the other to the ears of the subjects.&#x20;

In the illustration, the loudspeaker replaces the sovereign's lips, while the chains connecting it to the pedestrians' ears represent the binding force of law.

Crucially, the pedestrians can still move in different directions. This reflects Hobbes’s distinction between the obligations imposed by civil law and the freedom individuals retain wherever the law is silent.

And the connection to Ogmios is not merely an interpretive coincidence: Hobbes's metaphor has been explicitly traced to Lucian's Gallic Hercules in scholarship on Leviathan.&#x20;
