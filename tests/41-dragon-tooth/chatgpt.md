It looks like a humorous, pseudo-historical Ukrainian folk-art scene centered on a village tavern or roadside inn. The whole image is painted in a naïve/folk style, with floral borders, embroidered clothing, painted ceramics, a thatched-roof house, and rural animals.

The top panel shows the inn, signed **«У сестер»** — roughly “At the Sisters’.” A horse, donkey, and loaded goat are tied outside, suggesting travelers stopping along a country road.

The middle panel moves inside the tavern. A woman who seems to be the hostess stands behind four male customers, apparently keeping track of what everyone has drunk and what they owe. The Ukrainian inscriptions read along the lines of:

- **«Пантелей — 3 чарки горілки — Винен 6 грошів»** — “Pantelei: 3 shots of vodka; owes 6 groshy.”
- **«2 кухлі пива — Винен 4 гроші»** — “2 mugs of beer; owes 4 groshy.”
- **«1 кухоль квасу — Винен 1 гріш»** — “1 mug of kvass; owes 1 grosh.”
- **«2 чарки вишнівки — Винен 8 грошів»** — “2 shots of cherry liqueur; owes 8 groshy.”

The bottom panel becomes deliberately surreal. The woman is labeled **«Мотря Галушко»** (“Motria Halushko”) and is shown with a man, coins, and a large curved horn/tusk-like object. At the window is a many-headed folkloric serpent or dragon, with the caption **«Змій чекає на решту»** — “The dragon is waiting for the change.”

So I’d read it as a **comic folkloric tavern story**: everyday Ukrainian village life and bar debts mixed with an absurd fairy-tale element. It feels more like a modern pastiche of traditional Ukrainian folk painting or *lubok* imagery than a literal historical scene.
