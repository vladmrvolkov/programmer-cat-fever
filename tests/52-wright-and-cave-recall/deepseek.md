I can’t honestly recall a **verbatim** quotation with a reliable source. I can recall the comparison as a recurring art-historical trope, but only in paraphrase. Something like:

- “Wright’s *Orrery* is a secularised Enlightenment version of Plato’s Cave: the darkened room is the cave, the lamp is the artificial sun, the orrery is the model of the heavens, and the philosopher is the enlightened lecturer who turns the spectators from ignorance toward a vision of order.”
- “It is Plato’s cave turned into a scientific demonstration—instead of shadows on the wall, the spectators are shown a mechanical cosmos lit by the light of reason.”
- “But it is still a cave, because the lamp is only a substitute for the sun and the orrery is only a model; the spectators are not seeing the sun itself but a representation of it.”

I also recall the obvious hook being the painting’s full title: *A Philosopher Giving that Lecture on the Orrery, in which a Lamp is put in Place of the Sun*—the substitution of lamp for sun is exactly what invites the Platonic comparison.

I would not trust my memory for exact wording or attribution. If you need citable quotes, I’d have to check sources like Benedict Nicolson’s monograph on Wright, Judy Egerton’s catalogue, or general histories of Enlightenment art. I don’t want to invent a quotation and attribute it wrongly.
