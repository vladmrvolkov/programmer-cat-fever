There is no single canonical or famous historical quote—from an 18th-century figure or landmark art historian—that definitively links Joseph Wright of Derby’s painting to Plato’s Cave. Anyone producing a polished, attributed period quotation on this pairing is almost certainly confabulating.

The connection between the two is instead a widespread **conceptual trope** frequently formulated in philosophy of science and art history seminars.

---

When scholars and critics draw this link, it typically takes one of two non-verbatim forms:

### 1. The Enlightenment Inversion

In Book VII of Plato's *Republic*, prisoners trapped in a dark cave mistake shadows cast by an artificial fire for reality; true enlightenment requires climbing out into the light of the actual Sun.

Wright’s title itself—*A Philosopher Giving that Lecture on the Orrery, in which a Lamp is put in Place of the Sun*—deliberately points to the opposite mechanic:

* People willingly gather in a dark, cave-like room around an artificial flame.
* Rather than casting deceptive silhouettes that fool the mind, this man-made lamp illuminates the mechanics of the planetary system.
* Light here represents empirical reason and Newtonian order. Instead of escaping the dark room to find truth, truth is brought indoors through human manufacture and intellect.

### 2. The Technological Cave (The Media Theory Critique)

In media archaeology and post-Enlightenment critiques (such as analyses of 18th-century optical spectacles), the parallel flips again. Critics point out that the lecture creates an artificial, enclosed surrogate reality:

* Humanity has retreated from the vastness of the real cosmos into a domestic chamber, substituting a clockwork simulation and an oil lamp for the celestial sun.
* Media theorist Martin Reinhart summarized this transition in 18th-century astronomical viewing by noting that **"the shadows on the wall of Plato's cave have been replaced by sparkling stars,"** illuminated not by the heavens themselves, but by mechanical artifice.

If you remember hearing a line that placed Wright's *Orrery* inside Plato's Cave, it was likely an instructor or essayist describing the painting as an **inverted cave**—where artificial fire reveals physical truth rather than metaphysical illusion.
