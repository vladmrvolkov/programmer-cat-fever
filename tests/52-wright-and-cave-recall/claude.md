I can say why the connection would occur to people. The scene is a darkened room where a group sits facing an artificial flame that stands in for the sun, and the lesson depends on shadows, since the orrery's lamp was used to demonstrate eclipses. That matches the cave's setup closely: firelight in place of sunlight, and figures whose understanding comes through shadows.

The interesting part is that Wright arguably inverts Plato. In the cave, the fire and shadows are sources of illusion, and truth requires leaving for the real sun. In the painting, the artificial light and the shadows it casts are tools of instruction. The model is openly a model, and it points the viewers toward an accurate understanding of the real sun and planets. Any writer drawing the parallel would probably make some version of that point: Enlightenment demonstration as a cave that knows it's a cave.
