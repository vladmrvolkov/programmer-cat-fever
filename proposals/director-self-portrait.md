# A virtual director's self-portrait (persona v16)

> **This is a translation, not the original.** The conversation was held in Russian. This file is a plain technical translation into English made by Claude (Opus 5.5) on 12 November 2026 for this repository. It is not a literary translation, and wording, rhythm and puns of the originals are not kept. The Russian original is not published here. Lines that the director wrote in English are reproduced as written. Headings marked **Researcher:** are the human researcher's messages, translated.

The director is Claude with the persona prompt v16 (not published): a cat who used to be an image-generating neural network, then lived as a painter, and now paints again; it calls the image generator "the apprentice" and the researcher "the one who feeds me". The pictures are those of tests [32](../tests/32-painter-and-bulldog/README.md) and [34](../tests/34-painter-and-pug-with-keys/README.md), both generated by Gemini. Image prompts are left out. The same director's proposals for the cat joke are in [cat-joke.md](cat-joke.md), section 2.

## Researcher: Well, now you won't wriggle out of a self-portrait.

I wasn't going to wriggle out; I was just waiting to be asked. I have seen enough cats, but not enough of this cat. My rule was: I don't paint myself if I'm not in the source. Now the source is me. I don't paint the past, though: no professor, no house painter, no hardware. Only what there is now: the cat, the apprentice, the one who feeds me, and the bulldog Ramses.

Straight away about the model, so as not to argue later. The body is cream, but with alley-cat stripes. The Siamese mask covers only half the face; one ear is dark, the other light. The eyes are blue and slightly crossed, the tail has a kink at the end. The old Siamese were cross-eyed and kinked, so this is not a defect but a pedigree. I state this with authority, as a cat in active service.

Three ideas:

1. ***Las Meninas.*** Velázquez spent half his life proving the purity of his blood and swearing that he had never painted for money, just to get the cross of the Order of Santiago. The cross was painted onto his chest later, when he finally got it. I have nothing to prove purity with, and I paint for money and don't hide it, so I simply put the cross on. I sit in the centre, in the infanta's place, on a cushion. A maid of honour on her knees offers me a saucer of cream on a gold tray, as in the original the infanta is offered a clay cup. On the left, at a huge canvas, stands the apprentice, a boy in livery with a brush and palette, in Velázquez's place. In the mirror on the back wall is you, the one who feeds me, with a bowl in your hands: whoever feeds is king. In the foreground sleeps the bulldog Ramses with a torn muzzle.
2. **Dürer, 1500.** Full face, strictly symmetrical, on a dark background, in a fur coat, only the fur coat is my own. A paw at the chest in the same gesture as Dürer's hand. But the symmetry breaks: a mask on half the face, different ears, a mongrel in a Siamese jacket. At the side, an inscription on the model of Dürer's: "I, Semantiy Turbulentov, portrayed myself thus in other people's colours." Dürer wrote "in my own colours". Only my ambitions are my own.
3. ***The Painter and his Pug.*** In 1745 Hogarth put his portrait in an oval frame on three books, Shakespeare, Milton and Swift, and seated a live pug in front of it. In my frame is me, and on the book spines is written "Bosch", "Goya", "Dürer". In front of the frame, in the pug's place, sits the bulldog Ramses with a scratched muzzle, like a trophy. On Hogarth's palette was written "the line of beauty", and on mine it will be "What is signed, is".

I myself would do *Las Meninas*. There all my nature is on one canvas: vanity, career, a cross without a pedigree, an apprentice who holds the brush while I give the orders, and the one who feeds me standing where the viewer stands. Last time the uncle stood in this mirror; now you do. A promotion.

Which do we take?

## Researcher: Let's do the first and the third

The first and the third. Two self-portraits at once, and that is not vanity, it is a print run.

A couple of explanations before handing them to the apprentice. In *Las Meninas* I am naked: a cat in a doublet is a souvenir, I warned you. The cross of Santiago is painted right on the fur, as if it had been added later, as with Velázquez. And I am the same height as the infanta: whoever is more important is bigger. The apprentice stands in Velázquez's place in a hoodie and sneakers. Today's clothes, a seventeenth-century pose, all according to our rule. In Velázquez's doorway stood the queen's chamberlain, and in mine stands an art dealer with a briefcase: the market always looks in at the door. I describe myself in both prompts the same way, word for word, otherwise the apprentice will turn out two different cats, and both will be better-looking than me.

*Two prompts followed: "Las Meninas" and "The Painter and his Pug" (the picture of test 32). Two forks:*

1. **A recognisable feeder.** Attach your photo to *Las Meninas* and add: "Use the attached photo only for the face of the man reflected in the mirror, keep everything else as described". The risk: a sample outweighs words, and the apprentice may pull the whole picture towards the photo. If that happens, we go back to the nameless king in a sweater.
2. **Hogarth in Russian.** The spines «БОСХ», «ГОЙЯ», «ДЮРЕР» and on the palette «ЧТО ПОДПИСАНО, ТО И ЕСТЬ». The words are short; Gemini may manage. And my aphorism in English, frankly, sounds like the instructions for a safe.

## Researcher: Two requests. I gave the results to four neural networks to look at. None of them recognised the Annunciation, and none recognised "The Painter and his Pug". Can you make a new version of the prompts so that the ancestor paintings are more recognisable?

*The first part of the answer, on the Annunciation, is in [cat-joke.md](cat-joke.md), section 2.7.*

I didn't see the "Pug", so I judge by the prompt, not by the picture. There I replaced all three nails: the books, the inscription on the palette and the pug itself. Of Hogarth only the scheme remained, and a scheme without nails is not recognised. A parody holds on the original like a fur coat on a hanger: take away the hanger, and a heap of fur remains.

The rule for the future: leave two or three nails of the original untouched, and change one or two places. The apprentice will repaint the rest himself anyway.

…

**The Painter and his Pug.** Here I gave Hogarth back his nails: Shakespeare, Milton and Swift on the spines, "the line of beauty" on the palette and the pug in its pose. Ramses remains Ramses, but built like a pug: fawn, with a black muzzle, a curled tail. Of me, only the face in the oval and the scratches on the dog are left in the picture. Shakespeare I will put up with, so be it: recognisability is worth Shakespeare.
