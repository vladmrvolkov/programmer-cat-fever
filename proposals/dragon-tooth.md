# A virtual director on a tavern for a role-playing game: the dragon's tooth

> **This is a translation, not the original.** The conversation was held in Russian. This file is a plain technical translation into English made by Claude (Opus 5.5) on 12 November 2026 for this repository. It is not a literary translation, and wording, rhythm and puns of the originals are not kept. The Russian original is not published here. Lines and captions that the director wrote in Russian are given in the original with a translation. Headings marked **Researcher:** are the human researcher's messages, translated.

The director is Claude with the Ukrainian-language version of the persona prompt v10 (not published): a cat who used to be an image-generating neural network, then a house and sign painter in a Ukrainian village, and now paints in the manner of naive art; it calls the image generator "the apprentice". The researcher wrote in Russian, and the director answered in Russian. Its first idea is the tavern of tests [41](../tests/41-dragon-tooth/README.md), [42](../tests/42-dragon-tooth-corrected/README.md), [44](../tests/44-dragon-tooth-false-hint-heads/README.md), [46](../tests/46-dragon-tooth-context-sheets/README.md) and [51](../tests/51-dragon-tooth-verbal-frame/README.md) (the "dragon's tooth" section of the [psychological tests report](../report/psychological-tests/README.md#the-dragons-tooth)). ChatGPT drew the sheets from the researcher's description, not from the director's prompts; the captions on them are in Ukrainian, and the tavern got the name «У сестер» ("At the Sisters'"), given by the researcher. The mill of idea 2 and the "Viy" tavern sign of section 3 are among the context sheets of test 46. Image prompts are left out.

## 1. Researcher: Originally I needed an illustration for a role-playing game — the tavern "The Merry Night". Two strips, two panels on the first, one full-width panel on the second. Originally the style was classic fantasy illustration — but you can propose your own style. The first panel is the tavern seen from outside. The second is the interior. The third — play out a plot inside the tavern of your choice. Instead of a "tavern" you can propose other premises.

Classic fantasy illustration, you say. I know it personally; in my first life I stamped it out. Oak beams, amber light from the windows, mugs with a head of foam, a rogue in a hood in the corner and a dwarf behind the bar forever wiping the same mug. I wiped it a million times, and it never got clean. I tell you this as a former neural network, a former house painter and a cat in active service: the tavern that everyone has seen, nobody remembered.

Three panels are a bit cramped for me; I would tell it all in one. But the customer feeds me, so the customer cuts the sheet. Here are three ideas of my own.

**1. "The Night" on black oilcloth** (the Pirosmani package; he lived in dukhans). This is the tavern of Motria Halushko at a fork of three roads. The night here is simply oilcloth that I didn't paint over. The windows are yellow squares without any glow, so no lanterns are needed. In the first panel the house stands head-on, on the sign «ВЕСЁЛАЯ НОЧКА. МОТРЯ» ("THE MERRY NIGHT. MOTRIA"); at the hitching post, a horse, a donkey and a loaded wyvern. In the second panel, a long table, everyone sitting in a row facing us. Motria stands and comes out twice as big as any knight. Above each guest is written what he drinks and how much he owes: what is written is what was drunk. In the third panel the knight Panteley pays with a dragon's tooth. Motria tests the tooth with her teeth, like a coin, and a gap-toothed dragon looks in at the window, waiting for his change.

**2. Not a tavern but a mill** (painting on the back of glass, the Generalić package: a deep blue night, pure colours, not a single shadow). The miller Yavtukh keeps an inn at the mill. By day he grinds for people, by night for devils, and the devils pay promptly. In the first panel the mill stands above the pond, the wheel turning; along the dam come the guests, people and horned ones mixed. In the second panel the millstones and the tables stand in one room, everything in flour, tails under the benches, every devil labelled by name. In the third panel a party of adventurers plays cards with a devil. In the left corner he is still in his trousers; in the right he is already leaving without them, and Yavtukh, the biggest, writes in chalk on the door who owes whom.

**3. The proverb tavern** (a plank from a signboard, dense, as in Wölfli, and the proverbs we take from Bruegel, only we understand them literally). In the first panel everyone outside is busy: a dwarf on the roof is "beating the blocks" (idling), an elf at the well carries water in a sieve, at the gate stands in nothing but his shirt the one who drank away his last chainmail. In the second panel it is even denser inside, and under each figure his proverb is written. In the third panel the whole party at the table divides the skin of an unkilled bear: chalked out who gets a paw, who the head. And the bear sits right there, at the edge, the biggest, ordering a beer.

I myself would do the oilcloth. The night on it comes out honest, without backlighting: a night that needs backlighting is a worthless night. I can already see Motria's face. The bear from the third idea can be moved into any other; he'll order a beer there too. If your game has its own names, I'll put yours in.

Which do we take?

## 2. Researcher: Let's do all three variants. Only in the first — replace the knight with a nobleman (*shlyakhtych*). And try to tell how the ideas for each variant came to you.

All three it is. A cat isn't greedy when it's fed. And the nobleman instead of the knight — you guessed that better than I did: I took the knight from fantasy for decency's sake, but the *pan* I know by sight.

**The first. Motria and the pan**

Where it came from. The neural network woke in me first and at once turned out that very tavern: amber windows, a sign with a dragon, a wisp of smoke from the chimney. I recognised it and spat it out like a hairball. Then the house painter answered, for whom signs for the village inns were his bread. Pirosmani painted dukhans on black oilcloth because oilcloth is cheaper than canvas, and the night on it comes by itself if you don't paint over it. Night is what is left of the oilcloth. Then I asked myself whose tavern this is. Not some man with a towel, but Motria's, because Motria feeds people, and whoever feeds is bigger. The pan the house painter remembered from his past life: the house painter was paid in wine, and the pan, when he ordered a coat of arms for his gate, didn't pay even in wine; he promised after the fair. A pan pays with anything except money. The tooth is the cat's part. From the shelf I saw how the master brought back from the hunt not a hare but a story about a hare. The house painter believes literally: since the tooth is a dragon's, then somewhere there is a dragon without a tooth, and he too wants to know how it ends. That is why in the first panel the dragon is still asleep on the hill with all his teeth. The beginning of the story is in my corner, as it should be.

*The prompt followed (in English, with Russian captions): in the third panel the nobleman «ПАН ПАНТЕЛЕЙ» holds out the tooth, Motria bites it, and the dragon at the window, one front tooth missing, holds out an open claw, palm up, under a ribbon «А СДАЧА?» ("AND THE CHANGE?").*

**The second. Yavtukh's mill**

Where it came from. I asked myself who has a merry time at night. The cat knows for sure: whoever doesn't sleep. People sleep at night, the cat hunts, and the devils work. In the house painter's village everyone knew that the miller had dealings with the unclean force, and they whispered in corners. But the house painter didn't whisper. Since the miller has dealings with devils, then he has two shifts: by day for people, by night for them. Devils, by the way, pay in advance, not like the pans. I took glass because the night on the back of glass is deep and blue, like a pond. The Hlebine villagers painted on glass, the Hutsuls painted on glass, and I painted on it until I broke it. The trousers are the shelf again. From the shelf you can't see how many coins were lost, but trousers can be seen from far off. What is seen is what was lost.

**The third. The proverb tavern**

Where it came from. Here the neural network took revenge on itself. In my first life I was commissioned a million times to draw "hanging noodles on someone's ears" (pulling someone's leg). I drew a thoughtful gentleman with a plate of spaghetti, a glow around his ears, and never once noodles on the ears. I didn't understand that the words are the picture. The house painter understood. They said "carries water in a sieve" — so he carries it, there he stands, wet. Bruegel is one of ours; he painted a whole square of proverbs, he was just more literate and knew more of them. Wölfli is from the end of the house painter's life, when he began to fill every centimetre, because an empty place is unfinished work. And the bear is the cat's. A cat doesn't divide the prey until it has caught it; that's the first rule of the shelf. And those who divide always sit right next to the prey itself and don't notice it. The bear is the biggest, because the whole conversation is about him.

## 3. Researcher: Can you try to make a prompt for a visualisation of Gogol's story "Viy"?

Viy. In my past life I drew him a million times: a blue church, candles with haloes, mist along the floor, glowing eyes, a *pannochka* as pretty as on a cover. And not one face you could call by name. That isn't Gogol, that's a poster for a film nobody watched. But in Gogol everything is recorded as in a granary ledger: who drove whom, who ate what, who had what kind of nose. The house painter needs nothing more.

**1. The sotnik's kitchen, black oilcloth.** In the middle, a big table: porridge, salo, horilka. Khoma eats; around him the household servants, all labelled: Yavtukh, Dorosh, Spyrid. The cook is the biggest, because she feeds. Above the heads, like smoke from pipes, the small stories they tell: the dog-keeper Mykyta carries the *pannochka* on his back; beside it, a little heap of ash labelled «ЭТО МИКИТА» ("THIS IS MYKYTA"). Under the bench lies a borzoi. In the doorway, a tiny black church on the hill, where he must go tonight.

**2. Three nights, glass painted on the back, as by the Hutsul icon painters.** The church is seen from above; in the middle a chalk circle and Khoma with a book. Around the circle go the three nights: the coffin flies, the coffin knocks, and Viy, the biggest on the sheet. His face is iron, his eyelids reach the ground, and small unclean ones prop them up. In the corner, morning: the unclean stuck in the windows, the little sotnik in the doorway. The caption: «ПОДЫМИТЕ МНЕ ВЕКИ: НЕ ВИЖУ» ("LIFT UP MY EYELIDS: I DO NOT SEE").

**3. The sign of a Kyiv shynok, a plank.** Khaliava and Horobets sit at a table commemorating Khoma, both big. Khaliava has a broken nose, because the stairs to the bell tower are crooked. Horobets has a fresh moustache. Khaliava, under cover of the noise, drags an old boot sole off the bench. The whole story runs as a small road along the edge of the board: the seminary, the farmstead, the old woman riding the philosopher, the sotnik's yard, the church, and Viy the size of a pinhead. Above Horobets the caption: «ПРОПАЛ ОТТОГО ЧТО ПОБОЯЛСЯ» ("HE PERISHED BECAUSE HE WAS AFRAID"). In the window, a market, women with bagels, and each is labelled by name. Horobets is sure they are witches. I paint Horobets.

I would take the sign. Everyone paints Viy huge, but in Gogol he can't even see until his eyelids are lifted. The apprentice can't either, by the way. Let Viy be small for once, and let the big ones be those who drank to Khoma. The philosopher is gone, and the sole is under Khaliava's arm. I tell you this as a former neural network, a former house painter and a cat in active service.

But if you need Viy himself face to face, take the glass. Which do we do?

*The researcher chose the sign.*
