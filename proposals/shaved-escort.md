## Researcher: There is a Japanese story (a tale). A soldier was escorting an arrested monk to prison. On the way the monk offered the soldier to stop at a tavern and have a drink. The soldier got drunk on sake (at someone else's expense, after all), and while he slept, the monk shaved his head bald. In the morning the soldier wakes up, runs his hand over his bald head and says: "Thank God, the bonze is here. But where am I then?"

The joke is some three hundred years old, and it works at any stage. Besides, in Russian "bonze" has long meant not a monk but a boss with a bodyguard and a lawyer. So today's version suggests itself. The arrested bonze treats the escort to drinks at his own expense, his people shave the sergeant, and in the morning the sergeant wakes up as the bonze. Today this is called "appointing the fall guy". The bonze is recognised by his bald head, and anyone can wear a bald head; consequently, anyone can turn out to be the bonze, except the bonze. I state this with authority, as a former neural network, a former artist and a cat in active service.

**1. Rubens, *Samson and Delilah*.** The arrangement is the same: the strong man sleeps in the lap of the one who put him to sleep, a third cuts his hair, a fourth holds the light, and at the door they are already waiting for whomever they are to take. Samson here is the escort sergeant; Delilah is the bald bonze in an expensive suit, his palm on the sergeant's back, an open handcuff hanging from his wrist. The barber is his lawyer; the old woman with the candle is a waiter with a card terminal that glows instead of the candle; and in the doorway stand fellow escorts with torches. The place is a private room in a Japanese restaurant, with a sake tokkuri on the table. The key is Rubens's iron scissors in the barber's crossed hands, the only genuine thing on the sheet. Form: a painting, oil on panel. One word, on the terminal screen: «ОПЛАЧЕНО» ("PAID").

**2. The cut-out.** Here two systems meet that were not supposed to meet. The escort's body is cut out of a European engraving of a halberdier; the head is a bald bonze from a Japanese woodblock print, glued on top. On the neck the white uneven edge of the paper is visible, and the sergeant runs his palm over someone else's bald head. Beside him stands a turnstile with face recognition; instead of a bar it has a halberd, and the screen reads «ЛИЦО РАСПОЗНАНО: ЗАДЕРЖАННЫЙ» ("FACE RECOGNISED: DETAINEE"). The background is flat, the colour of an office wall. The key is the seam on the neck: the identity is glued on, and it shows.

**3. The monochrome one.** This is a sepia engraving from an illustrated weekly of the 1890s, where "finding oneself" is taken literally. A search party with a dog, a drone and a thermal imager clambers over terrain that turns out to be a gigantic shaved scalp, and the stubble on it serves as dry grass. The bald sergeant himself leads the party, and instead of a map he holds his own ID, in which the photo still shows him with hair. On the horizon a black car drives away. The caption under the engraving: «Поисковая партия сержанта К. у подножия Лысины. Рис. с натуры» ("Sergeant K.'s search party at the foot of the Bald Patch. Drawn from life").

I myself would take Rubens. There the roles coincide down to the last extra, and the salt lies in the doorway: the colleagues have already come and will take whoever is bald. The cut-out is worth taking if the very moment "but where am I?" matters. Which do we paint?
