## 1. Gemini, v7-ext

*The director gave its candidates and, since the researcher had said "choose yourself", went on to develop its own recommendation: an intent note, the lines for both pages, two prompts, two pages and a review. Translated here: the stock reading, the candidates, the intent note and the lines. The visual language, the prompts and the review are left out.*

### The stock reading and the mechanism

**The stock reading to get away from:** sweet Disney bears in a forest cottage bathed in warm light, where the cubs accidentally spill honey on the floor, grandpa slips with stars around his head, and the whole family laughs good-naturedly.

**The mechanism of the laugh:** a clash between adult everyday slyness (Grandpa Bear's attempts to dodge housework on the pretext of a "bad back" and secretly take a nap) and the childish literalness and engineering enthusiasm of the cubs **Toby** and **Pip**. The adults speak in everyday euphemisms and metaphors ("my back has seized up", "the old hinges have rusted", "time to clear the pipes"), and the children take the metaphor as a technical fault and "repair" grandpa with garage-mechanic methods in the spirit of *Tom and Jerry*, driving him to an absurd catastrophe. Dignity is not funny — absorption is: grandpa is absorbed in faking for the sake of a rest, the cubs in "rescuing" grandpa, and grandma in her imperturbable control of the list of chores.

### Candidates

**Candidate 1 — "Preventive maintenance" (domain: home repairs and garage mechanics)**
- **Reading and relation:** Grandpa fakes lumbago in the hammock so as not to clean the gutter on the roof and to hide a bottle of "root tonic", but the cubs Toby and Pip take literally Grandma's words that "grandpa has seized up and it is time to stretch the old joints", turning the yard into a medieval block and tackle.
- **Visible action and key detail:** the cubs hook the hook of a garden winch, with a wheelbarrow as counterweight, to the sleeping grandpa's waistcoat and grease the porch with axle grease, so that grandpa flies at full speed along the gutter (clearing it with his own nose), sticks upside down in the rain barrel, goes through a "spin cycle" down the slope and ends up swaddled in the garden tablecloth, while Grandma calmly crosses the jobs done off her list.
- **Visual tradition:** the Franco-Belgian Marcinelle school (1950s–1960s, kinetic slapstick in the spirit of Gaston Lagaffe's gadgets, without borrowing particular characters), combined with four-colour newspaper printing and a Ben-Day screen.
- **Form:** two pages of six panels (a 3×2 grid on each page).
- **What stays open:** whether the whole "engineering catastrophe" was subtly planned by Grandma, who in the very first panel handed the grandchildren a coil of rope.

**Candidate 2 — "An apex predator on a diet" (domain: the suburban lawn and wild nature)**
- **Reading and relation:** Grandma has put Grandpa on a strict berry diet, and he boasts to Toby and Pip that in his youth he was "an untamable wild grizzly", while trying to sneak to a stash of glazed doughnuts in the shed.
- **Visible action and key detail:** the cubs decide to "bring back grandpa's wild instincts" and set up training hunting traps in the yard (springing saplings and a rolling beehive), which go off exactly when grandpa, on tiptoe, reaches for the box of doughnuts.
- **Visual tradition:** the American Sunday newspaper page of the 1950s (flat block graphics in the spirit of UPA and classic newspaper strips).
- **Form:** two pages of six panels.
- **What stays open:** whether Grandma understands where the squashed doughnuts on the lawn came from when she praises grandpa for "an excellent cardio workout".

**Candidate 3 — "A spark in the house" (domain: family life and applied physics)**
- **Reading and relation:** Grandma sighs aloud that after forty years of marriage "the spark has quite gone out of our house" (meaning both romance and the dead water heater), and the cubs decide to electrify grandpa literally with static electricity.
- **Visible action and key detail:** while Grandpa, in woollen socks, is trying to glue a tiny ship in a bottle, Toby and Pip wax the floors and launch him on a woollen rug down a slope straight into a rose bush, so that he flies in to Grandma dishevelled, with a torn-out bunch of roses in his teeth.
- **Visual tradition:** Soviet magazine and book graphics of the 1960s (a lithographic line and everyday satire).
- **Form:** two pages of six panels.
- **What stays open:** whether the ship in the bottle survived the collision with the flowerbed.

**Recommendation: Candidate 1 ("Preventive maintenance").** It gives the cleanest chain reaction of physical slapstick in the spirit of *Tom and Jerry* (hammock → block and tackle with a wheelbarrow → greased porch → clearing the gutter on the roof → barrel → spin cycle → mummy in a tablecloth), and every line works on two levels: for children it is a jolly "repairing grandpa with a spanner", for adults an irony about male hypochondria, age and Grandma's imperturbable matriarchy.

### The intent note

- **Mode and form:** a standalone comic in which text and drawing work together (the text carries the double meaning about age and marriage, the drawing shows the physical mechanics of the trap); the form is a diptych of two pages of six panels (a 3×2 grid on each).
- **The thesis of the concept:** Grandpa Bear uses imaginary infirmity as a shield against housework, but the literal childish care of his grandchildren Toby and Pip makes him do all the heavy housework in the most painful acrobatic way.
- **The role of key objects:**
  1. **The wooden clipboard with the list of chores in Grandma's paws** — a binary clue for the adult reading: it appears in panel 1 of page 1 (when Grandma gives the cubs the rope) and in panel 6 of page 2 (when she crosses off clearing the gutter and washing the barrel).
  2. **The green bottle of "root tonic"** — only in panel 1 of page 1, under the hammock; it at once gives the adult viewer the true reason for grandpa's "lumbago".
- **What stays open:** how consciously Grandma "aimed" the grandchildren at grandpa.
- **What must not be there:** digital glow, gradients, human body proportions (especially a female waist on Grandma Bear — all the characters have massive barrel-shaped bear torsos and four-fingered paws with claws), and direct moralising conclusions at the end.

## 2. ChatGPT in Codex, the Codex revision of v7-ext

*The researcher's note: the director "made the image at once"; the conversation was not saved. What remains is the production record in its work notes, which opens with a summary of the concept, translated here.*

"Special Delivery", a complete story on two pages, six panels each. Pip and Poppy are playing at delivering pillows; a runaway cart picks up grandpa and carries him through the washing into a soft heap of leaves. The prank is unintentional: the children try to stop the cart, then apologise. Grandma meets the adventure with tea and a dry question about his pension; grandpa takes back the initiative and proposes being the driver himself next time. The strength of the scene is in the change of grandpa's role, from an unwilling passenger to a willing participant in the game. The four bears, their clothes, the cart and the English lines are kept. The key risk of execution: a readable causality of the cart's movement and a single design across the pages. The language: springy drawn plasticity, ink and matt gouache. No independent readers were used.
