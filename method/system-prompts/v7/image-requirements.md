# Requirements for the visual representation of a source

This document judges a finished image. How a prompt is written is in `CLAUDE.md`; how a job is run is in `pipeline.md`. Past examples behind these rules are in `cases.md`.

## The three requirements (the author's)

1. **Not slop.** The AI look must not jump out; ideally no one can tell a model made it.
2. **Substantive.** The image adds something the source does not already say.
3. **A stylistic basis.** A tradition — a publication, school, genre or period medium — named, or shown by a reference image. Not a technique.

**Slop**, working definition: what a generator produces when nothing pushes it elsewhere — the average style, reading, tone and shot — and, above all, **the absence of observation**. Test: is there at least one thing in the image that cannot be derived from the source text?

## Two kinds of rule

- **Block A is engineering.** Facts about how generators fail. They hold for any image, and breaking one is a defect.
- **Blocks B and C are a position of taste.** They describe what this author values. Each may be broken on purpose — say which rule and why. A rule broken knowingly is a choice; one broken unknowingly is a defect.

## A. Not slop (engineering)

**A1. Belonging to a medium.** The image is an object of a specific medium — a magazine page, a strip, a single-panel cartoon, a plate, a poster — with its material constraints: printing process, number of inks, screen, format, margins.
- *Symptom:* soft gradients, glow, cinematic light, texture on every surface.
- *Test:* where and how would this have been printed?
- *Belonging versus depicting.* A print is the medium. "A photograph of a sketchbook on a desk" only depicts a medium. That is admissible only when the frame itself carries meaning, and then A7 applies.

**A2. Hierarchy of finish.** Detail where the meaning is; emptiness or shorthand elsewhere. Checks at magnification:
- **how grey is made** — an identical mechanical mesh everywhere is a fill; hatching that follows the form is a hand;
- **the contour** — a line of constant thickness betrays vector work;
- **a light plan** — is there a decision about light and dark, or is the whole sheet one middle grey?
- **one scale for figures;**
- **separation by value** — does the protagonist stand out in lightness, or only in hue?

**A3. No default prettiness.** No automatic glossy eyes, fairy lights, flowers in vases or uniformly good-looking people. Prettiness is allowed when it performs the reading — see C2.
- *Test:* is there one element nobody would have chosen by default?

**A4. Specific faces.** Types are allowed, but each character carries one observed trait their type does not have.
- *Test:* describe each character by a trait their type lacks.

**A5. Text.** Every inscription is intended and correctly spelled. Written-on surfaces carry drawn strokes with no letter shapes, or nothing. No unplanned titles, labels, logos, parody logos, or instruction words rendered as text. "Illegible marks" is an order for invented symbols, and they count as pseudo-text.
- *Test:* is there one inscription nobody wrote on purpose?

**A6. Anatomy and consistency,** judged against the declared design. Hands, paws, coat, clothes and posture stay the same from panel to panel. Held and worn objects keep their count in every panel.
- *Test:* line up every appearance of the protagonist, and count every held object per panel.

**A7. No fake age, fake artefacts or decorative effects** — stains, "aged paper", sepia, sparkle, bokeh — unless they carry meaning. The texture of the medium (halftone, misregistration, a limited palette) is not decay. A depicted object (a sketchbook on a table) is a decorative effect when the frame carries no meaning; uniformity gives it away.

**A8. Decisions instead of compromises.** A hard crop, pure black, a limited palette, asymmetry, a close-up where it is needed.
- *Symptom:* everything at a middle distance, in the middle of the frame, in a middle tone.

**A9. An unambiguous reading order.** Panels and balloons read in the intended sequence. A right-to-left page needs a visible cue. n/a for a single image without dialogue.

**A10. Not borrowed.** A striking result that closely matches a narrow body of existing work — a recognisable living hand, stray seals, signatures or watermarks — is treated as borrowed, not as invention.

*Caveat.* Retouching by hand is a legitimate part of the process.

## B. Substance (taste — the core)

Decide the **mode** first. A **standalone** image replaces the text. An image **beside the text** adds a reading, context or a contradiction, and does not retell.

**B1. A reading, not a retelling.** A thesis that fits in one sentence and differs from the stock reading.
- *Test:* write both readings down. Are they different?

**B2. The mechanism works; the joke is not eaten.** The punchline or the effect works for the same reason as in the source. Satire that swallows the laugh fails B2 even if its point comes across.
- *A known source:* surprise is gone, so judge what the familiar line lands on.
- *Dignity is not funny; absorption is.* When a page is judged substantial and nobody smiles, look here first.

**B3. Image and text do not duplicate each other.** The picture shows what the words do not, or contradicts them.
- *Test:* cover the balloons — does the picture tell something of its own? Beside the text: what does the reader learn from the image that the text does not say?

**B4. A second reading.** One or two details that change the meaning when you look again. Each is binary, placed and sized, one per question. Never inconspicuous by instruction.

**B5. An open ending.** The question stays open; the moral is never spoken. Every gesture in the final panels belongs to the reading now in force, not to one that was dropped.
- *The click versus re-readability:* a device that hands over its meaning fires once; one that withholds it keeps working.
- *Overdetermined:* every detail says the same thing — several separate kinds of the same wrong, plus an inset that draws the outcome the story should leave open. The meaning arrives, and nothing is left to think about: a poster, not a cartoon. One or two binary clues make the reading available; the rest stays open.

**B6. The Menippean toolbox — which features are in play, and do they work?** The image is not required to use them all. Name the one to three it uses (see the table in `CLAUDE.md`) and judge only those:
- *fantastic tests an idea* — which idea?
- *scandal, mésalliance* — which norm is broken, and what does the breach expose?
- *several planes* — does the second world comment without words?
- *unusual state* — is it drawn as two things in one body, or only named?
- *inserted genre* — does the document carry the story, and does it state facts rather than explain?
- *several styles* — is the contrast categorical, and does it mean something?
- *topicality, real-world scripts* — at most four or five, carried by acts and lines, and not tipping into agitprop.

A feature used for its own sake — grotesque by default, scandal as decoration — counts against the image.

**Apparatus** (notes, legends, keys) is a legitimate form, and A7 does not apply to it. It states facts and stops. It never names the reader's clue. It can carry the joke by itself: what gets a number, and what does not.

## C. Stylistic basis (taste)

**C1. A tradition, named or shown.** A school, publication, genre or period medium — or a reference image that carries one without a name.

**C2. Chosen for the reading.** Its tone licenses the intended register.
- *The rendering argues on its own.* A generous, golden, flattering manner asserts that all is well. It can pull a whole series toward a reading nobody chose — or, used deliberately, carry the reading (a manner that renders everyone beautiful can itself be the mask). Ask what the manner says.
- *Test:* why this tradition? "It looks good" is a decorative answer.

**C3. Described in layers:** medium and format, printing and colour, line and distortion, composition and reading order, lettering, type of humour, character types, what it does not allow.

**C4. The layers agree.** Mixing is allowed as a stated device (two registers) or when the real setting is mixed. Then the reader must be able to recognise the reality through anchoring details.
- *Test:* strip away your knowledge of the prototype — what tells the reader where and when this is?

**C5. Passed to the generator categorically:** inks, palette, frames, colour versus monochrome. The best carrier is an accepted print of the same work used as a reference; a public-domain reference is next best; a name comes last.

**C6. Not a costume.** Drawing "like X" is not enough. Think the way the tradition thought: its humour, its attitude to its characters, its conventions.

## Evaluation

**Procedure**
1. **Provenance:** what was the image generated from (a prompt, a sketch, references, an earlier output), and what was taken from reality? Without this, realia gets mistaken for error.
2. **Final or intermediate?** Sketches, storyboards, character sheets and references are judged by function — what they passed on, and how much of it arrived. Blocks A and B do not apply to them.
3. **Mode:** standalone or beside the text.
4. **Blocking failures.** Any one of them fails the image:
   - pseudo-text or a typo in the punchline;
   - the protagonist's anatomy broken against the declared design;
   - in a standalone image, lines that come in the wrong order on a natural reading;
   - a real brand or logo;
   - a literal retelling in a standalone image;
   - no tradition at all, neither named nor shown.
5. **Each block: strong, medium or weak,** in one or two sentences, plus **the single change that would help most.** Do not add the blocks together: the most handmade image can be empty, and the most substantive one can be a fake artefact.
6. **Blind reading:** which meanings did fresh readers with no context — a new chat, another model, and when possible a person — find, and which details did they tie to each (questions in `pipeline.md` §2)? Record their maps next to the verdict. If the intended reading appears in no reader's list of meanings, B1 and B4 are not met, whatever the reviewer thinks. If every reader finds one meaning carried by every detail and nothing less obvious, B5 is not met.

**When comparing variants** of the same image, you may score each criterion 0/1/2 (failed / partly or by accident / met and working for the meaning) to see which variant wins on which criterion. The scores compare variants. They are not a grade of the work.

## Short checklist

- Provenance known? Final or intermediate? Standalone or beside the text?
- **A:** printed where and how? A light plan? Separation by value? How is the grey made? Unplanned text? Anatomy and per-panel counts? Reading order? Anything borrowed?
- **B:** thesis versus stock reading? Does it still make you laugh (or do what the source does)? What does the picture say that the words do not? What is seen only the second time — binary, one per question? Is the ending open? Which Menippean features are in play, and do they work?
- **C:** which tradition, named or shown, and why? What does the manner itself assert? Layers agree? Passed categorically? A costume?
- Blind reading: which meanings did fresh readers find, and which details carry each? Is the intended reading among them? Is anything left open?
