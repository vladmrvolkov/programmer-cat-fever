# Starting conditions and target criteria

These are the rules the experiment started with: what the human was allowed to do, and what counted as a successful image. The researcher wrote them in Russian for the framing article; the English translation is Claude's, and so is the note marked *(Claude)*. Later runs added many more requirements; the conditions below are the starting point.

## Starting conditions

1. **The task is not trivial to visualise:** a joke, an abstract concept, a literary image, an emotional state and the like.
2. **The starting point is a literary source, not a prompt that describes the picture in detail.** The human acts as the curator and writes no long prompts — a few sentences at most. All the work is to be done by AI. Several models may be used, and so may a long discussion with a model, after which the model writes the prompt itself. The concept of the picture and the artistic idea are the responsibility of the AI interlocutor model or of a virtual director (a model working under a system prompt).
3. **No theoretical preparation beforehand.** The thesis under test is that "anyone" can create any content with AI, and that this content need not be AI slop. The work is done from the point of view of someone who occasionally needs an illustration, not of someone who makes images all the time, with AI or without.
4. **The curator has no "right answer" in mind (or on disk) beforehand**, so as not to push the AI towards it. This requirement was intuitive at first; later cases showed that it was productive: a virtual producer kills the creativity of a virtual director. For the same reason the curator should not need the image for a practical purpose at the time — it is not a commission for an illustration to a particular publication. In practice, of course, a user will need a specific image; but the experiment set out to show the creativity of AI, not of the human.
5. **Reference images may be used for style and details, but not for the main idea of the solution**, except when the task itself is to reinterpret a source image.

## Target criteria for the resulting images

1. **The picture is not AI slop.** At the first stage AI slop was not defined: for the human it was a subjective impression, for the model its own criteria. Ideally it should be impossible to tell that the image was made by a model. That is not always possible, but at least "AI-generated" should not be glaring.
2. **The image is substantial:** a useful illustration or visual representation that adds something to the source text, that is, has value of its own.
3. **The picture is ready for publication** — if not 100%, then at least 90%; cleaning up hallucinations and the like is allowed. The experiment does not call for practical images, but two things have to be found out along the way: whether the image generator can carry out the director's idea, and whether the AI readers can read that idea from the picture.
4. **Optional: the image is made in one pass**, without the human stitching together or editing the generated files by hand. *(Claude)* This was not always met: several published images were retouched by hand, and the test pages say where (see [About the images](../README.md#about-the-images)).
5. **Optional: a stylistic basis is defined** (a graphic or visual language, a graphic tradition) — not "watercolour" or "pencil", but, for example, "the style of the magazine *Krokodil*", "classic manga" or "*The New Yorker* style".

How the reading tests were run, and what their numbers can and cannot mean, is described in the [protocols](protocols.md).
