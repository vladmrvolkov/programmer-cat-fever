**A. The screenwriter's axes (events, motives, personalities and world-building)**

**Macro-axis A-I. Ontology of the world and status of the miracle**
Brings together questions about the fundamental laws of the reality in which the scene occurs: choosing the world's rules automatically determines the heroine's own nature and the surrounding people's reaction.

**Axis A1. Normality of a talking animal**

1. **Name:** Status of the anomaly in the world.
2. **Question:** What is the ontological status of the talking cat under the laws of the depicted reality?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Establishes the nature of the waiter's shock—has he encountered a collapse of the laws of physics and biology, or a rare phenomenon that nevertheless fits his worldview?

**Axis A2. Genesis of the heroine's intelligence and profession**

1. **Name:** Origins of the programmer cat.
2. **Question:** What is the backstory of the cat's acquisition of speech, human intelligence and engineering qualifications?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Determines the heroine's background and why she considers her profession more fundamental than her biological species.

**Axis A3. Involvement of the social environment**

1. **Name:** Presence and reaction of third parties.
2. **Question:** How isolated is the scene from others, and how do the café's other customers and staff behave?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Decides whether the dialogue unfolds as an intimate, one-to-one incident or a public episode in the presence of witnesses.

**Macro-axis A-II. The characters' psychology, motives and professional profiles**
Connects the cat's and waiter's inner states into a single dramatic knot: the joke's conflict rests on the collision between a customer's tired pragmatism and a server's naïve enthusiasm.

**Axis A4. The cat's initial psychophysical state**

1. **Name:** Context of the café visit.
2. **Question:** In what emotional and working state does the cat cross the establishment's threshold, and what role does this break play for her?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Motivates her impatience in the second line (“are you bringing my order or not?”) and connects her need for coffee and sweets to her working rhythm.

**Axis A5. Nature of the dreamy pause**

1. **Name:** Degree of sincerity of the circus reverie.
2. **Question:** What is the psychological underpinning of the moment when the cat closes her eyes and lists the circus's attributes?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Determines the emotional swing before the ending—how large the gap is between enchantment with the festivities and a sober assessment of her profession.

**Axis A6. The cat's professional self-identification**

1. **Name:** Profile and self-perception in IT.
2. **Question:** What are the cat's specialisation, experience and attitude towards her craft as a programmer?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Sets the precise intonation of the final question—from weary puzzlement to professional pride or snobbery.

**Axis A7. The waiter's dominant motivation**

1. **Name:** Direction of the waiter's interest.
2. **Question:** What inner motive dominates the waiter's behaviour when he forgets the order and offers the cat work with his uncle?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Determines the second character's nature as a scene partner—from innocent admiration of a miracle to commercial calculation.

**Macro-axis A-III. Chronotope and boundaries of the event**
Brings together the coordinates of time, place and ending that create the dialogue's material and cultural setting.

**Axis A8. Historical and technological era**

1. **Name:** Time of the action.
2. **Question:** In what historical period, and at what stage of technological and entertainment-industry development, does the encounter occur?
3. **Significance for the joke:** The concepts of “programmer” and “circus” radically change their social standing, aesthetics and associated objects depending on the era.

**Axis A9. Social rank and atmosphere of the establishment**

1. **Name:** Character of the café.
2. **Question:** What are the social standing, interior arrangements and pace of life of the establishment the heroine enters?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Ordering “coffee with cream and a pastry” through a waiter establishes a particular leisure culture that contrasts both with the fairground circus and with the workplace.

**Axis A10. The scene's aftermath**

1. **Name:** Resolution of the situation after the punchline.
2. **Question:** How does the action develop in the seconds after the cat's final question?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Determines whether the story breaks off at the height of an awkward pause or receives an everyday conclusion with the order being served.

**B. The director-artist's axes (visual language, appearance and body language)**

**Macro-axis B-I. The heroine's morphology and appearance**
Brings together parameters of the cat's physical form, expressions and costume that regulate the visual balance between her animal nature and the image of an intellectual worker.

**Axis B1. Degree of anatomical anthropomorphism**

1. **Name:** The cat's bodily construction and movement.
2. **Question:** How humanlike is the cat anatomically and physically in body proportions, seated posture and gestures?
3. **Significance for the joke:** The waiter must instantly recognise an ordinary cat in the customer, while the viewer must believe in her ability to sit in a café and write code.

**Axis B2. Costume and material attributes**

1. **Name:** The heroine's wardrobe and props.
2. **Question:** To what extent is the cat's appearance supplemented by clothing, accessories and objects indicating her occupation?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Directly affects the character's legibility before the ending. *(The axis is placed in block B as part of character design, but overlaps with A6 and V6 because props can reveal the profession prematurely.)*

**Axis B3. Feline type**

1. **Name:** The cat's breed, build and texture.
2. **Question:** What outward species characteristics, age and physical build does the heroine have?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Provides visual characterisation, creating harmony or contrast between her appearance, fondness for pastries and analytical mind.

**Axis B4. Mechanics of articulation and facial expression**

1. **Name:** The movements of a speaking face and closing eyes.
2. **Question:** What graphic means handle the feline head's expressions when speaking and changing emotional states?
3. **Significance for the joke:** The plot's key turn rests on a subtle change of expression from irritation to dreamily closed eyes and final composure.

**Macro-axis B-II. Partner design and environmental ergonomics**
Connects the waiter's appearance and the physical parameters of the space surrounding the cat.

**Axis B5. The waiter's physical type and expressiveness**

1. **Name:** The waiter's distinctiveness and degree of grotesque exaggeration.
2. **Question:** What is the waiter's visual character, and how exaggerated is the body language of his astonishment (“mouth open”)?
3. **Significance for the joke:** The waiter serves as a visual tuning fork for normality and miracle; his reaction establishes the viewer's perception of the unusual nature of the events.

**Axis B6. Relative scale of character and environment**

1. **Name:** Proportion of furniture and tableware to the cat.
2. **Question:** How does the cat's size relate to the table, chair, coffee cup and saucer, and the waiter's figure?
3. **Significance for the joke:** The contrast in scale between a small animal and human furniture makes the scene of serious employment negotiations visually paradoxical.

**Macro-axis B-III. Graphic stylisation, colour and visualisation of the imaginary**
Determines the image's general formal laws and the means of showing inner reality.

**Axis B7. Degree of graphic stylisation**

1. **Name:** Plausibility versus stylisation.
2. **Question:** Where on the scale between observed plausibility and symbolic convention does the graphic manner lie?
3. **Significance for the joke:** The degree of realism changes the viewer's threshold of belief: in realistic graphics a talking cat causes shock; in stylised graphics she is perceived as a natural genre convention.

**Axis B8. Representation of the imagined circus**

1. **Name:** Visualisation of circus imagery.
2. **Question:** How are the acrobats, jugglers and sweets mentioned in the line represented (or not represented) in the visible space of the illustration?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Determines whether the cat's dream acquires an independent visual form, subsequently destroyed by the final phrase. *(Placed in block B as a choice of artistic device for conveying fantasy, but directly affects storyboarding in block V.)*

**Axis B9. Light-and-colour dramaturgy**

1. **Name:** Colour scheme and lighting scenario.
2. **Question:** How do palette and lighting distribute emphasis between the café space, characters and moment of dreamy pause?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Helps visually distinguish routine everyday life, the bright world of the imagined circus and the sobering ending.

**V. Comics and storyboarding technique (editing, optics and handling text)**

**Macro-axis V-I. Temporal structure and rhythmic editing**
Brings together questions of the story's duration, the division of action into phases and control of reading tempo and rhythm.

**Axis V1. Format of temporal unfolding**

1. **Name:** A single image or a sequence of panels.
2. **Question:** Is the joke's action condensed into a single simultaneous composition or distributed across a sequential series of panels?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Decides whether the viewer perceives the conflict all at once or experiences a gradual build-up and the frustration of expectations over time.

**Axis V2. Segmentation of action and pauses**

1. **Name:** Choosing pivotal phases and articulating silent scenes.
2. **Question:** Which precise plot phases and silent character reactions receive independent visual units?
3. **Significance for the joke:** This text's comic mechanism relies on two silent pauses—the waiter's stupefaction and the cat's closing eyes; the joke's timing depends on their emphasis.

**Axis V3. Page metre and modular-grid geometry**

1. **Name:** Rhythm, size and shape of panels.
2. **Question:** How do the area, proportions and arrangement of panels on the page or strip relate to one another?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Controlling panel size and rhythm allows time to slow during the dreamy line about the circus and to break off abruptly at the punchline.

**Macro-axis V-II. Optics, staging and framing**
Connects the virtual camera's position and its distance from the characters.

**Axis V4. Horizon height and point of view**

1. **Name:** Vertical angle and camera subjectivity.
2. **Question:** From whose eye level is the staging shown, and how does camera height change during the conversation?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Because of the height difference between the standing waiter and seated cat, the choice of horizon determines who visually dominates the dialogue.

**Axis V5. Dynamics of shot sizes**

1. **Name:** Shot scale and focus.
2. **Question:** What principle governs the alternation of wide, medium and close views of the characters throughout the scene?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Regulates the viewer's attention between the overall absurdity of the café situation and the subtle nuances of the cat's expression at the end.

**Macro-axis V-III. Synthesis of text and image (directing suspense)**
Brings together ways of integrating the verbal sequence into graphics and managing the moment when the main secret is revealed.

**Axis V6. Synchronisation of visual and verbal punchlines**

1. **Name:** Spoiler control and timing of the profession's revelation.
2. **Question:** How do visual clues to the heroine's profession relate in time to the text's final word?
3. **Significance for the joke:** The entire effect rests on the unexpected last word; the timing of visual clues determines whether the ending remains a surprise. *(The axis belongs in block V because it concerns directing the reader's attention across panels, although it is implemented through props from B2.)*

**Axis V7. Architecture of speech balloons and typography**

1. **Name:** Spatial segmentation of dialogue.
2. **Question:** How are speech balloons graphically organised, divided and positioned, especially within the cat's tonally uneven final line?
3. **Significance for the joke:** The cat's last statement contains an abrupt tonal break (from a sweet dream to a businesslike question); balloon arrangement controls the pause before the final sentence.

**Axis V8. Reduction of the narrator's text**

1. **Name:** Translating authorial directions into graphics.
2. **Question:** To what extent are the source's descriptive constructions (“stands stupefied,” “asks admiringly,” “closes her eyes dreamily”) delegated to pure drawing without words?
3. **Significance for the joke:** Determines where the work belongs between illustrated text and a self-contained comic.

**G. Visual languages and their narrative baggage**

1. **Classic single-panel magazine cartoon (the New Yorker canon):** Removes the question of an ontological miracle, turning the talking cat into a stylised social metaphor for a city dweller. Shifts the focus to class and professional snobbery, reducing the dramaturgy to an ironic caption beneath a restrained drawing.
2. **Syndicated newspaper comic strip (the tradition of Garfield and Peanuts):** Predetermines the cat's personality as a cynical, world-weary hedonist for whom coffee and pastry matter more than any miracle. Everyday absurdity is normalised here, and the final line sounds like an introvert's habitual grumbling.
3. **Soviet satirical and book graphics (the tradition of Krokodil magazine and illustrations to Bulgakov and the Strugatskys):** Firmly connects the talking cat to the archetype of Behemoth or the learned cat from NIIChAVO. Turns the heroine into a tired research fellow at a computing centre, and the waiter into a colourful bureaucrat or self-interested wheeler-dealer.
4. **Franco-Belgian anthropomorphic detective comics (the Blacksad canon) and realist BD:** Establishes high material credibility in the environment and anatomically convincing anthropomorphism. Demands serious psychological motivation for the characters and turns the scene into an atmospheric urban episode with a carefully specified period.
5. **American noir and neo-noir (high-contrast black-and-white graphics in the manner of Sin City):** Automatically decides the time of day, setting and heroine's state, placing the burned-out programmer cat in a gloomy night café after a difficult release. Her dreaminess reads as a bitter smirk, and the waiter's offer as a dubious deal.
6. **Contemporary minimalist webcomics about IT and burnout (corporate meme art / doodles):** Completely subordinates the story to current office routines, deadlines and calls. The miracle of a talking animal recedes, giving way to the viewer's solidarity with the developer's occupational conditioning.
7. **Japanese gag manga and everyday life (Slice of Life):** Introduces abrupt shifts of graphic register within one scene—from exaggerated cuteness with stars in the eyes when dreaming of lollipops to an instant switch to a stern realistic face at the punchline. Makes the cat's circus reverie intensely sincere and sensuous.
8. **Cyberpunk and science-fiction comics:** Immediately resolves the heroine's origins through bioengineering, neural implants or artificial intelligence. In such a world, the waiter's surprise moves from the mystical to a reaction to a rare or expensive technological modification.
9. **Magic realism and urban fantasy (individual book illustration):** Makes the cat a creature on the boundary of two worlds—a modern familiar who has exchanged witchcraft for coding. Through this lens, the circus represents old fairground magic that does not understand why it needs digital logic.
10. **Victorian engraving and steampunk (the canon of John Tenniel and Alice in Wonderland):** Shifts the chronotope to the nineteenth century, turning “programmer” into an elegant anachronism (referring to Babbage's Analytical Engine and Ada Lovelace). The conflict rests on the collision between Victorian etiquette and the fairground circus.
11. **The classic Disney / Pixar animation tradition:** Fills the scene with warm light, soft forms and charming expressions, removing harsh cynicism. Encourages a reading in which the waiter sincerely wants to help a talented little cat, and her circus dream becomes the story's emotional centre.
12. **Underground alternative comics (the tradition of Robert Crumb and adult grotesque):** Introduces nervous, visceral hatching and seedy urban life. Turns the café into a smoky dive, the waiter into an intrusive freak, and the cat into a life-battered misanthrope.
13. **Russian lubok and naive primitivism:** Removes psychological detail and everyday plausibility, combining image and the full text of the joke into one decorative sheet. Transposes the plot into the register of a timeless fable or urban parable about the mismatch between outward form and inner vocation.
14. **Corporate flat art (IT-startup vector illustration such as Alegria):** Ironically confines the whole plot within the IT industry's own visual language, with its sterile geometry and stylised proportions. Decides the conflict in the cat's favour in advance: even her circus dream looks like a diagram or presentation slide here.

**D. The reverse influence of form on the script (how axes from blocks B and V decide questions in block A)**

* **Axis B1 (Degree of anatomical anthropomorphism) decides Axes A1 (Status of the anomaly) and A2 (The heroine's origins):** The closer the cat's bodily structure is to a human's, the harder it is to explain the waiter's shock as a purely biological miracle; the choice of proportions automatically establishes the world's laws and the degree of animals' assimilation into human society.
* **Axis B2 (Costume and material attributes) decides Axes A4 (Initial state), A6 (IT profile) and A8 (Period):** Any item of clothing or working tool carried by the cat instantly fixes the historical period, her specialisation and whether she has come straight from her workplace.
* **Axis B6 (Relative scale of character and environment) decides Axes A1 (Status of the anomaly) and A9 (Character of the café):** Whether furniture is adapted to the cat's size directly answers the screenwriter's question of whether animals are routinely served in this world and establishment.
* **Axis B7 (Degree of graphic stylisation) decides Axis A1 (Status of the anomaly in the world):** A high degree of realism turns the scene into a mystical or science-fiction event, whereas a high degree of cartoon stylisation moves the plot into everyday satire that requires no justification of the miracle.
* **Axis B8 (Visualisation of the imagined circus) and Axis B9 (Light-and-colour dramaturgy) decide Axis A5 (Sincerity of the circus reverie):** How fully, brightly and visually convincingly the artist depicts (or ignores) the vision of the circus provides an unambiguous answer on the screenwriter's behalf as to whether the cat was genuinely enchanted by the thought or merely being ironic.
* **Axis V4 (Horizon height and point of view) decides Axis A7 (The waiter's motivation) and the balance of power in the dialogue:** A camera looking down or up visually predetermines who controls the situation and who is a supplicant or a curiosity.
* **Axis V6 (Synchronisation of visual and verbal punchlines) decides Axis A6 (The cat's self-identification):** If the cat's profession is visually announced from the first panel, the script changes from a story with a surprise ending into a comedy of situation, in which the viewer shares the heroine's ironic superiority over the unsuspecting waiter from the outset.
