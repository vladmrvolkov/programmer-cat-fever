The three concepts diverge on the main points: whose gaze the image adopts, when the viewer learns the profession, and what the final line means. The axes in the map have no codes, so their names serve as identifiers.

| | Whose gaze the picture adopts | When the viewer learns the profession | Meaning of the final line | Form |

## 1. “In Small Print”

**1. Main idea.** The whole picture is an old circus poster already selling a talking cat to the public. In his excitement, the waiter has turned a customer into an act in a second, while the cat is still waiting for coffee. Her line is the only thing that does not obey the poster, and it appears where the terms are printed in small type.

Before the line, the viewer sees an ordinary poster and becomes its audience. Afterwards, the sheet is reread: the pedestal turns out to be a table, the barker a waiter with an empty tray, the performer a customer who never agreed to an act.

**2–3. Key axes, decisions and their visibility**

| Axis | Decision | How it becomes visible |
|---|---|---|
| **Whose world dictates the style** | The circus, hence the waiter's gaze; the author argues with it | The café is drawn as a poster: border, ribbon, lettering |
| **Form of visual storytelling**, **The reader's path of attention** | One image; type size determines reading order | Headline → cat in a circle of light → waiter's gesture → vignettes → small line at lower right |
| **Light-and-shadow dramaturgy** | Light puts the cat on display; she does not accept it | The pendant lamp's cone reads as a spotlight; the cat looks at the waiter, not into the light |
| **The truth of the profession** | A statement only | No work-related object: the poster shows only what the waiter sees |
| **The intention of the final line** | Open | A calm face, one eye still narrowed; neither a smirk nor confusion |
| **The appearance of lettering and text** | The cat's speech uses rules alien to the sheet | A pure white caption box without paper grain, a thin border, monospaced type |
| **The period evoked by the manner** | A manner from the turn of the twentieth century, present-day action | A menu QR-code sign on the table, worked into the ornament |

**Coordination**
- **Accepted canon:** the poster's construction — arched headline, a figure in a circle of light, corner vignettes, a programme ribbon, four-colour printing.
- **Association:** a poster calls people in, so the person looking is the audience.
- **My interpretation:** the waiter is an enthusiastic nephew rather than a businessman; the uncle and circus exist only as ornament.
- **My additions:**
  - the waiter's words become the headline, and the poster has already removed the question mark;
  - “on any terms” becomes an advertising line, the cat's list a programme ribbon;
  - the QR sign on the table;
  - grey rather than black colouring, to avoid quoting *Le Chat Noir* and Behemoth.
- **Open:** whether she is a programmer; whether she understood the offer; what the uncle is like.

**Scene.** A café, a second after the waiter's words. The cat sits on a cushioned bentwood chair, front paws on the edge of a round table, head turned towards the waiter. Only a menu, napkin holder and sign are on the table: the order has not arrived.

**Characters.** The cat is an ordinary grey tabby, without clothes or human anatomy, with a customer's patience on her face. The young waiter, in a long apron, stands at lower left, where posters place the ringmaster. One arm is flung towards the cat, palm up; the other presses an empty tray to his chest.

**Composition.** A vertical 2:3 sheet. The window drapes act as a curtain, the tabletop as a pedestal. Corner vignettes: acrobats, a juggler, lollipops, candy floss.

**Manner, colour, light.** Chromolithography: black outlines, vermilion, ochre, blue-green, cream paper with grain and slight misregistration. The speech box is cool and smooth.

**Text**
- Arched at the top: “INCREDIBLE!”
- Below, in the largest type: “A TALKING CAT”
- A small line beside the border: “on any terms”
- Ribbon beneath the table: “acrobats · jugglers · lollipops · candy floss”
- Box at lower right, with a tail towards the cat: “Sounds tempting. But what would they need a programmer for?”

## 2. “In Plain Sight”

**1. Main idea.** From the first panel, the reader sees the cat working at a laptop. Only the waiter fails to see it, because he is looking at her ears. The cat talks with half her attention, without stopping work, while he speaks to a miracle rather than a customer. The final line is a sincerely asked clarification about the vacancy.

The concept is intended for people who know the joke: surprise is replaced by anticipation of the waiter lowering his eyes. After the line, only his knowledge changes.

**2–3. Key axes, decisions and their visibility**

| Axis | Decision | How it becomes visible |
|---|---|---|
| **Revealing the profession** | Immediately to the reader, in the last panel to the waiter | A screen with code is visible over the cat's shoulder; the waiter's gaze always passes above it |
| **The truth of the profession** | A fact of the world | A process, not an accessory, is shown: she types, and the test-run bar grows from panel to panel |
| **The distribution of knowledge within the scene** | The reader's knowledge is not passed to the character | In panel 4 the waiter's palm lies on her notebook with a diagram, while he looks over it |
| **Pictorial canon** | Ligne claire: uniform contours, flat colour and no shadows are adopted | The laptop is drawn as clearly as the tail; nothing is hidden by lighting |
| **The intention of the final line** | A sincere practical question | Ears forward, paw pointing at the screen, a direct gaze |
| **Point of exit** | On the line itself, without a panel afterwards | In the final panel the waiter lowers his eyes for the first time, phone already at his ear |
| **The environment's fit to the cat** | The café is not adapted; the cat has adapted herself | She sits on her own backpack placed on the chair |

**Coordination**
- **Accepted canon:** ligne-claire graphic rules and a calm panel grid. I do not transfer the adventure genre or the period of the classic albums.
- **Association:** a clear material environment reads as “nothing is concealed”.
- **My interpretation:** the waiter is a student who wants to please his uncle and is already dialling; the cat genuinely wants to go to the circus.
- **My additions:**
  - the test bar as the scene's clock;
  - the waiter's hand on the notebook;
  - a phone calling “Uncle”;
  - an order line constructed from the narrator's words.
- **Open:** whether the circus needs a programmer (perhaps it does); what the waiter will say into the phone; where talking cats come from.

**Scene and characters.** A daytime café, a table by the window. A tricolour cat of ordinary build and size, unclothed; stickers on her laptop lid. The waiter is a lanky young man of about twenty, in a white shirt and dark apron, carrying a notepad.

**Panels.** One page, three tiers of two panels.
1. **Wide shot.** The cat types without looking up; the waiter approaches, looking at his notepad. Cat: “Coffee with cream and a pastry, please.”
2. **Over the cat's shoulder.** In front, a screen with code; the test bar is one-third full. Behind it, the frozen waiter, a pencil dropped from his fingers. “You're a cat? A talking cat?! Incredible…”
3. **Medium shot of the cat.** A paw on the keyboard, one eye turned towards the waiter. “Yes, I'm a cat. Are you bringing my order or not?”
4. **The waiter at the table.** He leans in, palm on the notebook with a diagram, the stickered lid right in front of him. “Hold on a second… Listen, could you use a job? It's just, my uncle runs a circus, and he'd gladly take you on, on any terms…”
5. **Dream.** For the first time, the cat takes her paws off the keyboard and closes her eyes. Above her, a cloud contains four pictograms: a trapeze, clubs, a lollipop, candy floss. “A circus, you say? That's the place with the acrobats, the jugglers, the lollipops, the candy floss? Sounds tempting.” The waiter beams and dials; his phone screen says “Uncle”.
6. **The widest panel.** The cat's paw points to the screen; the bar has reached the end and turned green. “But what would they need a programmer for?” Phone at his ear, the waiter looks down for the first time. There is no coffee on the table.

**Manner, colour, light.** Even outlines, local colours without gradients or cast shadows, uniform daylight, a detailed and accurate interior.

**Text.** White balloons, neat handwritten capitals. The final line is lettered like all the others, without emphasis.

## 3. “The Fifth Row”

**1. Main idea.** Acrobats, jugglers, lollipops and candy floss are the circus seen from the audience. In her dream the cat sits in the fifth row with candy floss, while in the waiter's offer she stands in the ring. The story shows the second when she notices this, and how gently she steps out of the spotlight: she offers the waiter another word for herself.

Before the line, the viewer has already seen where she places herself, so the ending works through tone rather than surprise. Afterwards the waiter becomes a waiter again and brings her order.

**2–3. Key axes, decisions and their visibility**

| Axis | Decision | How it becomes visible |
|---|---|---|
| **The cat's conception of the circus** | A spectator's circus, not a performer's | In the dream she is in the audience, and the seat fits her height, unlike the café table |
| **Awareness of being exceptional**, **The intention of the final line** | She understands; the line is a tactful refusal | Inside the dream, the beam leaves the ring and finds her in her row; neighbours turn, she opens an eye |
| **Marking subjective images** | Change of medium | Café: transparent watercolour and pencil; dream: opaque gouache |
| **The medium's format** | A vertical scrolling strip | The dream is one long panel from dome to auditorium; the line cannot be seen until you scroll down |
| **The figure of the uncle** | Only the uncle as imagined by the cat appears | A white glove in the corner points at her |
| **The waiter's motive** | Goodwill | He crouches to speak at her eye level |
| **The post-punchline pause**, **The fate of the order** | A silent panel after the line; the order has arrived | Coffee, pastry, and a lollipop beside the saucer |

**Coordination**
- **Accepted rule:** only the strip format — one panel per screen and a long dream panel. The format does not prescribe the psychological reading; that is my choice.
- **Associations:** watercolour conventionally reads as quiet observation, gouache as festive decoration. Plot and light nevertheless set the tone.
- **My interpretation:** the cat understands what she was offered; the waiter is kind.
- **My additions:**
  - the cat's audience seat in her own dream;
  - the beam that finds her, and the glove;
  - a jar of lollipops beside the till;
  - a continuation after the line.
- **Open:** whether the waiter understood or simply offered a treat; whether she really is a programmer (the only hint is a flat bag beside the chair leg); whether she will go to the circus as a spectator.

**Scene and characters.** A small, almost empty café-patisserie towards evening. A glass jar of lollipops stands beside the till, visible from the first panel. An orange cat of ordinary build; paws on the table edge, chin just above the tabletop. The waiter is about thirty, soft and rounded, wearing a knitted waistcoat beneath his apron.

**Panels.** Read from top to bottom.
1. **Table beside the shop window.** The waiter has frozen with his notepad. “You're a cat? A talking cat?! Incredible…” — “Yes, I'm a cat. Are you bringing my order or not?”
2. **At eye level.** He crouches beside the table. “Hold on a second… Listen, could you use a job? It's just, my uncle runs a circus, and he'd gladly take you on, on any terms…”
3. **Close-up of her face.** Her eyes narrow. “A circus, you say?” The lamp's reflection in the window becomes a string of lights; the watercolour thickens.
4. **Dream, a three-screen panel.** At the top, dome and acrobats; below, jugglers in the ring's circle; further down, the warm darkness of the rows. A small orange figure with candy floss larger than her head sits on the aisle in the fifth row. Words stand beside what they name: “That's the place with the acrobats,” / “the jugglers,” / “the lollipops,” / “the candy floss?” Beside her happy face: “Sounds tempting.”
5. **Dream, no words.** The beam slides off the ring and stops on her seat. The candy floss glows; her neighbours' heads turn towards her; a white glove points from the upper corner. Her ears flatten slightly.
6. **The café again.** Pale and quiet, one eye open. “But what would they need a programmer for?” The waiter is still crouching, his smile unfinished.
7. **The table from above, no words.** Coffee with cream, a pastry, a lollipop beside the saucer; the waiter's hand leaves the frame.

**Manner, colour, light.** The café is painted in grey-ochre watercolour; light is unpainted paper, orange fur the only saturated patch. The dream uses red, gold and ultramarine; the beam is pure white paint. In the final panel the lollipop is the only object with gouache colour in the watercolour world.

**Text.** In the café: balloons with handwritten lettering. In the dream, words lie directly on the gouache without balloons.

I can expand any of the three into a panel-by-panel artist's brief or an image-generation prompt.
