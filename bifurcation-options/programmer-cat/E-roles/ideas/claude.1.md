## 1. “A Poster with an Objection”

**Idea.** The entire sheet is an old circus poster already selling the talking cat as an attraction. The final line, set in small, everyday type, objects both to the waiter and to the poster itself.

**Design**
- **Characters and ending.** The waiter is sincerely delighted and instantly turns his customer into a programme item; the poster's grandiosity belongs to him. The circus appeals to the cat as a spectator, while she understands the job offer as a vacancy in her field. The ending sounds like practical bewilderment. Three questions remain open: whether she really is a programmer, whether her question is naïve or ironic, and whether the uncle and circus exist.
- **Visual language.** A vintage circus poster in chromolithography: the star of the show in a cone of spotlight, ribbon cartouches, elaborate shadowed lettering, three or four inks, stone grain, worn folds. These signs read as “you are being invited to a spectacle”, and the viewer initially accepts the poster's rules.
- **Format and viewer knowledge.** One image. Letter size determines reading order: headline, cat, vignettes, and finally a small balloon. Before the line, the viewer knows only “wonder-cat, circus”. Afterwards, they reread the sheet as someone else's confident assumption, which they themselves had already begun to believe.
- **Coordination.** Accepted constraint: a single sheet and poster conventions (frontality, typographic hierarchy, economical printing). Association: a poster sells an attraction, and the manner evokes the turn of the twentieth century, although the action is contemporary. Free interpretation: the sheet's grandiosity belongs to the waiter, and the cat dreams of a seat in the audience.
- **My additions.** The clash of two typefaces as the vehicle of the punchline, the line about the uncle in the poster's imprint, the jabot the cat pulls away with her paw, and the attached kitchen receipt.

**Concept**
- **Scene.** A poster's circus ring replaces the café, in the first second of the final line. The cat sits on a circus pedestal; the waiter-barker points at her.
- **Characters.** An orange tabby of ordinary feline build sits upright. Around her neck is a starched jabot, which she pulls away from her throat with one paw. Her left eye is dreamily closed, her right eye open, her mouth slightly open. The waiter, in a long apron and bow tie, holds a tray under his arm; his free arm is extended towards the cat in a ringmaster's gesture, his mouth open in delight.
- **Composition.** A vertical 2:3 sheet. At the centre, the cat sits on a star-covered pedestal in a yellow cone of light; the waiter's small figure is at lower left. Four round medallions at the sides contain acrobats, jugglers, lollipops and candy floss. A narrow white receipt is attached with a paper clip at lower right.
- **Manner, colour, light.** Flat lithographic layers with slight misregistration: vermilion, ochre, blue-black, cream paper. The balloon and receipt are cold white and grainless, as if pasted in from another world.
- **Text.** The dialogue is shortened: the waiter's words become headlines, the cat's list becomes labels, and only the ending is retained verbatim.
  - An arc at the top: “INCREDIBLE!”
  - The main headline in two lines: “A TALKING CAT”
  - Medallion labels: “Acrobats”, “Jugglers”, “Lollipops”, “Candy floss”
  - A ribbon below the pedestal: “ON ANY TERMS!”
  - A small line at the bottom: “The circus director is my uncle”
  - A balloon from the cat's muzzle towards lower right: “Sounds tempting. But what would they need a programmer for?”
  - Receipt: “Table 4. Coffee with cream — 1. Pastry — 1”

## 2. “The Other Side of the Lid”

**Idea.** From the first panel the reader sits over the cat's shoulder and sees the screen with code. The waiter always stands on the lid side of the laptop and sees only the cat. The ending does not surprise but reverses the camera: for the first time we look through the waiter's eyes and discover that everything was right under his nose.

**Design**
- **Characters and ending.** The cat has come to work and eat, and the waiter's amazement gets in her way. He is enterprising: he wants to be the person who found his uncle a star, with no malicious intent. The final line sounds like a sincere professional question from someone already considering the task. Her profession is established here. Whether the uncle exists, what “any terms” amounts to, and where a talking cat came from remain open.
- **Visual language.** Franco-Belgian ligne claire: uniform contours, flat fills without hatching or shadows, equally precise objects. Cat, humans and coffee machine are drawn with the same line, so the cat looks like any other customer; only the waiter's face marks the miracle. The precision of the objects makes the screen and stickers legible.
- **Format and viewer knowledge.** One page, six panels in three rows. The viewer knows more than the waiter and waits for him to notice. After the line, the only reverse shot shows the waiter's view: a sticker-covered lid and a blank notepad.
- **Coordination.** Accepted constraint: ligne-claire conventions and keeping the camera on the same side until the last panel. I do not adopt this school's adventure genre or period; the action takes place today. Association: an orderly, credible world. The storyboard, not the style, determines who is mistaken. Free interpretation: a café where everyone has a laptop, and the waiter's motive.
- **My additions.** The laptop lid as a wall between two viewpoints, a single camera reversal, customers in headphones, a screen that goes dark during the daydream, a blank notepad. The order is put into the cat's mouth; in the joke it is narrated.

**Concept**
- **Scene.** A bright daytime coffee shop, a laptop on every table. The cat's table is beside the aisle, the laptop between her and the waiter.
- **Characters.** A black cat with a white bib and white paws, ordinary build, human behaviour: she sits upright and types with both paws. The furniture is ordinary, so she has placed her own backpack under herself. The waiter is about twenty-five, lanky, wearing an apron, holding a notepad and pencil.
- **Manner, colour, light.** Even, shadowless light, warm wood, mint tiles, white cups. The only saturated patch is the stickers on the lid, which the reader sees only in the last panel.
- **Panels and text.** Balloons occupy the top of the panels, with hand-lettered capitals. The final line uses the same size, without emphasis.
  1. Wide shot over the cat's shoulder: a screen with code, the room, the waiter approaching while looking at his notepad. The cat, without looking away from the screen: “Coffee with cream and a pastry.”
  2. The waiter looks up, mouth open, pencil falling. The cat types. “You're a cat? A talking cat?! Incredible…”
  3. The cat turns her ears and one eye towards him, paws still on the keyboard. “Yes, I'm a cat. Are you bringing my order or not?”
  4. The waiter glances around the room and leans in, placing his palm on the edge of the lid. “Hold on a second… Listen, could you use a job? It's just, my uncle runs a circus, and he'd gladly take you on, on any terms…”
  5. The cat leans back, paws off the keyboard, eyes closed; the screen goes dark. “A circus, you say? That's the place with the acrobats, the jugglers, the lollipops, the candy floss? Sounds tempting.”
  6. Reverse shot. At the bottom, the waiter's hand and notepad, its page blank. At the centre, the lid with stickers: “</>”, “404”, “sudo”, “it works on my machine”. Above it, the cat's ears and serious round eyes. “But what would they need a programmer for?”

## 3. “View from the Ring”

**Idea.** The cat's dreamy pause unfolds into a long panel of a circus seen from the middle of the ring rather than the audience. After it, the final line sounds like a quiet refusal: I have been there; now I am a programmer.

**Design**
- **Characters and ending.** The cat once performed, left, and trained in another profession. She has heard exclamations about a talking cat hundreds of times and answers evenly and wearily. The waiter is friendly and his offer heartfelt. “Sounds tempting” expresses unfeigned nostalgia, while the question about a programmer gently names who she now considers herself to be. Open questions: whether this is a memory or fantasy, whether this is the uncle's circus, why she left, and whether the waiter understood her.
- **Visual language.** Vertical scrolling, panels of different heights and empty intervals are taken from Korean webtoons. The manner is painterly, with gouache texture. The café lighting evokes Hopper: a large window, a dark street, an island of warm lamplight, plenty of space between figures. All this slows the reading and maintains distance.
- **Format and viewer knowledge.** Eleven panels. Nothing is known about her profession before the ending; the laptop is in a closed bag. After the long panel, the viewer suspects that the circus is familiar to her. After the line, they hear “Yes, I'm a cat…” and the list anew: these are things visible from the ring. The last silent panel shows her order delivered.
- **Coordination.** Accepted constraint: a vertical strip, with one panel fitting on the screen, so the punchline is physically hidden further down. Associations: webtoons are associated with psychological drama, Hopper's light with solitude. I take the slowing-down and distance without claiming that the cat is lonely. Her circus past is entirely my invention: it follows from neither format nor manner and rests on three details.
- **My additions.** The outward view from the ring, a cat's shadow on the sawdust, a faded gold star (a charm on her bag and a design on the pedestal), the crouching waiter, an empty interval before the punchline, and a silent ending.

**Concept**
- **Scene.** A rainy evening, an almost empty café, a table beside a large window.
- **Characters.** A lean, ageing smoke-grey cat with grey hairs on her muzzle. Ordinary build, very upright posture, tail neatly wrapped around her paws. A canvas bag with a star charm hangs from her shoulder. The waiter is young, stooped towards the end of his shift, sleeves rolled up, dark apron, open expression.
- **Manner, colour, light.** The café is painted in cool blue-green twilight, with one warm lamp above the table. The circus is painted in hot gold and vermilion, with a black auditorium and overexposed spotlights. At the end, the warm circle of the lamp meets the cold reflection of the screen on the cat's muzzle.
- **Panels and text.** In the café, balloons are white and tailed. In the vision, text lies directly on the image without outlines.
  1. A narrow, tall panel: the shopfront from outside, the small silhouette of the cat with her bag in the doorway.
  2. The cat on a chair by the window, bag on the table, waiter with notepad. “Coffee with cream and a pastry.”
  3. Close-up of the waiter's face, his notepad lowering. “You're a cat? A talking cat?! Incredible…”
  4. The cat looks out of the window rather than at him. “Yes, I'm a cat. Are you bringing my order or not?”
  5. The waiter crouches beside the table. “Hold on a second… Listen, could you use a job? It's just, my uncle runs a circus, and he'd gladly take you on, on any terms…”
  6. Close-up: the cat's eyes close. “A circus, you say?”
  7. A long panel. At the top, the tent dome and trapeze acrobats against blinding spotlights. Further down, a juggler's clubs hang motionless near the edge. Below them, dark rows where lollipops and pink clouds of candy floss glow in children's hands. At the bottom, sawdust, the edge of a pedestal with a peeling gold star, and a short cat's shadow. Three fragments of text along the scroll: “That's the place with the acrobats, the jugglers,” / “the lollipops,” / “the candy floss?”
  8. The golden sawdust becomes the tabletop; the cat's eyes are closed and she has a barely perceptible smile. “Sounds tempting.”
  9. An empty dark-blue interval one screen high, with only raindrops on the glass.
  10. The cat opens her eyes and looks straight at the waiter. Her paw rests on the bag's flap, with the corner of a laptop visible beneath it. “But what would they need a programmer for?”
  11. No text: coffee with cream and a pastry on the table, steam, an open laptop beside them. The waiter is reflected in the window, still looking at her.
