**Variant 1. “An Attraction in Someone Else's Dream” (Single poster illustration)**

**Developing the overall concept**

In this reading, the waiter sees the talking cat solely as a curiosity and a quick way to win favour with his impresario uncle, while the cat sees herself as a qualified specialist on a break. The final line sounds like calm professional bewilderment, without mockery: the cat was briefly carried away by the image of fairground sweets, but immediately returned to business logic. Where her gift of speech comes from, whether other intelligent animals exist in this world, and whether the waiter's uncle really runs a circus or is a product of his boasting are deliberately left open.

The chosen visual language combines a **vintage circus poster** and decorative **Art Nouveau** graphics with material signs of early computing (**steampunk / the Victorian era**). The poster composition, with shaped frames, spotlight beams and decorative ribbons, already tries through its form to “sell” the cat to the viewer as a circus attraction, visualising the waiter's gaze. **My addition:** the period is specified as the late nineteenth century, and programming is conveyed through a physical accessory — a stack of cardboard punched cards for Babbage's Analytical Engine, which the waiter mistakes for a card magician's props.

A **single illustration** is chosen with a tightly organised vertical reading path from top to bottom. Before reading the last line at the bottom, the viewer sees a triumphant circus presentation and reads the cat through the waiter's eyes — as a future star of the ring dreaming of lollipops. After reading the lower ribbon with the final question, the viewer's gaze falls on a detail at the very bottom — punched cards with binary code and a drawing of gears on the table. This completely reverses the poster's meaning: the poster form proves a false frame at odds with the heroine's real identity.

Coordination rests on distinguishing three connections. The adopted canon's constraint is the unified poster sheet: development in time cannot be shown except through spatial tiers and text ribbons. A conventional association is the expectation of an old-fashioned fairground wonder created by Art Nouveau forms and typographic banners. Free authorial interpretations are the era of mechanical tabulators, the waiter's calculating motive and the cat's complete seriousness — these properties do not automatically follow from Art Nouveau, but are set by a narrative decision.

**Brief presentation of the concept**

1. **Title and main idea:** “An Attraction in Someone Else's Dream” — a conflict between outward packaging (the cat as a fairground marvel on the waiter's imaginary poster) and the heroine's substance (a computing engineer over morning coffee).

2. **Key axes chosen and decisions:**

* **Meaning and the world's structure / The main subject of comedy:** A clash between an outward assessment by biological species and a deep professional self-definition.

* **The cat as a character / The truth of the profession:** An established fact of the world, confirmed by work objects on the table.

* **Tradition, canon and ownership of style / Whose world dictates the style:** The circus sets a false decorative frame, destroyed by a detail from the programming world.

* **Staging, comics and reading / The reader's path of attention:** Top to bottom — from circus dome and waiter to the cat and the final revelation of the table's objects.

* **Colour, light, text and movement / Light-and-shadow dramaturgy:** Footlights turn an ordinary café table into a stage before the cat has even agreed.

3. **Visible embodiment of the decisions:**

* The circus illusion takes shape in an upper arch of heavy burgundy curtains, where coffee steam swirls into a tent silhouette with acrobats and lollipops.

* The waiter's assessment is visible in his compère's pose, pointing gesture, and a golden beam striking the cat directly.

* The true profession is revealed in the sheet's lower third through a brass mechanical calculator and a fan of punched cards labelled `LOOP_INIT` on the marble table's edge.

**Description of the concept**

**Scene and action:** Inside a late-nineteenth-century Viennese coffee house, the waiter freezes beside a marble table, arms theatrically spread as though presenting a unique act to the audience, while the cat sits on a bentwood chair with closed eyes, inhaling the aroma of the coffee and pastry she has been served.

**The characters' appearance, poses and expressions:** The cat has ordinary feline anatomy, fluffy smoke-coloured fur, but sits with a perfectly straight back; tiny corded pince-nez sit low on her nose, her face expressing blissful thoughtfulness. The waiter is a lanky man with pomaded moustaches, in a waistcoat and long apron; his mouth is slightly open in delight, his torso leaning towards the table with predatory obsequiousness.

**Composition and details:** A vertical, symmetrical poster composition. In the upper tier, steam from the coffee pot forms a decorative medallion with silhouettes of jugglers, aerialists and spiral lollipops. At the centre are the cat and the waiter leaning in from the side; at the very bottom, on the table, a cup with cream, an éclair, a tattered notebook of formulas and a pack of punched cards (**my addition**).

**Visual manner, colour and light:** Lithographic poster graphics in the manner of Alphonse Mucha and Toulouse-Lautrec, with flowing dark contours and local patches of colour. The palette uses warm ochre, burgundy velvet and muted turquoise; warm golden light from below illuminates the cat, imitating theatrical footlights.

**Exact dialogue and placement:** Text is inscribed in decorative ribbon banderoles winding through the composition from top to bottom, in ornate poster lettering.

* Upper ribbon (above the waiter): “You're a cat? A talking cat?! My uncle runs a circus; he'd take you on, on any terms!..”

* Middle ribbon (around the coffee steam): “A circus, you say? That's the place with the acrobats, the jugglers, the lollipops, the candy floss? Sounds tempting.”

* Lower, strict rectangular box (directly above the punched cards, set in dry technical sans-serif lettering): “But what would they need a programmer for?”

**Variant 2. “Candy Floss and Harsh Production” (Four-panel manga — yonkoma)**

**Developing the overall concept**

The centre here is a tired developer's inner conflict between the childish temptation to abandon everything for simple pleasure and ingrained professional rationality. The waiter is a sincere, slightly naïve young man, genuinely amazed by the miracle and wholeheartedly offering what he considers an excellent vacancy. The final line is the cat's moment of sober awakening: she seriously dreamed of candy floss for a second, but reached a dead end when trying to apply her actual skills to the circus. Whether the uncle's circus really does not need automated ticketing, and whether the cat would accept if the waiter explained the business problem, are deliberately left open.

The visual language uses **gag manga** in a **four-panel yonkoma** format, with abrupt shifts between three registers: **slice-of-life seinen manga** (panels 1–2), exaggerated **chibi and kawaii codes with shōjo elements** (panel 3), and hard, realistic **gekiga** (panel 4). Changing graphic conventions makes visible the temperature difference between everyday life, a sincere childhood dream and stern professional sobriety. **My addition:** the last panel introduces “screen glow” — cold light from a smartphone notification about a crashed server falls on the cat's face, motivating her instant return from dreams to her profession.

A vertical sequence of **four panels** is chosen. In the first three, the viewer sees no hints of IT: the cat looks first like a fussy city dweller, then an adorable sweet tooth, and the viewer expects a joke about feline habits. In panel four, together with the final line, the cat's drawing changes abruptly and a phone screen with error logs enters the image, instantly redefining all her previous behaviour as a break between tasks.

Coordination accounts for the constraint of the classic yonkoma canon: the structure is strictly divided into introduction (*ki*), development (*shō*), turn (*ten*) and conclusion (*ketsu*) in exactly four equal blocks with no afterword. A conventional association is the link between large shiny eyes and background flowers and a character's naïveté in panel three. The free authorial interpretation is that this cute fantasy conceals a stern senior developer, while the waiter acts entirely selflessly — the manga format itself does not prescribe these motives.

**Brief presentation of the concept**

1. **Title and main idea:** “Candy Floss and Harsh Production” — the cat's sincere impulse towards festivity founders on her own inability to think outside her profession's categories.

2. **Key axes chosen and decisions:**

* **The cat as a character / Attitude to the offer:** A brief but entirely sincere enchantment with the dream of sweets.

* **The waiter and relationships / The waiter's motive:** Sincere delight and a desire to help an extraordinary being find her place in life.

* **Tradition, canon and ownership of style / Consistency of graphic rules:** A deliberate break in the cat's own graphic mode between panels 3 and 4.

* **Revealing information / Indirect informational clues:** No professional clues until the last panel, for maximum punchline impact.

* **Rhythm, pauses and page organisation / The pause of the cat's imagination:** All of panel three (the *ten* turn) is devoted to the sensory experience of the circus dream.

3. **Visible embodiment of the decisions:**

* The waiter's sincerity is conveyed through soft movement, a blush of admiration and radiant little stars around him in panel two.

* The cat's immersion in dreaming in panel three is shown by her figure becoming a soft, rounded *chibi* silhouette against watercolour candy-floss clouds.

* Professional sobriety in panel four is expressed through sharp *gekiga*-style ink hatching, heavy shadows beneath the eyes, and the glow of a work chat on her whiskers (**my addition**).

**Description of the concept**

**Scene and action:** A contemporary Tokyo or Moscow coffee shop; the dialogue at the counter develops from an everyday order to the waiter's enthusiastic offer, the cat's retreat into sweet dreams, and her sudden sobering-up.

**The characters' appearance, poses and expressions:** A short-haired tricolour cat sits on a high bar stool; her appearance transforms from an ordinary house cat (panels 1–2), through a fluffy ball with enormous eyes closed in happiness (panel 3), to a stern, sculpturally drawn profile with a tired squint (panel 4). The waiter is a young man in a neat apron with a name badge, leaning over the counter, eyes alight.

**Composition and details:** A vertical strip of four identical rectangular panels. In the first two, only an espresso cup, a cream jug and a small plate with a strawberry pastry stand on the counter; in panel four, the cat's paw moves towards a smartphone with a terminal line lying behind the napkin holder (**my addition**).

**Visual manner, colour and light:** Black-and-white digital ink with screentones, except for panel three, which suddenly explodes into pastel-pink and mint watercolour, and panel four, flooded with dense cross-hatching and a single cold blue reflection from the screen.

**Panel contents and sequence, exact dialogue:**

* **Panel 1 (medium shot across the counter):** The waiter is frozen with tray and open mouth; the cat taps her tail impatiently against the bar stool. At the top, an unevenly outlined waiter balloon: “You're a cat? A talking cat?! Incredible…” At the bottom, the cat's even balloon: “Yes, I'm a cat. Are you bringing my order or not?”

* **Panel 2 (close-up of the waiter):** He enthusiastically presses his hands to his chest, graphic sparks of enthusiasm around him. Left balloon: “Hold on a second… Listen, could you use a job?” Right balloon: “It's just, my uncle runs a circus, and he'd gladly take you on, on any terms…”

* **Panel 3 (subjective view, unframed):** The rounded *chibi* cat blissfully closes her eyes and presses her paws to her cheeks; behind her float pink candy-floss clouds, lollipops and acrobats beneath a tent dome. An airy balloon with soft lettering: “A circus, you say? That's the place with the acrobats, the jugglers, the lollipops, the candy floss? Sounds tempting.”

* **Panel 4 (extreme close-up of the cat's face in profile):** Hard graphic rendering of every hair, deep shadows, a cold smartphone glow on eyes looking straight at the viewer. A heavy rectangular balloon at the bottom in sans-serif lettering: “But what would they need a programmer for?”

**Variant 3. “Object Classification Error” (Three-panel strip in an interface environment)**

**Developing the overall concept**

In this concept, the story is shown through the cat's own consciousness: she sees the entire surrounding world as a system of functions, requests and architectural dependencies. The characters experience a data-exchange protocol failure: the cat sent a standard request for coffee and a pastry, and instead of executing the method, the waiter returned an untyped offer to change jobs. The final line is literally the result of a logical check in her head: she ran the “Circus” object (acrobats, jugglers, sweets) through a dependency parser and discovered that the `Programmer` module was not required there. Whether this interface is merely a metaphor for her analytical thinking or she literally has cybernetic vision is deliberately left open.

The visual language combines European **Franco-Belgian ligne claire** (“clear line”) for the café's physical space and **user-interface graphics / infographics** overlaid on the scene as augmented reality. The calm, geometrically precise *ligne-claire* material environment emphasises the *deadpan* nature of events, while pop-up windows, relationship graphs and object-recognition frames make the cat's professional gaze visible before she answers. **My addition:** the circus dream is not a sensory picture but an automatically generated flowchart (UML diagram) of circus entities inside her head, where all elements belong to the `B2C_Entertainment_Food` class, which has no connections with `Backend_Dev`.

A horizontal **three-panel strip**, set inside a debugger window, is chosen. Unlike the previous variant, the interface frame lets the viewer suspect from the first panel that they are seeing through a technical person's eyes, while the waiter inside the panel has no idea. Before the final line, the viewer follows how the cat processes his unexpected offer; with the last line, they see the system's finished conclusion — a polite refusal due to incompatible specifications.

Coordination distinguishes levels of convention. The adopted *ligne-claire* rule requires uniform contour thickness, no expressive chiaroscuro, and clear legibility of every café object. The conventional association is reading vector diagrams and monospaced type as signs of an IT environment. The free authorial interpretation is the distribution of knowledge: interface boxes belong only to the cat's inner gaze and the author's presentation to the viewer, while the conversation itself occurs in a perfectly ordinary physical café, not a virtual chat.

**Brief presentation of the concept**

1. **Title and main idea:** “Object Classification Error” — an everyday café conversation shown as debugging an incorrect recruitment request inside the engineer-cat's mind.

2. **Key axes chosen and decisions:**

* **Meaning and the world's structure / The bearer of common sense:** The world-picture belongs entirely to the cat; her systemic view is adopted as the work's visual norm.

* **The waiter and relationships / The distribution of knowledge within the scene:** The waiter sees only a talking animal; the reader and cat see a systems analysis of the scene from the start.

* **Visual language / Applied and media forms:** **User-interface graphics** and **infographics** overlaid on a **ligne-claire** drawing.

* **Colour, light, text and movement / Marking subjective images:** The cat's ideas of the circus are distinguished as a transparent holographic overlay with a flowchart.

* **Genres and emotional frameworks / Absurdist comedy (deadpan):** The cat's expression remains utterly unruffled as she analyses the absurd offer.

3. **Visible embodiment of the decisions:**

* The knowledge gap between viewer and waiter is shown through thin green recognition frames around the waiter (`Role: Waiter | Status: Shocked`) and an empty plate (`Order_Status: Pending`) (**my addition**).
* The dreamy pause in panel two looks like loading and constructing a “Circus” tree diagram with icons of a tent, juggling pins and candy floss.

* The final question in panel three is accompanied by a red-highlighted `Programmer [0 dependencies found]` node at the edge of her field of view (**my addition**).

**Description of the concept**

**Scene and action:** In a bright minimalist urban café, the cat sits upright at a table before an empty napkin, waiting for her order, while the waiter gestures as he offers work with his uncle.

**The characters' appearance, poses and expressions:** A sleek black cat, stylised as a clear geometric silhouette, without clothes or unnecessary facial expression: in panels one and three she looks steadily and calmly ahead; in panel two she lowers her eyelids as if switching to computation mode. The waiter is a tidy character in the manner of Hergé's comics (simple facial features, a round head, expressively raised eyebrows and active hand gestures).

**Composition and details:** A horizontal strip of three equal square panels with one fixed side-on camera (a profile staging across the table). Over the cosy café drawing, with its coffee machine and pastry display, lies a thin one-pixel graphic layer: dashed sightlines, a coordinate grid and neat vector pictograms.

**Visual manner, colour and light:** Uniform black *ligne-claire* contours, flat fills without gradients: warm beige and terracotta for the café environment, contrasting neon turquoise and coral for the interface layer's thin lines and infographics.

**Panel contents and sequence, exact dialogue:**

* **Panel 1 (wide profile shot):** The waiter has frozen to the left of the table, the cat sits motionless at right; above an empty place on the table glows a dashed outline of a cup and pastry labelled `AWAITING_DELIVERY`. In the waiter's ordinary white balloon: “You're a cat? A talking cat?! Incredible…” The cat's reply in a rectangular window with monospaced type: “Yes, I'm a cat. Are you bringing my order or not?”

* **Panel 2 (same shot, focus on the pause):** The waiter leans closer, the cat closes her eyes; a turquoise `CIRCUS_ENTITY` flowchart unfolds above her head, branching into vector icons of an acrobat, juggling clubs, a lollipop and candy floss, with green checkmarks `Attractive: TRUE`. In the waiter's white balloon: “Hold on a second… Listen, could you use a job? It's just, my uncle runs a circus, and he'd gladly take you on, on any terms…” In the cat's window: “A circus, you say? That's the place with the acrobats, the jugglers, the lollipops, the candy floss?”

* **Panel 3 (same shot, conclusion):** The cat opens her eyes; in the flowchart above her, the connection between `CIRCUS_ENTITY` and the `PROGRAMMER` console icon blinks as a red dashed line with a question mark, while the waiter freezes in puzzlement. In the cat's rectangular window, on two lines: “Sounds tempting. But what would they need a programmer for?”
