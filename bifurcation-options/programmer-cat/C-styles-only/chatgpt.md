# **104 variants for visualising the programmer-cat joke**

Variants cover all 13 specified styles, with eight concepts each. These are not literally all imaginable combinations (virtually unlimited), but a broad catalogue of fundamentally different solutions.

The selection varies composition, panel count, viewpoint, personalities, circus depiction, visual metaphors and timing of the final reveal.

## **1. Manga**

**1.1. Classic four-panel manga**

A vertical four-panel comic: order, astonishment, circus offer, puzzled programmer question. Expressive faces, contrast between his turbulent emotions and her composure, and characteristic manga effects.

**1.2. Exaggerated waiter reaction**

A gigantic dramatic central panel: shocked face, widened eyes, sweat, motion lines, exploding-looking background. The next small panel shows a wholly calm cat with coffee delivering the line.

**1.3. From the cat's perspective**

Early panels look upward from a small seated cat. He seems enormous and absurdly excited; finally the camera withdraws, revealing her annoyed face and laptop.

**1.4. Romantic manga with a circus dream**

At his mention of circus she closes her eyes; flowers, stars, shining acrobats and airy sweets surround her. Romance suddenly disappears in the final panel as she businesslike asks why they need a programmer.

**1.5. Comedy manga with chibi characters**

Tiny, big-headed characters with exaggerated expressions. He jumps in astonishment; she imagines circus sweets; finally her face becomes unexpectedly serious, like a professional developer's.

**1.6. Parallel realities**

A spread splits between actual café left and imagined performers and lights right. The circus takes more space as dialogue develops, but the final line returns to the table's laptop.

**1.7. Dramatic profession reveal**

Early panels anticipate something incredible: he believes he has found the greatest circus sensation. Last page: her close-up against radiating lines, with enormous emphasis on “PROGRAMMER”.

**1.8. Fantasy versus everyday life**

Ordinary quiet café, then a long sequence of spectacular circus imaginings. Deliberately mundane ending: she checks work notifications and asks her question while he is utterly confused.

## **2. Twentieth-century French magazine cartoon**

**2.1. Single-panel Paris café cartoon**

A little cat at a round table with coffee and pastry; a lanky, enormously moustached waiter bends in amazement. Final programmer caption below; elegant lively pen line, exaggerated faces, restrained colour.

**2.2. Two panels of frustrated expectation**

First, he delightedly discovers her speech and prepares to place her in a circus. Second, she adjusts her glasses, calmly speaks, and he loses balance with the tray.

**2.3. Poster in his imagination**

Above him appears a sumptuous ad: “For the First Time! Incredible Talking Cat!”, with spotlights and delighted spectators. Below the imagined poster, her emphatically prosaic self announces programming.

**2.4. Family connections**

He energetically offers work with his uncle; a wall photograph shows them with the pompous director. With a subtly ironic smile she asks how her qualifications relate to the family circus business.

**2.5. Intellectual cat**

Elegant, bespectacled, with briefcase and folded newspapers, she resembles a Paris intellectual. His grotesquely open mouth sees only a talking animal, missing the laptop nearby.

**2.6. No one else is surprised**

He is profoundly astonished while customers keep reading and drinking, as though speech were ordinary. Her final line emphasises absurdity; their faces stay demonstratively indifferent.

**2.7. Circus poster outside**

A poster column outside advertises a bright performance; he points enthusiastically. She considers it as though genuinely liking acrobats and candy, then asks the question.

**2.8. Humour-magazine cover**

Cat, tray-bearing waiter and giant imagined tent between them fill the cover. Expressive magazine typography frames them; the question becomes the principal punning headline.

## **3. Minimalist webcomic**

**3.1. Four identical panels**

The schematic cat at a table and waiter beside her never change position. Only dialogue and his eyes change; the last line wholly reverses earlier panels' meaning.

**3.2. Two panels: expectation and reality**

First, he imagines her with a microphone in the ring. Second, she sits at a computer asking why a circus needs a programmer; extremely simple drawing, no excess detail.

**3.3. Growing pause**

Order, his silence, continued silence, finally circus offer. A concluding panel much wider than the others contains her calm final line.

**3.4. Pictogram-only comic**

Eared shape for cat, tray-bearing stick figure, triangular tent, `</>` for programming. A few lines and changing pictograms narrate; circus/code juxtaposition is the graphic comic centre.

**3.5. Single-panel minimalism**

Almost empty background, two characters, tiny coffee-and-pastry table. His circus question receives her last line; precise positioning, pause and economical wording create the effect.

**3.6. Thought bubbles**

His bubble contains a talking ring performer; hers an ordinary work computer. They look at each other; below is “But why would they need a programmer?”

**3.7. Gradually changing scale**

Initially he is large and she tiny, reflecting his view of an unusual animal. Finally equal space makes her a full interlocutor with an ordinary profession.

**3.8. Revealing a hidden object**

Three panels show only tabletop, cat and his face. The last extends downwards: a laptop with code editor lies on her lap as the line appears.

## **Additional combinations**

These 104 concepts can vary further within the 13 styles. Suitable single images can become sequences; many multi-panel concepts can become one illustration with a final caption.

The following independent parameters provide the greatest further diversity:

| Parameter | Possible solutions |
| --- | --- |
| Format | One illustration, diptych, triptych, 4–8-panel strip, comic page, spread, scroll, poster |
| Depicted moment | Order, surprise, offer, fantasy, final line, subsequent reaction |
| Viewpoint | External, cat's eyes, waiter's eyes, overhead, behind counter, through window, from imagined circus |
| Cat appearance | Ordinary domestic, anthropomorphic, businesslike, fluffy, stern, sleepy, emphatically composed |
| Waiter character | Timid, enthusiastic, pushy, haughty, confused, energetic, overdramatic |
| Circus depiction | Actual tent outside, fantasy cloud, poster, miniature scene, parallel world, prop hints |
| Profession depiction | Laptop, screen code, work pass, bag, book, CV, desk, or no clues |
| Joke reveal | Text alone, object, fantasy–reality contrast, scale change, unexpected final panel |
| Text use | Full dialogue, shortened lines, final line only, caption, speech balloons |
| Emotional tone | Absurd, cosy, solemn, ironic, dramatic, dreamy, mundane |
| Spatial composition | Centred, diagonal, symmetrical, large empty background, multiple figures, simultaneous scenes |
| Narrative order | Strict chronology, memory, fantasy, beginning at ending, parallel expectation and reality |

A particularly significant difference is **when viewers learn she is a programmer**: only in the final line, hinted earlier through laptop or CV, shown initially, or inferred solely from her reaction.

These are thus 104 independent visualisation variants; the table permits many more combinations without introducing any style beyond the original list.
