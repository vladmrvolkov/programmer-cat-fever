### I. Interpretation

**1. Semantic emphasis**
*Question.* Which layer of comedy does the image foreground?
*Why it matters.* The joke has several layers: the premise itself is comic, both characters' behaviour is comic, and the final reversal is independently comic. Words hold all these layers together, whereas a drawing inevitably foregrounds one.

**2. Target of the laughter** (refines 1)
*Question.* Whom does the viewer laugh at, and whose side are they on?
*Why it matters.* The text passes no judgment: blindness can be seen both in the waiter's excitement and in the cat's puzzlement. The answer determines whom to draw sympathetically and whom to caricature.

**3. The cat's awareness** (refines 1)
*Question.* How well does the cat understand what exactly astonished the waiter, and with what intention does she ask the final question?
*Why it matters.* In the text the line allows different readings, but her facial expression in the final panel will have to be drawn unambiguously.

**4. Tone and genre register**
*Question.* In what mood and genre is the scene presented?
*Why it matters.* The joke's tone is neutral, but it contains material for different readings: “on any terms,” the circus as a place where animals are exhibited, the irritation of a customer who is not being served.

### II. The story world

**5. The ordinariness of the miracle**
*Question.* How unusual is a talking cat in the world in which this café stands?
*Why it matters.* In the text only the waiter is surprised; the cat behaves like an ordinary customer, and nothing is said about the rest of the world. A drawing cannot remain silent: the background will have to be filled, and it will decide which of the two is the strange one here.
- **5.1. Witnesses.** Is anyone else in the frame, and how do they regard what is happening? Third parties' reactions provide the viewer with a measure of normality.

**6. Place, time and cultural environment**
*Question.* Where and when does the action take place?
*Why it matters.* The joke brings together a circus with lollipops, a timeless image, and a programmer, a marker of a particular era. The setting determines how alien the last word will sound, and therefore the strength of the surprise.
- **6.1. Type of establishment.** What kind of café is this, and who are its usual customers? The café may be familiar territory for one character, in which case the setting itself helps prepare the resolution.
- **6.2. Localization.** In what linguistic and cultural realities does the scene exist? Signs, menus and character types determine how firmly the Russian-language joke is tied to a particular place.

### III. Characters

**7. Degree of humanization of the cat**
*Question.* How much of the animal remains in her appearance, body language and habits?
*Why it matters.* The waiter's astonishment requires her to be recognizable as a cat, while the ending requires it to be possible to see her as a professional. - **7.1. Scale and physical mechanics.** How big is she compared with people and furniture, and how does she cope with human surroundings? She must enter, sit down, order and speak to a person, and each action depends on scale.

**8. The cat's character type**
*Question.* What is she like: age, appearance, means, bearing?
*Why it matters.* The text tells us only her sex and manner of speaking. The character type determines how her impatience and dreaminess are read. This also includes deciding whether to acknowledge that the heroine is grammatically feminine while the profession named at the end is masculine.
- **8.1. Emotional arc.** How does her state change, and how sharply? In a few lines she passes through businesslike composure, irritation, dreaminess and bewilderment. These changes are her acting material.

**9. The image of the programmer**
*Question.* What conception of programmers does the image rely on?
*Why it matters.* The ending works only if the viewer instantly recognizes the profession and has a ready reaction to it. Conceptions differ inside and outside the profession, and this determines whether the result is self-irony or an outsider's view.
