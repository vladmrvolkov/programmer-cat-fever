# **144 variants for visualising the programmer-cat joke**

This joke permits an enormous range of visual solutions because it contains several sources of humour: a talking cat, the waiter's astonishment, a circus job offer, dreamy imaginings of circus pleasures, and the unexpected revelation of her profession.

Literally listing every possibility is impossible: the number is virtually infinite. But a broad catalogue of fundamentally different solutions can cover composition, plot, angles, panel sequences, style, degree of absurdity and delivery of the final joke.

Below are **144 independent concepts**, in 12 groups. Each can become a complete illustration or comic; many can also be combined.

## **I. Single-panel illustrations: the classic café situation**

1–12 · The entire joke concentrated in one scene

**1. Classic illustration of the joke.** The cat sits at a café table before coffee and pastry, with a stunned waiter beside her. Unflappably, she delivers the programmer line. Style: traditional magazine cartoon with expressive characters.

**2. Unflappable cat, shocked waiter.** Completely calm, she businesslike holds a cup in her paw and looks at him with mild puzzlement. He freezes open-mouthed, a tray suspended above him. Style: economical humorous illustration with exaggerated expressions.

**3. An elegant intellectual cat.** A refined cat in glasses, with a neat hairstyle or tie, resembles an experienced specialist. Dignified, she explains why a circus is a strange place for a programmer. Style: sophisticated European book illustration.

**4. The most ordinary cat.** No human accessories: an ordinary fluffy cat sits on a chair looking at the waiter. Comedy comes from an entirely normal-looking animal calmly discussing a professional career. Style: realistic animal painting with speech balloons.

**5. A chair too high.** A tiny cat occupies a huge restaurant chair, barely reaching her saucer. The waiter bends over her, astonished by what he hears, while she discusses programming work. Style: amusing children's illustration emphasising scale differences.

**6. Empty café, solitary customer.** No one but cat and waiter occupies a cosy evening café. Ample space, soft light and silence emphasise the surreal strangeness. Style: atmospheric watercolour or cinematic digital painting.

**7. Among ordinary customers.** People fill the café, talking, eating and using phones, ignoring the talking cat. Only the waiter is stunned by her abilities; she finds his offer absurd. Style: crowded humorous scene with many everyday details.

**8. A regular customer.** Barista, cook and other staff routinely serve her; the new waiter encounters her for the first time. She looks mildly tired of repeating the same conversation. Style: everyday-sitcom comedy illustration.

**9. Close-up of the last line.** Her face fills almost the whole drawing, eyes slightly narrowed, one eyebrow raised. The wide-eyed waiter is blurred behind. Style: expressive portrait drawing with an enormous speech balloon.

**10. The waiter loses balance.** Hearing the final line, he drops a tray; spoons, cups and pastries hang in the air. She remains still and composed. Style: dynamic caricature exaggerating physical reactions.

**11. Coffee matters more than career.** She reaches for long-awaited coffee while he insistently explains the circus. She answers as though employment were an annoying distraction from dessert. Style: everyday comedy with subtle irony and cosy interior details.

**12. An aristocratic scene.** Luxurious restaurant, crystal chandeliers, porcelain, waiter in tails and cat sitting regally. Solemn surroundings contrast comically with a circus vacancy discussion. Style: richly detailed satirical painting.

## **III. Comics revealing the joke sequentially**

**30. Eight cinematic panels.** Almost a film scene: café wide shot, order, waiter close-up, tray, cat's eyes, fantasy, return, punchline. Style: thoroughly storyboarded graphic novel.

**31. Nine panels in a square grid.** A regular 3×3 grid turns the joke into economical actions and reactions. The final cell contains the motionless cat's question; neighbouring panels emphasise his growing puzzlement. Style: minimalist indie comic.

**32. A prolonged waiter reaction.** Several successive panels repeat his stunned face while she calmly waits. Offer and unexpected explanation follow quickly. Style: absurdist comic using awkward silence.

**33. Almost silent comic.** Gestures, expressions and coffee/circus thought images convey most of the story. Only the last panel has full text: “But why would they need a programmer?” Style: economical visual humour without unnecessary words.

**34. Vertical webtoon.** Cosy setting, order, increasingly elongated astonished face, bright circus panorama, suddenly mundane answer unfold downwards. A large empty gap between fantasy and ending creates a pause. Style: colourful vertical digital comic.

**35. A page turn.** The first page ends with his circus question and her dreamy smile. Turning the page reveals the profession in a large panel. Style: humorous book comic using surprise.

**36. Through the waiter's eyes.** Readers follow his observations: cat, talking cat, incredible circus opportunity. Only the last panel destroys his conception. Style: subjective graphic novel with exaggerated expression.

**41. Zooming comic.** Each panel moves from the whole café closer to table, face and eyes. The last line abruptly returns to a wide shot of the confused waiter. Style: cinematic storyboard.

**43. Dialogue as an object route.** Lines occupy menu, cup, napkin, window reflection and circus sign. The eye follows conversation through the café, ending on the programmer cat. Style: complex puzzle illustration.

**44. Vast empty-space minimalism.** Two tiny figures sit at the bottom of a large white page. After visually modest dialogue, her last line occupies unexpectedly large space. Style: intellectual minimalist comic.

**45. Huge main panel, small explanations.** An impressive café illustration fills almost the whole page, surrounded by small panels of earlier events. The main image fixes the final-line moment. Style: contemporary graphic novel with asymmetrical layout.

**46. Triptych.** Three vertical sections: “Café”, “Circus Fantasy”, “Professional Reality”. The last replaces fantasy with a cat at a computer asking the question. Style: decorative ironic triptych.

**47. Long panorama.** Both characters recur across one very wide café image, sequentially enacting the conversation. A colourful fantasy occupies the middle. Style: continuous narrative recalling medieval illustrated scrolls.

**48. False branches.** Several imagined paths leave his offer: acrobat cat, clown cat, juggler cat. All end in one final panel revealing the actual programmer. Style: ironic comic with alternative-history diagrams.

## **V. Angles and cinematic devices**

49–60 · Different ways to view the same scene

**49. The waiter's subjective view.** Readers look from standing human height, as though hearing impossible speech themselves. Below, she looks directly at them, businesslike asking about programming. Style: realistic first-person shot.

**50. The cat's subjective view.** An enormous waiter looms, gesticulating about the circus. Paws, coffee and pastry appear below; her last line sounds entirely reasonable. Style: comic first-person angle.

**51. Directly overhead.** Tables, chairs, characters and place settings form geometry. Speech balloons create a dialogue pattern around the tiny cat. Style: neat isometric-flat editorial graphics.

**52. Under the table.** Foreground paws and hanging cloth frame his towering legs. Balloons above the table edge convey the conversation. Style: playful comic illustration with unusual camera height.

**53. Over his shoulder.** His shoulder and notepad hand frame her slightly mocking gaze. Attention rests on her confident final question. Style: cinematic realism with shallow depth of field.

**54. Through coffee.** She appears through a transparent cup or distorted metal-pot reflection. Unusual optics intensify the absurd programmer-circus career discussion. Style: artistic photographic illustration.

**55. Through the window from outside.** From the street, viewers see his emotional gestures and her entirely ordinary customer behaviour through a large lit window. Style: atmospheric urban sketch.

**56. CCTV.** Grainy monochrome, timestamp and interference make the incredible conversation funnier through dry documentary treatment. Style: mock-documentary comedy.

**57. Fisheye.** Curving counter and distorted interior make him gigantic; she sits at the geometric centre. Distortion emphasises the offer's incredibility. Style: grotesque digital caricature.

**58. Extreme close-up of speech.** First the human response to a moving feline mouth, then a separate detail of lips and whiskers delivering the final line. Comedy comes from almost documentary scrutiny of an impossible detail. Style: hyperrealistic macrocomic.

**59. Theatre stage.** A café set, one spotlight, the characters central. Sudden darkness accompanies the last line, with light solely on her. Style: theatrical illustration.

**60. Opposed portraits.** Two large facing portraits: astonished waiter, composed cat. Small lines between them contain the story; the last visually belongs to her. Style: psychological portrait comic.

## **Additional dimensions for varying any of the 144 variants**

The concepts do not exhaust possibilities: each can vary along independent parameters.

| Parameter | Possible solutions |
| --- | --- |
| **Cat's appearance** | Ordinary domestic, fluffy pedigree, stray, anthropomorphic, cartoon, entirely realistic, elderly, kitten, robotic |
| **Cat's character** | Composed, sarcastic, dreamy, irritated, shy, businesslike, haughty, naïve, absent-minded |
| **Waiter's character** | Timid, enthusiastic, hyperactive, simple, cunning, businesslike, theatrical, excessively emotional |
| **Café type** | Parisian, Soviet, contemporary coffee shop, luxury restaurant, roadside, old-fashioned, themed, futuristic |
| **Circus character** | Classic big top, antique, fairy-tale, avant-garde, cosmic, digital, dark, cosy, absurd |
| **Presenting profession** | Text alone, laptop, badge, code screen, workstation, fantasy, visual symbol |
| **Timing of final reveal** | Caption, last balloon, last panel, after a pause, page turn, hidden detail |
| **Character count** | Only cat and waiter, extra customers, director uncle, other animals, circus troupe |
| **Comic emphasis** | Talking cat, waiter reaction, professional difference, absurd logic, fantasy–reality clash |
| **Colour scheme** | Black-and-white, monochrome, pastel, bright circus, neon, limited palette, realistic |
| **Realism** | Photorealism, realistic caricature, stylisation, cartoon, conventional signs, abstraction |
| **Image format** | Square, vertical poster, horizontal strip, book page, spread, panorama, circular composition |
| **Speech delivery** | Balloons, captions, text below image, intertitles, inscriptions on objects, messaging interface, entirely wordless |
| **Mood** | Cosy, touching, energetic, dryly ironic, melancholy, solemn, surreal |

Thus **144 concepts are 144 starting points, rather than 144 final images**, yielding thousands of different visual readings.

For example, no. 17 (circus from coffee steam) could become a medieval miniature, pixel art, engraving, cyberpunk or watercolour. No. 37 (two viewpoints) could be a silent comic, photographic collage or manga page. Not only appearance and technique but perception of the comic ending would differ.

The joke's distinctive feature is that **visual humour can rest on a talking cat's existence or on speech being entirely unremarkable to her — unlike the strange idea of sending a programmer to a circus.** This permits simple cartoons and complex layered compositions without changing the original joke.
