The visualisation of this joke rests on the clash of two absurdities: for the waiter, the miracle is that the cat can talk; for the cat herself, her feline nature is commonplace, whereas the main part of her identity is her profession as a programmer. To encompass every possible embodiment of the story, we will first unfold a construction kit of semantic axes (supplementing each with new parameters and adding missing dimensions), then distribute specific illustration and comic concepts across these axes in the thirteen stipulated styles.

**Axis 1. Reading and subtext (who understands what, and what they want)**

* **Naive professional tunnel vision:** The cat is so immersed in code that she has genuinely forgotten her biological uniqueness and assesses the circus solely as an odd employer with no obvious IT infrastructure.
* **The “IT is already a circus” metaphor:** The word “circus” brings an ironic smile to the cat's face, because her current job has plenty of clowns and deadline jugglers of its own, though at least they do not hand out cotton candy there.
* **Self-interested impresario:** The waiter is not merely surprised — his business instincts kick in immediately as he calculates his commission from his producer uncle for an exclusive “talking exhibit”.
* **The temptation of downshifting:** In the cat's imagination, the circus is a heavenly refuge of simplicity, sugar and childhood joy, somewhere to escape burnout and endless bugs.
* **The uncle as a shadowy puppeteer:** The uncle ranges from a kindly fairy-tale big-top director to a predatory capitalist in a top hat or a Soviet nomenklatura official allocating jobs through patronage (blat).

**Axis 2. Characters (cat, waiter, customers)**

* **Zoological realism (0% anthropomorphism):** An ordinary four-legged cat sits on a chair before a cup, maximising the contrast between an animal's appearance and a senior developer's line.
* **Everyday geek anthropomorphism (50%):** A hunched cat in a hoodie or glasses, holding a cup in her paws; breed establishes character — a hairless, nervous Sphynx, a bearded Maine Coon sysadmin, or a well-groomed British Shorthair team lead.
* **Full humanisation (90%):** A lady cat in a suit, beret or historical dress, differing from humans only in her head and tail, which makes the waiter's shock comically tactless.
* **Waiter type:** From a prim elderly waiter with impeccable bearing or a hard-bitten noir bartender to a fussing student barista or Soviet buffet attendant.
* **The room's reaction (extras):** The customers either freeze in a silent tableau (dropping spoons, glasses and newspapers) or sit engrossed in laptops, ignoring the talking cat at the next table.
* **Animal society (inversion):** Everyone around is a talking animal of a different profession (a dog accountant, a goose lawyer), and only the new human waiter inexplicably sees the cat as a circus curiosity.

**Axis 3. Setting (environment and atmosphere)**

* **Classic grand café or Viennese coffee house:** Marble tables, velvet, mirrors and silver create an elevated backdrop to an earthly conversation about recruitment.
* **Coworking space and third-wave café:** A space packed with freelancers and MacBooks, where a programmer cat fits in more naturally than the waiter himself.
* **Late-night diner or station buffet:** A tired, smoky or institutional place with cheap coffee for night-shift workers.
* **Historical or mythological location:** A seventeenth-century Dutch tavern, an Edo-period Japanese teahouse or an ancient Egyptian banqueting hall.

**Axis 4. Chronology (what happened before and what happens afterwards)**

* **Before — release night:** The cat spent the night fixing a crashed server, closed a critical bug towards dawn, and came to reward herself with caffeine and quick carbohydrates.
* **Before — escaping a call:** The cat has just slammed her laptop shut after an absurd client meeting and gone down to the café around the corner to breathe.
* **After — back to code:** Leaving the waiter stunned, the cat puts on headphones, opens a terminal and resumes coding right at the table.
* **After — automating the big top:** The cat does call the uncle and takes a contract to develop a cloud CRM system to track the jugglers' props and ticket offices.

**Axis 5. Objects and details (material clues)**

* **Direct IT markers:** A sticker-covered laptop, a tech-giant badge on her collar, a USB drive, a smartwatch on her paw, or a book on algorithms beside the saucer.
* **Historical analogues of code:** A soroban abacus, punched cards, teletype paper tape, Pascal's mechanical calculator, or scrolls with binary hieroglyphs for retro styles.
* **Specific food details:** A Viennese mélange with a high cap of cream and an éclair, a saucer of milk beside an espresso, or a Soviet basket tart with a buttercream rose.
* **Materialising the circus:** A big-top poster outside, the tent's reflection in a coffee spoon, or a cloud of cotton candy merging with cappuccino foam.

**Additional axis 6. Composition, format and punchline mechanics**

* **Single-panel scene with a “dream cloud”:** An everyday café scene at the bottom of the sheet, with a bright circus vision swirling above the closed-eyed cat's head, shattered by the final line.
* **Multi-panel strip (timing the pause):** Alternating shots from a wide view to a close-up of the dreamy face and a sharp “zoom” onto the sceptical look at the end.
* **Visual spoiler versus hidden clue:** Either viewers immediately see the laptop beside the cat's paws (and laugh at the waiter's blindness), or the programmer detail appears only in the final panel.

**Additional axis 7. The cat's IT speciality (shade of humour)**

* **Backend developer or DevOps engineer:** A gloomy, red-eyed introvert who, on hearing “circus”, imagines the big top's infrastructure collapsing under load.
* **Frontend developer or UI designer:** An aesthete sincerely attracted to the circus only by the lollipops' colour palette and the acrobats' smooth animation.
* **Test engineer (QA):** A cat who, even while dreaming of cotton candy, mentally tests the gymnasts' cables for breaking-point boundary conditions.
