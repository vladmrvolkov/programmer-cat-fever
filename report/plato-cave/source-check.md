# Source check: Wright of Derby, Plato's cave and Repin

*Checked by Claude (Opus 5.5) on 13 October 2026, for the report [Plato's cave: why the readers found it, and where the artist found Wright](README.md). The claims checked here came from ChatGPT and Gemini, asked by the author whether anyone had linked Wright of Derby's "A Philosopher Lecturing on the Orrery" to Plato's cave before, and from Gemini's answer in [test 52](../../tests/52-wright-and-cave-recall/README.md). The check was a web search and reading of the pages found; nothing beyond that.*

## Checked

**Martin Reinhart, "A brief history of stargazing in the 18th century"** ([reinhart.media/stargazing](https://www.reinhart.media/stargazing)). Named by ChatGPT as the closest predecessor of the interpretation; quoted by Gemini in test 52.

- The essay exists.
- The sentence Gemini quoted — "the shadows on the wall of Plato's cave have been replaced by sparkling stars" — is in it.
- That sentence is about Boullée's cenotaph for Newton (1784) and its artificial night sky, not about an orrery or a planetarium.
- Wright's painting is discussed in another paragraph, about the spectators stepping outside the model of the solar system. The essay does not link the painting to the cave.
- In test 52 the readers were asked to recall, not to search. Gemini produced this sentence verbatim; whether it searched anyway is not visible in the answer.

**Mikkel Bille and Tim Flohr Sørensen, "An Anthropology of Luminosity: The Agency of Light"**, *Journal of Material Culture* 12(3), 2007, pp. 263–284, DOI [10.1177/1359183507081894](https://doi.org/10.1177/1359183507081894). Named by ChatGPT as discussing both the cave (p. 267) and Wright's painting (p. 273), without linking them.

- The article exists, with these details.
- Whether the cave and Wright's painting are on the pages ChatGPT gave was not checked.

**The system prompts.** The persona prompt v17 (artist A) and the prompt reworked by ChatGPT (artists B, C, D) were searched for Wright, orrery, Plato, cave and Repin, in Russian and English. None of these words occurs in either prompt, so the idea did not come from the instructions.

## Not checked

Gemini's statements that David Solkin and Martin Kemp had noted the irony of the painting's title in Plato's terms, and that Paul Duro, Jesse Molesworth and Barbara Maria Stafford read Wright's lamp as a source of epistemic illusion. ChatGPT, given Gemini's answer, could not confirm the Solkin and Kemp claim. Neither I nor the models found a published comparison of Repin's "They Did Not Expect Him" with the cave; Gemini first said the parallel "arises regularly" and then withdrew it as a rhetorical exaggeration.

## Limit

The same family of models made the pictures, read them and searched for their sources. A search of the art-historical literature by a person would be the independent check.
